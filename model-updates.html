Model Updates

A running log of model improvements. Each entry explains what changed, why it matters, and the measured impact on the backcast — so the calibration's evolution is legible, not just a diff. Most recent first.

2026-06-04

Outages: per-unit, from CAMPD — coal units stop hidingOutages

What changed

The backcast's outage overlay used to work on each plant's total metered output. That has a structural blind spot: when one unit at a multi-unit plant trips, the other units keep the plant's combined CEMS signal alive, so the outage is invisible. The worst case is a mixed coal/gas plant like W A Parish — a coal unit can be down for months while the gas units mask it completely. A patch existed (a hand-built unit list) but it only covered January–August 2023.

A new step (scripts/derive_campd_unit_outages.py) now detects outages on each generating unit's own hourly EPA CAMPD output, for the full 2023 and 2024, and removes that unit's capacity (its EIA-860 nameplate) from dispatch during the gap. Coal units — which run as baseload — are flagged whenever output sags below ~5% for a sustained stretch; load-following gas and combined-cycle units are flagged only when they go fully dead, so a CC turbine that is simply idle for economic reasons is never mistaken for a forced outage.

Why it matters

Real units come offline for maintenance and forced outages; if the model keeps that capacity available, it dispatches coal and gas-steam the grid did not actually have, distorting the merit order and prices. Resolving outages at the unit level lets the backcast remove exactly the megawatts that were out — including the coal-unit outages the plant-level method could never see — and extends real outage coverage to 2024, which previously had none at the unit level (0 derated tranches → 263).

Verdict: the right input, with a re-tune to follow. Sourcing outages per-unit fixes the largest gas-steam bias outright (2023 ST_GAS goes from +10.2% to +2.6%) and improves 2024 lignite, because the model now sheds capacity that was genuinely out rather than leaving it to run. The energy that capacity was carrying has to go somewhere, and in this no-commitment config it lands on CT peakers — a class the config already over-runs because peaker start-up limits aren't modeled until the commitment pass. So the unit-level extract is the more accurate input, but it un-centers the peaker/gas-steam tuning that was fit around the sparser hand list; the natural follow-up is a light peaker re-tune (or turning on the unit-commitment pass for CTs). Coal totals stay close (2023 +6.2%, 2024 +1.8%) and the grid energy balance holds at about +1%.

2026-06-04

Storage: efficiency fix + a foresight guardStorage

What changed

Two storage changes. (1) Round-trip efficiency fix: the backcast battery fleet was hard-coded to the lithium-ion RTE constant (0.86), so the storage_rte_4hr calibration knob (set to 0.85) never actually reached the model — the lever was silently disconnected. It now flows through, matching the forward-entry path. (2) Daily-cycling toggle: a new optional constraint (storage_daily_cycling) forces each battery's state-of-charge back to its day-start level every 24 hours, so storage can no longer bank cheap energy across days.

Why it matters

The dispatch solves the whole year as one optimization, which gives storage perfect foresight over all 8,760 hours — it charges in the globally-cheapest hours and discharges in the dearest, a level of clairvoyance no real operator has (day-ahead markets see ~24–48 hours). That over-flattens net load and can bias the thermal parameters we calibrate against history. The daily-cycling toggle caps storage to within-day arbitrage — the realistic limit for the short-duration (4-hour) batteries that make up the 2023–2024 fleet, which physically cannot shift energy across days anyway.

Verdict: negligible — and that is the finding. Removing storage's perfect foresight changed every fossil class by less than 0.12% and nudged the average price by a single cent, with peak prices and scarcity hours untouched. The direction is exactly as expected — with foresight off, peakers run a hair more (storage no longer shaves every peak perfectly) and baseload coal a hair less — but the magnitude confirms that the single-LP foresight assumption is not distorting the 2023–2024 backcast. That holds because the historical fleet is almost entirely 4-hour batteries, which physically can't shift energy across days no matter how far ahead they see. The toggle is kept as a guard: the moment longer-duration storage (12 h+, flow, iron-air) enters a backcast, this same comparison should be re-run, where the gap is expected to widen.
