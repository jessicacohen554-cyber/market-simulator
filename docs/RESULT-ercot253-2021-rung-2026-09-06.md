## 3. THE FINDING: the model prices 31 hours ABOVE ERCOT's published offer cap

Decomposing the maximum hour: **λ $1,904.5 + ORDC adder $8,866.9 = $10,771.4.**

* The **adder alone is well-behaved** — its maximum all year is $8,905.6, under the
  2021 VOLL of $9,000. That is the ercot-213 `(VOLL − λ)` anchoring doing its job.
* **The SUM is not capped.** 31 hours clear above the 2021 system-wide offer cap of
  $9,000 (mean overshoot $199.6, max $1,771.4) — prices the market was
  administratively incapable of producing.

**Why no previous run saw this.** ercot-213's G-CAP gate measured the 2023 keeper
landing at "lambda 298.86 + adder 4701.14 = exactly 5000.00 = VOLL, so the
published protocol cap binds and holds." It holds there because λ is ~$300 in
those hours: `(VOLL − λ) + λ = VOLL` identically **whenever λ is the λ the adder
was computed against**. Push λ to $1,904 — a real Uri scarcity λ, an order of
magnitude above anything in 2023-2025 — and the identity that made the cap appear
to bind is the very thing that breaks it, because the adder is anchored on one λ
while the settled price adds another. **The protocol cap has been holding in the
keeper years by arithmetic coincidence of a low λ, not by construction. There is
no explicit HCAP clamp on the total price anywhere in the model.**

D3 (the published pre-Uri cap) did not cause this defect — it **exposed** it, which
is what an accurate input is supposed to do (rule 14 `[R-ACCURATE]`). **The fix is
not to revert D3**: that would restore a cap the 2021 market did not have and bury
a real structural error inside a knowingly wrong input, which rule 14 forbids in
terms. The successor is an explicit protocol-cap clamp on the settled price,
scoped and screened under rule 29, and it is **named here, not armed.**
