**My inference (not a primary-source quote)**
- How established NW models treat the phenomena behind the miss: NWPCC's GENESYS and BPA's own studies dispatch hydro with an hourly *water-value* that is set by **the West-wide market price** (the Council's Mid-C price forecasts are AURORA/Western-market outputs, https://www.nwcouncil.org/sites/default/files/Appendix_C__Electricity_Price_Forecast__1.pdf). A NW-only LP with a fixed interface cannot reproduce that coupling; the model's monthly-flat price is the signature. Lever B is the structural fix; A is the cheaper proxy for its between-day component.
- The coal-conservation behaviour of 2023 (utility holds coal when stockpile is short) is a *utility fuel-procurement* decision outside every public dataset; NEMS/ReEDS do not model it; IRPs treat it as a fuel-cost input. It belongs in the ledger, not in the LP.
- A FERC-714 lambda benchmark is structurally the *same object* as the LP dual (marginal cost of serving load in a cost-based pool), and would cover **every** backcast year, unlike WEIM (2023-06+). Its known caveats: hydro-dominated BAs (BPAT, CHPD/DOPD/GCPD, SCL, TPWR, AVRN) may file no lambda or a non-thermal one; a demand-weighted footprint lambda would then be the thermal-following members' (PACE/PACW, IPCO, NWMT, PSEI, PGE, AVA, NEVP ≈ 65–70 % of load). That is a *coverage* question the STOP gate must fix ex ante.
