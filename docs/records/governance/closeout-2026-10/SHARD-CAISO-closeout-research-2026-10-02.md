**Headline.** The ISO is NOT-YET only on the 2019–21 fold, and every fold miss traces to one object: the model cannot buy
the real DSW (Palo Verde / WEIM) import volume in years with no measured intertie hub print (OASIS GroupZip serves nothing
before 2021-04-27). The lane ledgered that residual as "data-availability limited" (R-CAISO-20). **That ledger is now
wrong on its premise:** CAISO opened a *Historical OASIS Data Downloader* (notice dated 2025-11-11) serving OASIS
market-price history back to **2016** from a requester-pays S3 bucket — the lane's own README (`data/raw/lmp-data/CAISO/README.md`,
caiso-263 paragraph) states "2019, 2020 and Jan–Apr 2021 hub/DLAP LMPs are NOT obtainable from OASIS by any endpoint",
which predates the tool. Fetching those prints (a) makes 2019–20 C3a/C3b/C3c scoreable and (b) arms the four
already-K clean import rungs on their own measured triggers in 2019–21, which is the identified carrier of the C1/C4 fold
misses. Everything else in this report is second-order to that one intake.

**My inference**
- The model's import architecture (firm blocks + priced gas tranches + measured-hub clean rungs under a MIC seam cap) is closer to the market than the ADS/PLEXOS convention (hurdle-rate paths with a historical net-import cap), and nothing in the external practice suggests a different *mechanism* for the fold — only that the measured hub price is the indispensable input, which L1 restores.
- The DMM narrative that 2020 imports shifted SW→NW *excluding* EIM, while EIA-930 DSW held at 41.9 TWh, implies that in 2020 the DSW channel was disproportionately EIM transfers (AZPS/NEVP/SRP, SRP joining Apr 2020). The model's clean rungs are exactly that channel, so 2020 should respond most to L1 — and is also the year with the largest C1 gap.
- 2020's documented load decline (COVID, BTM solar) is already in measured demand; hydro (dry) is in the budget; neither explains the CC excess (R-CAISO-7 cleared both).
- The DMM 2020/2021 observation that < 90 % of RA capacity was offered in RT during high-load hours argues *against* adding any CC must-offer floor beyond the P0-detected RA bridge — consistent with the lane's R verdicts on must-run families.
