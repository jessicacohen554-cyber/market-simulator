## 1. The statutory read

### 1.1 F-1 — §45U(d)(1) multiplies the NET, not the rate

§45U(a) is **already a net quantity**: "the amount by which — (1) the product of (A) 0.3 cents,
multiplied by (B) the kilowatt hours ... exceeds (2) the reduction amount for such taxable year."
§45U(d)(1) then says: "the amount of the credit determined under **subsection (a)** shall be equal
to such amount ... **multiplied by 5**."

The 5x operand is therefore the **post-phase-down** amount. §45U(b)(2)(A) sets the reduction as the
**lesser of** the (a)(1) amount or 16 % of the excess of gross receipts over the product of
2.5 cents and those kWh — the "lesser of" being what floors the credit at zero. Per unit:

```
§45U (cents/kWh) = 5 x [ R_year - min(R_year, 0.16 x max(0, GR - T_year)) ]
                 = 5 x max(0, R_year - 0.16 x max(0, GR - T_year))
```

with `R_year` / `T_year` the §45U(c)(1) adjusted 0.3¢ and 2.5¢ amounts.

**As it stood**, `ira.py` folded the 5x into the rate (`SECTION_45U_CREDIT_CENTS_PER_KWH = 1.5`)
and then subtracted an **unmultiplied** reduction. Same $15/MWh below the threshold, but the
phase-down slope was 5x too shallow.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| credit below threshold | $15.00/MWh | $15.00/MWh (unchanged) |
| phase-down slope | 0.16 $ per $ of gross receipts | **0.80 $ per $** |
| zero-out price | **$118.75/MWh** | **$43.75/MWh** |

Both zero-out numbers are pinned in
`tests/unit/policy/test_ira.py::TestSection45UStatutoryOrdering::test_credit_zeroes_out_at_43_75_not_118_75_usd_per_mwh`,
along with the memo §2.3 D=0 rows ($30.80 → $10.36/MWh, $42.85 → $0.72/MWh).

### 1.2 F-3 — the §45U(c)(1) inflation adjustment, from the printed amounts

The memo flagged that its own 2026 figures were **its arithmetic from the factor**, because the IRB
PDF did not parse for it, and asked the implementation to read the notices' printed amounts. Done —
the IRB text was extracted directly and cross-checked against the IRB HTML rendering.

| sale year | factor | source | printed §45U(a)(1)(A) | printed §45U(b)(2)(A)(ii)(II)(aa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 1.0000 by construction | no §45U notice published | 0.3¢ (statutory nominal) | 2.5¢ (statutory nominal) |
| 2025 | 1.0242 (deflator 2024 125.234 / 2023 122.273) | Notice 2025-37, IRB 2025-30 | **0.3¢** | **2.6¢** |
| 2026 | 1.0539 (deflator 2025 128.986 / 2023 122.39) | Notice 2026-41, IRB 2026-29 (2026-07-13) §3.01 | **0.3¢** | **2.6¢** |

Notice 2025-37 prints its own arithmetic verbatim — "0.3 cents (0.3 cents (or $0.003) x 1.0242,
then rounded to the nearest multiple of 0.05 cent)" and "2.6 cents (2.5 cents (or $0.025) x 1.0242,
then rounded to the nearest multiple of 0.1 cent)" — which independently confirms the rounding
denominations the memo could only take from LII's rendering of §45U(c)(2).

Two corrections to the memo's numbers, neither material: the 2026 factor as printed is **1.0539**,
not 1.053, and **2025 already carries the 2.6¢ threshold** — the memo treated the shift as a 2026
event. The resulting applicable amounts are the same pair the memo derived.

**Years beyond the last published notice HOLD the last published pair** (and years before the first
hold the first). Deterministic, zero DOF, and cited as such: no factor is published for a forward
year, and inventing an escalator would be a tuned forward assumption with no primary source. The
hold is one-sided conservative — a frozen threshold understates `T_year`, which understates the
credit. **F-3 landed without a tunable**, so no escalation was needed.

F-4 (no start-year gate) is memo-noted and forecast-inert; out of scope, untouched.

## 2. The before/after credit curve

At the nominal amounts (`R`=0.3¢, `T`=2.5¢), $/MWh against gross receipts:

| GR $/MWh | before | after (F-1) | delta |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20.00 | 15.00 | 15.00 | 0.00 |
| 25.00 | 15.00 | 15.00 | 0.00 |
| 30.80 | 14.07 | 10.36 | 3.71 |
| 42.85 | 12.14 | 0.72 | 11.42 |
| **43.75** | 12.00 | **0.00** | **12.00** ← max delta |
| 60.00 | 9.40 | 0.00 | 9.40 |
| 118.75 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

The **maximum possible** revenue the fix removes is **$12.00/MWh**, at exactly the new zero-out
price; beyond it the pre-fix credit keeps decaying to its own $118.75 death and the gap narrows
back to zero. F-3 then adds back $0.80/MWh wherever the credit is live and unphased (the 2.6¢
threshold), so the net effect at the 2025 basis is $12.14 → $1.52/MWh rather than → $0.72.

## 3. Stage 1 — per-unit measurement, no solve

`scripts/probes/_f1_45u_stage1_margin.py`. Fleet from the raw EIA-860 operable file: the 10-plant /
13-unit MISO nuclear fleet, summer capacity summing to **11,519 MW** (reproducing the figure
`constants.py`'s `NUCLEAR_MONTHLY_CF_BY_YEAR["MISO"]` comment cites). Capacity factor from the
shipped measured EIA-923 monthly overlay. Nuclear variable cost from shipped config:
0.576 $/MMBtu x 10.0 MMBtu/MWh + $2.50 VOM = **$8.26/MWh**. Bar = `fixed_om_nuclear` = **$130/kW-yr**.

### 3.1 The credit, before vs after

| year | RT ATC | fleet CF | $1/MWh | before | F-1 only | F-1+F-3 | recovered (F-1) | recovered (F-1+F-3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | $30.80 | 0.8939 | $7.83/kW-yr | $14.07/MWh | $10.36 | $10.36 | $29.1/kW-yr | $29.1/kW-yr |
| 2025 | $42.85 | 0.8978 | $7.86/kW-yr | $12.14/MWh | **$0.72** | **$1.52** | **$89.8/kW-yr** | **$83.6/kW-yr** |

The **F-1-only** column reproduces memo §2.3's today-vs-(c) deltas exactly — $14.07 → $10.36 and
$12.14 → $0.72, ~$90/kW-yr recovered at the 2025 basis. The **F-1+F-3** column is what actually
ships: F-3 returns $0.80/MWh of the F-1 reduction, so the shipped recovery is **$83.6/kW-yr**, not
$90. (Fleet CF differs trivially from the memo's 0.8992 because the probe weights the shipped
monthly overlay rather than dividing measured TWh by nameplate x 8760.)

### 3.2 Margin vs the $130/kW-yr bar — no crossing

Every one of the 13 units carries the same per-kW economics here (one fleet-wide CF, one ISO-wide
price basis), so the table collapses to one row per year:

| year | energy margin | + §45U before | + §45U after | vs $130 bar |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2024 | $176.5/kW-yr | **$286.7** | **$257.6** | both far above — no crossing |
| 2025 | $272.0/kW-yr | **$367.5** | **$284.0** | both far above — no crossing |

The point evaluations alone would not settle it, so the probe also solves for the **crossing band in
CF space** — the interval of capacity factors in which the post-fix margin falls below the bar while
the pre-fix margin sits at or above it (both margins are linear in CF):

| year | crossing band | modelled fleet CF |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 0.405 ≤ CF < 0.451 | **0.894** — outside |
| 2025 | 0.318 ≤ CF < 0.411 | **0.898** — outside |

No MISO reactor is anywhere near that band, and the model applies **one fleet-wide monthly CF to
every nuclear unit**, so no unit can individually land inside it.

Three things make this conservative in the right direction: the energy margin above uses a **flat**
ATC price, whereas the screen sums `max(0, p_t − mc)` hourly, which is ≥ the flat-price value; the
screen adds MISO capacity and reserve revenue on top; and neither addition can push a unit *down*
across the bar.

**⇒ Stage 2 is SKIPPED.** The charter gates the paired MISO forecast solve on a stage-1 crossing,
and there is none — at either committed price basis, at any capacity factor the model can produce.
The margin tables above are the evidence.

## 4. Backcast contact — inert, and pinned

No keeper metric can move, by **reachability**, not by re-solve:

1. `section_45u_credit_per_mwh` has exactly **one** production call site —
   `apply_economic_retirements`, `model/capacity_evolution/retirements.py`.
2. `apply_economic_retirements` runs only inside `evolve_fleet`.
3. `evolve_fleet` has exactly **one** call site in the whole repo — `runner.py::run_scenario_iso`.
4. **A backcast solve never enters `run_scenario_iso`.** It reaches the LP through
   `scripts/run_calibration.py::run_year` and `pipeline.solve.run_energy_solve` — established
   independently of this charter and documented in `run_calibration.py`'s caiso-162 comment ("a
   BACKCAST solve, which reaches the LP through this module and pipeline.solve.run_energy_solve,
   silently ignored the flag").

Links 1 and 3 are the two a future refactor could break silently, so both are pinned by
`tests/regression/test_45u_backcast_inertness.py`. If that test fails, the inertness argument no
longer holds and a §45U change needs a measured backcast re-solve before it can land.

## 5. Scope guards honoured

- **`retirements.py` untouched.** The §45U call site keeps its energy-only basis, its `max()` fold
  and its "owner call pending" annotation. F-1 landed without needing it — the credit function's
  signature is unchanged, so the seam never had to move.
- **No new `ScenarioConfig` field.** The fix is statutory arithmetic and a cited applicable-amount
  table; nothing about it is a scenario choice. No `_CACHE_KEY_OPTIONAL_FIELDS` or defaults-ledger
  entry was needed.
- **Zero degrees of freedom (rule 21).** Every constant is statutory or IRS-published. The
  hold-last-published rule is deterministic, not fitted.
- **Rule 26 `[R-DELETE]`.** `SECTION_45U_CREDIT_CENTS_PER_KWH` — the pre-multiplied 1.5¢ rate that
  *was* the defect — is **removed**, not zeroed. A constant that still parses is a re-armable
  answer key.
- **Mechanism matrix.** The `ira_credits` row's citation records the fix, the primary sources, the
  stage-1 measurement and the stage-2 skip. **No cell verdict moves** — this is a bug fix, not a
  lever test.
