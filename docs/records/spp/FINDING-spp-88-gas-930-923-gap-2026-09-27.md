- **Scorer bench − raw 923** has two parts. (i) CHP classes are scaled to about 0.65 of their EIA-923 net in every year
  (−1.6 to −1.8 TWh). (ii) In 2023–25, the fossil reconcile scale raises gas (×1.039 / 1.054 / 1.065), which SPP-87
  already showed is pushing coal station service onto gas targets. `OTHER_FOSSIL` is a pure re-label and nets to zero.
- **So in the train tier (2023–25) the whole bench − 930 gap is the reconcile scale.** Raw EIA-923 and EIA-930 gas agree
  within 1.4 TWh there. This belongs to SPP-87's open basis question, not a new object.
- **In 2019–22 the raw gap is real:** EIA-923 reports 2.7–5.1 TWh/yr of SWPP-BA gas that SPP's metered mix does not
  carry. The portal matches EIA-930 in every year (within 1.4 TWh), so both of SPP's publications agree and the
  difference is on the EIA-923 side of the boundary.

## 2. It closes in 2024, in SPP's metering and not in the fleet

- Monthly EIA-930 − EIA-923 gas runs a steady **−0.2 to −0.5 TWh/month** from 2019 to mid-2023, then goes to about 0
  in 2024–25. It drifts rather than steps.
- SPP's own **"Gas Self" category jumps +2.8 TWh in 2024** (6.3 → 9.15), with no matching rise in EIA-923 gas or CHP
  output (frame CHP net 5.22 → 5.39).
- **Total** EIA-930 generation minus total EIA-923 steps from **+2.7 … +4.4** TWh (2019–23) to **+7.5 / +8.5** (2024 /
  2025). The model's demand is EIA-930 generation (SPP-87), so **the demand basis carries the same 2024 break**.
- The CEMS hourly fit (930 gas on CEMS gross of the frame's gas plants) has slope 0.98–1.01 on non-CHP plants. The CHP
  coefficient is unstable (0.54–1.32, collinear) and cannot identify the missing plants. J. Lamar Stall (56565, 2.5–3.5
  TWh/yr) and Elk Station (58835) have **no CAMPD series** at all (the known SPP-46 R-8 gap), so a CEMS-based plant
  attribution cannot be completed from data on disk.

**Reading:** before 2024, some SWPP-BA gas generation reported to EIA-923 (≈ 3–5 TWh/yr: industrial/CHP host load,
self-served or non-registered units are the candidates) was outside SPP's metered fuel mix. In 2024 SPP brought about
3 TWh of it into "Gas Self". **Which plants is NOT resolved here.** That needs SPP resource-registration data, which is
not on disk.

## 3. What it means for scoring (stated, NOT landed)

- **2019–22, rule 14 misalignment:** the model serves EIA-930 demand, which excludes that gas, and C1 scores its gas
  classes against EIA-923, which includes it. The benchmark therefore over-states achievable gas by roughly the
  bench − 930 column (1.0–3.3 TWh). That is **too small to explain CC_REGULAR 2021 / 22 (−9.5 / −10.3 TWh)**, which
  FINDING-spp-88 wind-headroom §3 ties to the all-hours coal/CC merit-order swap.
- **2023–25:** only the reconcile scale is involved, which is already SPP-87 Q1.
- **Not landed**, for the same reasons SPP-87 §4 gives: a benchmark-basis change is a rubric decision, and the rule
  is ISO-agnostic. **Routed into the owner's ruling of this session** ("measure every ISO's gross/net coal alignment
  first"): that successor should also measure (a) gas coverage, i.e. 923 vs 930 gas per ISO, and (b) whether a
  metering-coverage break like SPP's 2024 step exists elsewhere. No determination moves on SPP either way. The train
  tier stays CALIBRATED and the validation-tier FAIL set does not depend on it.
