## 0. Owner report

**The construction, in one paragraph.** The redistribution that splits the measured system wind MW `M(t)` across
zones weights each zone by `cap_z · ramp_z(t) · SHAPE_z(t)`. That share is invariant to a *common* rescaling of
every zone's shape but **not** to a *per-zone* one, so what a wind-shape builder owes downstream is not "a shape up
to an arbitrary per-zone constant" — the claim both builders' docstrings made — but an estimator of each zone's true
fleet capacity factor **on one common absolute scale**. What both shipped was the capacity-weighted mean over the
**six largest plants** of each zone, which covered between **13.8 % and 100 %** of a zone's capacity depending on
the zone (§3.1) and therefore carried a per-zone level bias identified by nothing measured — an undeclared per-zone
free parameter multiplying the split (rules 21 / 24). **R-LEVEL** replaces it with the definition it was
approximating: `SHAPE_z(t) = Σ_{i∈F_z(Y)} cap_i·cf_i(t) / Σ_{i∈F_z(Y)} cap_i` over **every** EIA-860 operable wind
plant in the zone, at each plant's own coordinates and its own hub height, through the **unchanged** 1/7 shear law
and the **unchanged** generic IEC power curve — one line of selection deleted, one constant (`_SAMPLES_PER_ZONE`)
deleted with it, net **−1** free parameter and nothing added. It is now, by construction, the zone's
capacity-weighted fleet capacity factor, and therefore **partition-consistent**: `C_A·SHAPE_A + C_B·SHAPE_B ≡
C_C·SHAPE_C`, so re-cutting a boundary can no longer move any *other* zone's weight. **Forward test (rule 13):
YES, and more strongly than the rule it replaces** — "every operable plant in the zone at the run's information
vintage, at its own coordinates and hub height, through fixed physics, driven by a chosen weather year" regenerates
for any forecast year from the model's own evolved fleet, reads no measured outcome, and responds to new siting, to
taller hubs and to retirements; the six-site rule responded to none of those unless the change happened to land
inside the six largest. The shared rule is expressed **once**, in `scripts/lib/wind_shape.py`, which contains no ISO
name, no per-ISO constant and no ISO branch (test-enforced); each ISO's zones, plants, capacities, hub heights and
clock come from its own registry entry and its own EIA-860 rows, so no SPP number reaches MISO and none of MISO's
reaches SPP (rule 25).

## 2. The repair

### 2.1 The defect, as arithmetic

`renewables._redistribute_preserving_total` gives zone *z* the share
`s_z(t) = cap_z·ramp_z·SHAPE_z(t) / Σ_k cap_k·ramp_k·SHAPE_k(t)`. Multiply **every** zone's shape by one constant and
`s` is unchanged; multiply **one** zone's by `λ_z` and the split moves. So the builders' shared claim — *"The
absolute CF level this script prints is a diagnostic only — nothing downstream reads it"* — is true of a global
constant and **false per zone**, and the six-largest-plants estimator's per-zone level bias `λ_z` was an
unidentified parameter sitting in the middle of the split.

Its sharpest symptom is that the estimator is not partition-consistent: `top-6(A) ∪ top-6(B) ≠ top-6(A ∪ B)`.
Measured on SPP's own plant set (§0 identity table, bottom two rows): re-cutting the boundary changed the *southern
region's own* shape-weighted potential by **15.0 / 15.6 / 15.0 % of its annual energy** and by up to **4.6 GW in a
single hour**, with **not one plant, capacity or weather value changed**. Under R-LEVEL the same quantity is
**exactly zero**. That is the whole defect and the whole repair, in one pair of numbers.

### 2.2 What changed, and what deliberately did not

Changed: the selection (`nlargest(_SAMPLES_PER_ZONE, "cap")` → the whole zone), and the constant is **deleted**
rather than raised (rule 26 — a retained sample cap, or a `--samples-per-zone` flag, is a re-armable answer key for
exactly the channel this closes; test-enforced across all three files). The construction moved to
`scripts/lib/wind_shape.py` so the rule is written once (rule 25, charter item 1); each builder keeps its own
docstring, provenance, clock, CLI and — for SPP — the GenMix reconciliation leg.

Unchanged, and checkable: `SHEAR_EXPONENT` (1/7), `REANALYSIS_HEIGHT_M` (50), `DEFAULT_HUB_HEIGHT_M` (90), cut-in /
rated / cut-out (3 / 12 / 25 m s⁻¹), the model clock, the parquet schema and column names,
`_redistribute_preserving_total`, `_distribute_by_eia860`, `_wind_zone_reanalysis_shapes`, `_WIND_ZONE_SHAPE_ISOS`,
`_WIND_ZONE_SHAPE_GATES`, and **every** `ScenarioConfig` field. No gate moves, no default flips, the solve surface
does not move (§4), and no mechanism-matrix duty is triggered because no solve-affecting field was added.

| parameter | before | after | identification |
|---|---|---|---|
| `_SAMPLES_PER_ZONE` = 6 | a free parameter in both builders, and through the per-zone level bias `λ_z` it multiplied the inter-zone split | **deleted** | never identified by anything measured — that is why it goes |
| per-zone level bias `λ_z` | implicit, unequal across zones, unmeasured | **≡ 1 by construction** | the definition of a capacity-weighted fleet CF |
| anything added | — | **none** | — |

**Net −1 free parameter.** The memo added in §2.4 is not a parameter: it changes no value, proved by byte-identical
cold and warm builds (P7) and by a unit test.

### 2.4 A memo, not a knob

R-LEVEL reads reanalysis at every plant (SPP 230, MISO 360, per year) instead of six per zone, so the shared module
memoises each `(lat, lon, year)` speed series under `data/clean/` — the repo's own home for derived, disposable,
gitignored artifacts. `--no-cache` bypasses it, and the two paths produce the same bytes.
