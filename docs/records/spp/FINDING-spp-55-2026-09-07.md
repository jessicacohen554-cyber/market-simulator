### 1.2 The object, corrected from SPP's own protocols (PRECOMMIT §2.1)

The charter expected the $250 spinning VRL as step 1 and the $50,000 power-balance VRL as the ceiling. SPP's Market Protocols v119
price a **contingency-reserve capacity shortage into the LMP through the Contingency Reserve Demand Curve** — scarcity factor
0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 × (Safety-Net Energy Offer Cap $1,000 + Contingency Reserve Offer Cap $100) = **$275 / $550 / $1,100 per MW**
(§4.1.5(1)(a), §4.1.5.2(1), §8.2.5; §4.1.5(2)(a): "the Energy LMP is increased by the Operating Reserve shortage price"). The $250
Spinning Reserve VRL caps the shadow price of the spin **sub**-constraint (§4.1.4.1(5)); the $50,000 VRL is the energy-balance
relaxation. **The posted MCPs corroborate this to the dollar:** every reserve-short interval's Supplemental MCP is exactly 275, 550 or
1,100 (never above 1,100), and the Spin MCP maxima 1,344.5 / 1,200 / 1,350 are the curve with the $250 VRL stacked on top. All five
reserve zones post identical MCPs in all 315,648 intervals — the BAA-wide curve is the measured structure, and the posted `SPP` row is a
zero placeholder.

### 1.3 The requirement, from SPP's own RSG rule, reconciled to SPP's own posted reserves (PRECOMMIT §2.3–2.4)

`req_t = 0.964 × 1.2 × MSSC_t`, MSSC the largest single reserve-eligible generating unit ≥ 600 MW nameplate, availability-scaled
(RSG Operating Process – East 0820EXT00002 v4.0 §4.1 / §4.2 / §4.3). On keeper-3's fleet the MSSC is Wolf Creek 1 (1,296.3 MW) →
1,500 MW; SPP's posted cleared Spinning + Supplemental (RTBM-OR, 2023 + 2024 archives read to scratch, not landed) has an hourly-mean
median of **1,514 / 1,484 MW** — model ÷ measured median **0.983 / 0.961**, p5–p95 0.94–1.04 in 2023. The one declared misalignment:
in the MSSC unit's refuelling months the model's monthly nuclear derate drops the requirement to 756–1,320 MW while SPP's stays ~1,480
(its MSSC passes to another ≥ 1,250 MW event the construction does not carry) — 2–4 months a year, reading LOW, so it can only make
the family bind less.

### 1.4 The one measured share

`SPP_BA_CR_REQUIREMENT_RATIO = 0.964`: the SPP BAA's §4.3 requirement ratio, posted to members and not published, identified once
before any result as the pooled 2023–2024 hourly-mean median of cleared CR ÷ (1.2 × Wolf Creek 1 nameplate) — 0.973 (2023) /

monthly-derate misalignment above and is immaterial to the verdict (headroom is 3.4–17× the requirement inside the measured window).

## 2. Why there is no solve (rule 29 step 0), and what a solve would have shown

The reserve balance row (`Σ R + Σ shortfall ≥ req`, `R ≤ cap − P` per zone) can carry a positive dual only in an hour where the
reserve-eligible headroom is below the requirement; elsewhere `R` absorbs the requirement out of existing slack at zero cost and
keeper-3's dispatch stays optimal unchanged. Keeper-3's headroom (`headroom.py`: `pmax × availability` over `_reserve_eligible`
rows from the `fleet_only` rebuild, minus the P1 dispatch of the same classes) is **below the requirement in 0 / 1 / 0 hours**, and
that one 2024 hour (h5727, August, demand 50.1 GW, keeper price $247.8) is an hour SPP posted **no** shortage in. Leg (i)(a) is 0.00
in every year and cannot be moved by any admissible requirement level (3.4–17× headroom inside the window), by the width reading, or
by the monthly-derate misalignment (which reads LOW). The owner's rule 29 step 0 — "an arm that has a computable pre-solve gate does
not reach a solve until that gate passes" — supersedes the charter's step (2), so no LP was spent.

**A verification solve remains available:** keeper-3's recipe + `--energy-reserve-coopt` on 2025, ~10 minutes wall clock on this
box, would write `hourly/reserve_family_2025.parquet` with `dual = 0`, `shortfall_mw = 0` in all 8,760 hours (and, on 2024, one hour at
the $550 step: a 151 MW shortfall is inside the second 125 MW band). It is offered to the desk, not run: its outcome is what the
arithmetic states, and rule 29 forbids spending it.

## 3. The structural reading (rule 1 — the object, not the residual)

1. **SPP's contingency-reserve scarcity is a 5-minute object.** 140 / 117 / 109 short intervals a year in 68 / 45 / 37 events of median
   length ONE interval (ASOM 2024 p. 99: ~10 intervals a month; "the scarcity … can be caused by a lack of capacity or a lack of
   ramp", and the MMU cannot tell which). At the hour it survives as a mean-price event in 4 / 3 / 7 hours. An hourly LP with
   perfect foresight, no intra-hour ramp and no deliverability bound on the held MW cannot see it: the requirement is cleared out of
   6–18 GW of slow-unit headroom in 99 % of hours — the miso-38 gate-4 lesson, repeated on SPP's own data, and the reason card P4
   made SPP-56 prove non-inertness before arming anything. **Making this family LIVE is a deliverability question** (a 10-minute
   ramp bound on the held MW — the `miso_reserve_pergen` construction), i.e. SPP-56's object, not a scarcity-curve question.
2. **The C3c tail is not a reserve-shortage object.** 1 / 0 / 0 of the 42 / 59 / 68 > $200 hours carry any reserve-short interval, and
   the reserve-short hours price at a median $32–36. The tail hours are real-time-only (DA > $200 in 0 / 14 / 0 of them), mostly
   system-wide (both hubs > $200 in 32 / 31 / 50) with an elevated hub spread (|N−S| median $37–64 vs $5–7 otherwise): 5-minute SCED
   price formation — ramp scarcity, VRL relaxations, forecast error — at ordinary load, exactly FINDING-spp-price-family §1.3's
   "79–93× gas at the 40th–84th load percentile". No reserve demand curve, at any level, reaches it; neither does any hourly object
   this model has. This is the model-class limitation rule 22's C3c standing rule already ledgers as a caveat.
3. **What the curve WOULD do if the LP were ever short:** exactly what SPP does — price the shortfall at $275 / $550 / $1,100 and lift
   the LMP by the dual (`TestSppContingencyReserveLP`). The mechanism is faithful; it is inert because the LP is never short.
