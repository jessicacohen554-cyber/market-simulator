## 0. Bottom line

**The SPP North↔South link TTC is 3,400 MW** (`iso_configs._spp_config`, replacing SPP-20's
48,700 MW placeholder), by the construction the PRECOMMIT fixed before any limit was read:

> TTC = binding-hours-weighted median over the corridor's identified flowgates of
> **T*_f = L_f / ψ_f** — the North→South transfer at which flowgate f reaches its own effective limit —
> with L_f the 2026 limit-at-bind (median `Real Time Effective Limit` over the constituent's
> BINDING/BREACHED intervals, registry rating where it bound < 100 intervals) and ψ_f the
> constituent's hub-transfer sensitivity identified from SPP's own hourly RT hub spread × shadow
> prices, 2023–2025 (OLS, HC1). Weighted median = **3,355 MW → 3,400** (nearest 100).

| declared condition | result |
|---|---|
| R1 ≥ 3 identified constituents with an L_f | **12** — pass |
| R2 TTC < B_plaus 23,300 MW · R3 < B_hard 37,400 MW | 3,400 — pass, the link **can** bind |
| R4 weighted p25 / p75 span ≤ 10× | 3,355 / 6,437 = **1.92×** — pass |
| R5 ≥ 90 % of daily files pulled | **221 / 221** — pass |

**Its misalignment, stated as rule 14 requires** (carried verbatim into the link's citation
comment): (i) 2026 limits on a 2023–2025 solve — the effective-limit column exists from
**2026-03-17** only (a correction to SPP-14's "2026-01-28", §3); (ii) a flowgate limit is an
element-under-contingency rating, not a corridor capability — SPP publishes **no** North↔South
interface flowgate (§1), so the corridor is `_ercot_config`'s `N_TO_H` case and the element limit
is reconciled through ψ rather than used literally; (iii) ψ is the Nebraska-hub → central-Oklahoma-
hub sensitivity, not the bubble-to-bubble PTDF; (iv) the leave-one-year-out re-fits of ψ move the
same construction to **2,645 / 3,681 / 11,121 MW** (drop 2025 / 2023 / 2024) — the honest
uncertainty, reported beside the pooled 3,400, never averaged into it (§4). The value is rule-14
reconciled Tier-2, never a Tier-1 rated interface.

**The dominant constituent decides it.** The Franklin 161/69 kV transformer (Westar) carried 4,103
of the corridor's pooled 2023–25 binding hours — 44 % of the identified set — at ψ = 0.030 and a
100 MW registry rating (its 86 binding intervals in 2026 read 100.1 MW, so the rule's registry
fallback and the archive agree to 0.1 %). The next constituents by T*: LEC–LAWH 3,487, Sibley
345/161 kV 4,574 / 4,919, Mullergren–Ellsworth 6,408, Cooper–St Joe 345 kV 6,437, Nashua 345/161 kV
9,378, First Creek–Roanridge 13,051 MW.

**The corridor is bidirectional, and the reverse reading agrees.** Eight constituents identify with
ψ < 0 — South→North-loaded (the Viola transformer at Wichita, Stilwell–Redel, Spearville–
Mullergren, Overland Park–Merriam, Craig–Lenexa, Waverly–LaCygne, LaCygne–Stilwell, Coli–Tech). The
same construction on that set gives **4,206 MW**, within 25 % of the N→S figure, so the symmetric
link SPP-20 registered is not contradicted by the corridor's own reverse direction (§6 routes the
asymmetric-pair question to SPP-DESK rather than deciding it here).

**Nothing was tuned.** There is no SPP solve and no SPP residual; the construction, the thresholds
and the bounds were pushed first, and the number fell out of them. The six keepers' cache keys are
unmoved (§7).

### 5.2 The residual-blind cross-check (PRECOMMIT §4)

The corridor binds 55.5 / 51.6 / 63.1 % of hours (2023–25) at a mean |shadow| of $140–227/MWh while
the mean RT hub spread is $1.9–8.4/MWh (mean |spread| $12–17, p90 $28–41). A link at 3,400 MW is
far inside the bounds (B_plaus 23,300 from the North's 34,617 MW of non-gas capability less its
11,299 MW minimum load; B_hard 37,400), so unlike the 48,700 MW placeholder it is a limit the
two-zone LP can reach. The identification's aggregate ψ (0.022 single-regressor; 0.060 hours-weighted
over the identified set) sits at the low end of the §4 back-of-envelope 0.05–0.1 — consistent, and
the regression is the measured version of that envelope, not an input to it. No modelled price or
flow was compared to anything: there is no SPP solve.

### 5.3 The misalignment (rule 14), and why each construction was rejected or kept

Stated in PRECOMMIT §3 and carried into the link comment: (i) vintage 2026 on a 2023–25 solve;
(ii) element rating, not corridor capability — reconciled through ψ; (iii) hub pair, not bubble
pair; (iv) `n_s_corridor` is an area-token superset, and the ψ screen is the measured separation
(18 of 30 constituents above the floor do not identify as N→S-loaded: 8 are S→N-loaded, 10 are
Kansas-internal delivery constraints); (v) the link is symmetric while the corridor is directional —
the reverse reading is 4,206 MW.

Construction (A) literal — the dominant flowgate's rating as the ERCOT-GTC analogue — would have set
the link at **100 MW** (the Franklin transformer). Construction (C) literal — the hour-wise minimum —
at ~**64 MW** (Marshall–Knob). Either islands two zones whose measured mean |hub spread| is
$12–17/MWh: that is the `N_TO_H` misalignment rule 14 names, and it is why (A) is kept only as the
skeleton and (C) only as the instrument. Construction (B) — the parallel-path sum — was not
computable: the registries list monitored elements, not the cut-set, and the census found no
North↔South-crossing SWPP element at all except the SPS tie.

### 5.4 What the number is not

It is not a rated interface, not an ITP transfer capability, not a Tier-1 value, and not a fit to
anything the model produces. It is the transfer at which SPP's own most-binding corridor element
reaches SPP's own effective limit, with the element→corridor conversion identified from SPP's own
