## 0. Bottom line

**The arm was killed, as pre-registered, on legs (i) and (iii) of the STOP gate.** The mechanism is built exactly
as declared — one ISO-exclusive field, four MEASURED constants, the rule-18 physics gate, the shared detector — and it
**fired**: 7,091 unit-hours floored, 0.414 TWh of floor volume, 1,019 floored segments none longer than one DA
operating day, D-2 forced share 0.5 % on both classes. What the screen adjudicated is not whether it works but
whether it is the right object for SPP's gas split, and the answer is no, for a structural reason the measurement
makes exact:

- **A commitment bridge can only refuse to STOP a unit the model itself started. SPP's gas problem is units the
  model never starts.** The measured committed-state gap in 2023 is 4,209 GWh (PRECOMMIT §2.5: the real ST_GAS
  fleet holds ~835 MW online at minimum load through 5,914 hours where the keeper dispatches the class at zero);
  the bridge, anchored on the model's own P0 run pattern, can reach **0.414 TWh** of it — a tenth — and the
  commitment-real screen drops **62 % of the CC and 78 % of the ST_GAS P0 runs as phantom** (runs whose own P0
  margin cannot repay one start). The P0 pattern is thin because the LP does not commit these plants at all; there
  is little for a bridge to hold together.
- **Where the CC floor does land, it lands on plants CAMPD says were mostly off.** Leg (i): the floor-MWh-weighted
  share of bridge-floored CC plant-hours in which CAMPD has that plant ONLINE is **0.694 against an unconditional
  online fraction of 0.660** (lift +0.034; bar 0.76) — no better than chance. The D-4 unit-conduct check names
  them: 55178 (0.028 TWh of floor at a measured zero-load share of 0.62), 201 (0.53), 8000 (0.92), 3604 (0.95) and
  the ST_GAS plant 3485 (0.61) — the economic-lay-up / capacity-only membership class nyiso-144 / nyiso-152 found on
  NYISO's bridge. ST_GAS by contrast agrees at **0.968** (lift +0.469): SPP's steam fleet IS the committed-state
  object the bridge describes — it is simply not reachable from the model's P0 pattern.
- **The displaced energy is coal, not CT.** ΔE(CC + ST_GAS) = +0.180 TWh (inside the [0.25 V, 2.0 V] band the
  floor's own arithmetic implies — leg (ii) passes); COAL_PRB −0.124 TWh; **CT_PEAKER −0.044 TWh**, below the
  0.05 TWh noise floor, i.e. "CT not displaced" exactly as the PRECOMMIT declared would be reported rather than
  gated. The gap floor lands in cheap hours where coal is marginal; the CT half of the object (+6.8 TWh over-run in
  2023) is a peak-hour phenomenon this mechanism does not touch.

### 4.1 Why it is a kill and not a tie (E-6)

Leg (i) misses by 0.066 with a lift of +0.034 over chance — a floor that lands on plants at their unconditional
online rate is not anchored to real commitment. Leg (iii)'s five D-4 FAIL plants carry 0.051 TWh, 12 % of the floor
volume, on plants whose own meter is at zero 53–95 % of the year. Neither is within rounding of its bar.

### 4.2 The structural reading (rule 1 — the object, not the residual)

1. **Reach.** 0.414 TWh of floor against a 4.2 TWh measured committed-state gap and a 2024 object of −8.6 / +9.9
   TWh. The bridge's three legs all anchor on P0 runs, and the commitment-real screen — the leg that keeps a bridge
   from extending phantom fragments — shows the P0 pattern is 62–78 % phantom on these classes. The model does not
   start SPP's steam and combined-cycle plants at the times the real market commits them, so there is no run for
   the bridge to hold. The mechanism is structurally sound and structurally beside the point.
2. **Membership.** The CC agreement failure is a membership defect of exactly the nyiso-144 / nyiso-152 class: the
   bridge floors plants that read ~100 % available in the outage extract while their meter says laid up or
   capacity-only (55178, 201, 8000, 3604; 3485 on ST_GAS). `derive_campd_bridge_layup_exclusions.py` exists for
   this; it is a SECOND membership channel and was not chartered — reported, not repaired (§6 R-18).
3. **Displacement.** The floor lands in low-price hours where COAL_PRB is marginal (−0.124 TWh); CT_PEAKER moves
   −0.044. The CT over-run (+6.7 TWh in 2023, +9.9 in 2024) is not a night-gap phenomenon; a night floor cannot
   reach it. The pre-declared "CT not displaced" reading fires.
