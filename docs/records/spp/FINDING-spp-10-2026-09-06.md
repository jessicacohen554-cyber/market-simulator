## The five things the desk needs before sitting #2

1. **Fleet census reconciles.** EIA-860 `BA == "SWPP"`: **828 plants / 715 with operable
   generators / 1,646 generators / 103,330.8 MW nameplate / 97,070.4 MW summer**, in 14
   states. SPP's own MMU publishes 102,792 MW (YE-2024) and 106,291 MW (YE-2025); the
   repo's 2025-Early-Release vintage lands between them at **+0.5 % / −2.8 %**. Every
   technology row reconciles with a stated reason; two carry open questions routed to
   SPP-30 (a −7.7 % coal shortfall against SPP's YE figure, and a CC/CT classification
   seam that nets to +5.1 % on total gas). Audit §2.2–§2.3.

2. **The missing-CEMS list in the plan was wrong on two states — nine files, not twelve
   — and lane SPP-11 CLOSED the gap on the same day.**

   ```
   NE_2023  NE_2024  NE_2025
   NM_2023  NM_2024  NM_2025
   OK_2023  OK_2024  OK_2025
   ```

   **`WY` has zero EIA-860 plants with `Balancing Authority Code == "SWPP"`** — Wyoming
   SPP-adjacent generation files under `WAUW`, a WECC balancing authority — so no `WY`
   extract is needed. **`CO` IS in the footprint** (8 plants, 19.5 MW, all small solar)
   but has **zero CEMS-eligible units**, so it needs no extract either; it should still
   be listed in `campd.ISO_STATES["SPP"]` on the PJM-`NC` precedent. The gap that
   was the critical path: it hid **44.9 % of SPP's CEMS-eligible fossil MW** —
   **61.2 % of gas-CC, 60.1 % of gas-ST, 37.1 % of coal, 31.8 % of gas-CT**, and 294 of
   715 plants. Audit §2.4. **SPP-11 landed OK/NE/NM/WY × 2023–2026 (16 parquets) while
   this audit was being written**, so the critical path is unblocked; the four `WY_*`
   files are **inert for SPP** but harmless, since `load_campd_hourly` filters every
   loaded state to the ISO's own fleet. The correction still binds forward: it is what
   `campd.ISO_STATES["SPP"]` should list.

3. **The committed `SWPP hourly.parquet` carries three defective hours in the training
   window, and the repo catches only one.** The 2023-06-12 21:00 hour is a ~100× unit
   slip on Demand, Net generation **and `NG: WND`** simultaneously (+3.62 TWh of demand,
   +3.59 TWh of wind on 2023 alone); `_screen_demand_spikes` catches its demand leg at
   117.7× the annual median, but **no screen touches the fuel-mix columns**, so the wind
   inflation flows into any benchmark SPP-31 builds. The 2025-06-21 05:00 hour reads
   1,505 MW against a next-lowest of 24,390 MW and SPP's own published 2025 minimum of
   23,378 MW, propagating a physically impossible **33.3 GW net export**; the
   2024-07-19 00:00 hour propagates **11.1 GW**. `_screen_demand_dropouts` flags only
   exactly-`0.0` readings by design, so **neither low-side hour is caught by anything in
   the repo today**. Also: **`NG: BAT` is 100 % null for every hour 2015–2025**, so SPP
   storage output is unobservable in every backcast year. Audit §3.3–§3.4. The low-side
   screen is a **card for the desk**, not something SPP-20 should improvise — it is a new
   screen parameter with a cache-key risk (plan G8) and it is not SPP-specific.

4. **P6 and P7 can be served now, from published or committed evidence.**
   * **P6 `TAIL_THRESHOLD["SPP"]` → $200.** Measured off the committed hub-mean parquet:
     RT hours above $200 are **42 / 59 / 68** for 2023/24/25 versus **15 / 16 / 32**
     above $300. $200 is populated and year-discriminating; $300 is thin.
   * **P7 screen year → 2024**, on a published, residual-blind statistic: SPP MMU
     on-peak day-ahead hub prices (SOM 2025 Fig 4–7) give South − North of **+$2 (2023),
     +$12 (2024), +$2 (2025)**. SPP-12's hourly per-hub series should confirm on mean and
     p90 |N−S| before the screen is committed.
   * Bonus structural fact for SPP-40's screen gate: SPP runs **992 / 1,172 / 1,018 RT
     hours below $0** per year (11.3 % / 13.4 % / 11.6 % of all hours). A model that produces few
     negative hours is wrong even at a good MAE. Audit §4.

5. **Card P1 needs one amendment, and it is substantive.** SPP's MMU reports that
   **seven of the ten highest-shadow-price constraints in 2025 were in Oklahoma**, with
   the two highest-value pockets in **northern and southern Oklahoma**, and names
   **Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Lubbock and Kansas City** as the 2024 Frequently Constrained
   Areas; total congestion rent was **$1.6 bn in 2025**. Under a 2-zone N/S topology,
   both top constraints and three of the four FCAs sit **inside one zone**. Separately,
   SPP's two published hubs are **node clusters — "generally in Nebraska" and "generally
   in central Oklahoma"** — not zonal aggregates, which bounds what a hub spread can
   prove. The audit still **recommends registering 2 zones at W2** (real fuel-mix
   asymmetry stated by SPP itself, a 50.2 / 49.6 % load split, lowest-risk path to a
   keeper), but recommends the desk also:
   * keep **SPP-54 (SPS/Panhandle)** as a pre-declared lever without fixing its rank;
   * **add an Oklahoma-pocket lever** as its competitor; and
   * make SPP-12's binding-constraint grouping **at least three-way** — N↔S corridor /
     SPS-tie / **Oklahoma-internal** / other — because the plan's two-way test cannot
     see the largest object in SPP's congestion.

   Audit §6, with the FIPS/sub-BA mechanics of all three options in §6.3.
