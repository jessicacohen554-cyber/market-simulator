Three classes carry a **stable same-sign** miss, and those are the real objects:

### 1. COAL_BIT — the biggest, the most consistent, and the one miso-260 pushed out of band
Negative in five of six years, mean −4.35 TWh, and **it is the class miso-260's seam
repair took from −7.00 to −10.29 in 2020**, out of the ±8 TWh band. Meanwhile
COAL_PRB **swings** (+3.69 / −5.10 / +7.90 / −1.49 / −4.25 / −11.23). One coal class
persistently short and the other oscillating is an **intra-coal merit-order split**,
not a coal-volume problem — miso-259 already fixed coal's *level* with the fuel-inventory
ceiling (2022 coal error +42.61 → +7.98) and this survived it.

Phase 0 before any solve: the keeper bundle commits `hourly/unit_hourly_<y>.parquet`
with per-unit `mc`, so the BIT-vs-PRB merit crossing is reconstructible with **no LP**.
Ask what separates them — delivered $/MMBtu (both priced through
`coal_plant_monthly_pricing`), heat rate, the take-or-pay tranche
(`coal_committed_takeorpay_regulated=True`), or the PRB sigmoid passthrough
(`coal_prb_passthrough=1.0`, sigmoid off).

### 2. ST_CHP — negative in ALL SIX years, ~−2.5 TWh, the tightest signature in the table
Six of six with a standard deviation under 0.4 TWh is a **level** defect, which is
what lever 3 of the last handoff wrongly claimed of CC_REGULAR. It sits squarely in
the CHP/BTM partition miso-253 was working (`mustrun_chp_btm_holdout`,
`data.chp.chp_btm_pct`), and it is small enough that it will never gate on its own —
but it is the cleanest identification target in MISO right now, and it is adjacent to
the bench-attribution question above. **Settling the attribution may settle this too.**

### 3. ST_GAS — negative in five of six, ~−2.6 TWh
Same shape, one notch noisier. `st_gas_mustrun_per_plant` is armed and D-2 attributes
31.9 % of ST_GAS energy to it, so check the floor before the offer.

## THE FIRST THING TO DO, and it is not a solve

**Settle the bench fuel-family attribution.** It decides whether MISO has a gas object
at all, it is the largest single uncertainty in the ISO's scoring, and it is blocked on
data rather than on compute. miso-253 named exactly what would settle it: *"a
per-generator EIA-930 fuel attribution or MISO's registered-resource roster, neither on
disk — intake work, not a solve."* Both are public. Until it is settled, every
fuel-family number in every MISO document is quotable only with the ±33 TWh caveat
attached, and no lane should spend an LP on a gas hypothesis.
