Evolution & Policy

Policy & Scarcity

How tax credits, RPS mandates, carbon pricing, and operating-reserve demand curves shape the dispatch and drive investment. Plus reserve co-optimization for ISOs that endogenize scarcity in the LP.

①

IRA Tax Credits (§45, §45Q, §45V, §45E)

The Inflation Reduction Act (2022) provides production tax credits (PTC) and investment tax credits (ITC) for clean energy. These credits reduce the effective marginal cost of low-carbon generation in the dispatch or lower the capital costs for capacity investment decisions.

Credit Timeline

Each credit has a scheduled expiration date. §45 Wind PTC and §48 Solar ITC phase out in 2027 (OBBBA), while geothermal (§45E) and CCUS (§45Q) extend further. Hydrogen (§45V) is tied to cost-reduction schedules.

⚡ How they enter dispatch: The §45 wind PTC is applied as a negative marginal-cost adder (wind_mc = −ira_ptc_wind, $26/MWh), driving wind's effective offer below $0. Solar's dispatch MC is unchanged ($0) — its §48 ITC reduces capex, not dispatch cost. Hydrogen (§45V) reduces the fuel cost of hydrogen production. CCUS (§45Q) applies per tonne of CO₂ captured. For capacity investment screens, LCOE calculations deduct the PTC or apply the ITC to capex.

RPS Annual Clean-Energy Floor

Many states mandate a minimum fraction of retail electricity sales from renewable or clean sources. This is modeled in the LP as an annual constraint binding energy-balance rows: each zone must generate (or purchase) at least the RPS target fraction of demand from eligible resources.

The dual on this constraint is the REC (Renewable Energy Certificate) shadow price — the value of one MWh of renewable generation. This REC price competes with exogenous EAC prices (see §6) in new-entry capacity economics: a unit capturing both receives the higher of the two, not both.

The RPS is not a force-build step. Instead, we solve the LP with the constraint active, and the resulting REC shadow price tells us the system's implicit value of one additional MW of renewable capacity. This price feeds next year's entry screen and competes with exogenous credits.

③

Carbon Pricing Resolution

Thermal generators' marginal costs include carbon emissions multiplied by a price per tonne of CO₂. The model resolves a carbon price by priority:

  1. Flat override: if ScenarioConfig.carbon_price > 0, use that ($/tCO₂).
  2. State program: if state_carbon_pricing=True, use measured cap-and-trade (CA) or RGGI auction settlements (Northeast), 2023–2025 actuals.
  3. Trajectory: if carbon_price_path is specified (e.g., 'rff_low', 'rff_mid', 'rff_high'), interpolate from that named curve.
  4. Fallback: $0 if none of the above apply.

ORDC & RCPF Scarcity Curves

Operating Reserve Demand Curves (ORDC) and Reserve Constraint Penalty Factors (RCPF) add a post-solve scarcity overlay when reserve co-optimization is disabled. The overlay lifts the energy price endogenously based on how tight operating reserves are — capturing the real-time cost of potential load shedding.

At 500 MW reserves, penalty hits ~$4,700/MWh (near VOLL). This overlay feeds into next year's entry screen, incentivizing investment in fast-start resources and energy-storage capacity.

Comparison: NYISO RCPF & PJM Stepped ORDC

Different ISOs have different scarcity models:

  • NYISO RCPF (Reserve Constraint Penalty Factors): Nested locational reserve families (10-min spin ⊂ 10-min total ⊂ 30-min total; NYC ⊂ SENY ⊂ East). Penalties stack as deeper reserve categories become binding. Captures congestion-induced scarcity.
  • PJM Stepped ORDC: Discrete steps (Synchronized ⊂ Primary ⊂ 30-min) based on Largest Single Contingency (LSC). Requirement = 1.5 × MSSC. Post-solve overlay like ERCOT but with steeper steps.
  • CAISO: Embedded in energy prices (E-ASM market design). Reserve scarcity is reflected in real-time LMP without a separate ORDC adder.
⚡ Why post-solve? When reserve co-optimization is off, the LP solves for energy dispatch only. Reserve requirements are soft constraints or implicit in startup availability. After the solve, we measure online/offline reserves, compute their LOLP, and add a scarcity penalty to the energy price. This penalty then feeds next year's capacity investment screen. When co-optimization is on, reserves are endogenous LP variables and the overlay is skipped (to avoid double-counting).
⑤

Reserve Co-Optimization (Optional In-LP)

Some ISOs (ERCOT, PJM, NYISO, MISO) offer the option to endogenize reserves inside the LP — clearing energy and reserve products jointly. This allows demand relief and storage to compete for capacity and generates endogenous scarcity prices. When enabled, the post-solve ORDC overlay is skipped.

  • Demand: requirements (MWh/h) set by load, ramp, and VRE forecast error.
  • Shared-headroom constraints: nested products share available capacity.
Shared headroom (ERCOT multi-product nesting)

The objective includes both energy dispatch cost and reserve shortage penalties (ORDC steps embedded as demand-curve steps in the constraint RHS). The dual on the reserve-balance constraint is the clearing price for that product, which can be negative (if overabundant) or high (if tight).

ERCOT Multi-Product Nesting

Regulation Up (fastest) ⊂ RRS (10-min spinning) ⊂ ECRS (30-min online) ⊂ Non-Spinning. Capacity used for a tighter product counts toward looser ones. Load-resource and storage provide AS credits (MWh available minus committed dispatch).

⚡ Impact on dispatch: Reserve co-optimization makes the system reserve-aware in real-time. If reserves are tight, the marginal reserve cost gets internalized into the energy price (via shared-headroom constraints and reserve-balance duals). Dispatch volumes change compared to pure economic dispatch. This results in higher prices and different retirement/entry economics.

When disabled (default for most scenarios), reserve requirements are not explicitly modeled — instead, we use the post-solve ORDC overlay to approximate scarcity. This simplification keeps the LP tractable while still capturing the essence of reserve value in the price tail.

⑥

Environmental Attribute Credits (EAC)

Beyond carbon pricing and RPS, some clean technologies receive exogenous environmental credits as a $/MWh adder to their dispatch or as a $/MW/year capacity value. These are distinct from the RPS shadow price; a unit captures the higher of EAC and REC price, not both.

⚡ Policy layering: A single MWh can be: (1) subject to RPS (if in a state with a mandate); (2) eligible for federal EAC (if the tech qualifies); (3) eligible for state or ISO capacity-market credits (if the region has one). These stack in the capacity investment screen (max of REC and EAC, plus capacity revenue), but a single MWh = one certificate — it's not sold twice.
🎯 Policy Interaction: Tax credits (IRA), mandates (RPS), carbon pricing, scarcity overlays, and reserve co-optimization all feed into dispatch via marginal-cost adjustments or constraints, and into capacity investment via next-year price signals. A tight RPS drives REC prices high, which crowds out exogenous EAC. High carbon prices lift thermal costs, making renewables marginal. Reserve co-optimization makes scarcity explicit in energy prices instead of post-solve. These interactions are why the order of magnitude — and structure — of policy matters as much as the headline rates.
