The hours the grid was actually short

An hour qualifies here when the actual load-weighted zonal settlement price clears $300/MWh — the same tail definition the ERCOT lane uses throughout. The hours are chosen from the measured settlement archive alone; the model’s own price plays no part in selecting them. That matters: it makes this a test the model can fail.

Frequency, not just the mean

The averages flatten a skewed distribution. Counted hour by hour, coal over-runs in 58% of the scarcity hours and wind in 48% — and where they over-run they do it hard, peaking at +1,943 and +2,233 MW. Wind’s median is only +139 MW against a p90 of +1,078: the surplus is concentrated, not uniform.

The cheap stack, taken together

Summing wind + coal + solar + hydro against actual, the model carries a surplus of cheap resource in 118 of 122 scarcity hours — median +871 MW, reaching +3,470 MW. It exceeds 1 GW in 48 hours and 1.5 GW in 21. Against the gas deficit the correlation is -0.77 — close to a one-for-one displacement, which is what a merit-order substitution looks like. This is the “~1.5 GW too much cheap supply” of the ERCOT‑101 diagnosis, measured hour by hour at the scarcity hours themselves.

Method and caveats

Scarcity is defined on the actuals. An hour qualifies when the load-weighted zonal settlement price clears $300/MWh. The model’s own price plays no part in selecting the hours.

Load is an input, so the level matches by construction. The backcast is driven by measured demand; what is compared here is which resources served that load, not how much was served.

Storage is the one leg this cannot test. EIA‑930 publishes no battery series for ERCOT in 2023, and the committed artifacts carry model storage only as an annual total — 0.77 TWh of throughput for the year, 116 GWh in August (~156 MW monthly mean). That bounds the model’s battery contribution at these hours to a few hundred MW against a 1.7 GW gas gap, but it does not resolve it. Whether the model over-discharges batteries into scarcity hours is open, and needs an hourly series on both sides.

The gas sub-classes are model-only. Measured gas is a single series at this grain, so the CC / CT / steam split has no measured counterpart here.

Hydro doubles off a tiny base. +176 MW on 174 MW actual is a real relative error but a rounding error against a 77 GW stack — reported for completeness, not as a finding.

Every figure on this page is read from the ercot_netrev_margin bundle, which was ERCOT’s designated keeper (2026-07-23-ercot100-netrev-margin-keeper) when the finding was written and which that finding left unchanged. ERCOT’s keeper has since moved — it is 2026-08-17-ercot215-arm-decontam (frontend/data/backcast/keepers/ERCOT.json) — and this analysis has not been re-run against it, so read these numbers as the record of that probe rather than as the current keeper’s behaviour.
