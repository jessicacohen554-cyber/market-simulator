The Engine

The LP Core

The linear program formulation: variable layout, constraint families, objective function, and how the duals become locational marginal prices.

①

The Variable Layout

The LP has a single flat column vector containing all decision variables, arranged hour-major: all variables for hour 1, then hour 2, etc. Within each hour, variables are grouped by type.

Per-Hour Block Layout

Hour t block:
P[g,t] × n_gen
W[z,t] × n_zones
S[z,t] × n_zones
Chg[s,t] × n_storage
Dis[s,t] × n_storage
SOC[s,t] × n_storage
Flow[l,t] × n_links
Slack[z,t] × n_zones
Dump[z,t] × n_zones

Variable Definitions

Variable Symbol Meaning Units
Thermal generation P[g,t] Output of generator g at hour t MWh
Wind dispatch W[z,t] Wind generation dispatched at zone z, hour t MWh
Solar dispatch S[z,t] Solar generation dispatched at zone z, hour t MWh
Storage charge Chg[s,t] Energy flowing into storage device s at hour t MWh
Storage discharge Dis[s,t] Energy flowing out of storage device s at hour t MWh
Storage SOC SOC[s,t] Energy stored in device s at start of hour t MWh
Transmission flow Flow[l,t] Power flowing on transmission link l at hour t (signed: positive = forward) MWh
Load slack Slack[z,t] Unserved energy at zone z, hour t (priced at VOLL) MWh
Overgeneration Dump[z,t] Excess generation / curtailment at zone z, hour t MWh
⚡ Renewables are decision variables: Wind and solar are NOT netted from demand. Instead, they are decision variables with capacity-factor upper bounds: 0 ≤ W[z,t] ≤ cf[z,t] × wind_capacity[z]. The LP decides how much wind/solar to dispatch (up to their available capacity). This lets the LP re-curtail endogenously when supply is abundant and prices turn negative.
②

The Objective Function

The LP minimizes total system cost. Each variable has a cost coefficient that is either a real economic cost or a shadow cost (for variables we want to minimize incidentally):

P[g,t]
Thermal generation cost = heat_rate × fuel_price + VOM + emissions costs − credits
W[z,t]
Wind MC = 0 (unless negative offer floor applied for dynamics)
S[z,t]
Solar MC = 0 (unless negative offer floor applied for dynamics)
Chg[s,t]
Storage charge cost = ε (0.001 $/MWh, tiebreaker)
Dis[s,t]
Storage discharge cost = ε + dispatch_adder − EAC
SOC[s,t]
State of charge: no cost
Flow[l,t]
Transmission flow: no cost (congestion managed via constraint)
Slack[z,t]
Unserved energy: VOLL = $5000/MWh (penalty for shortfall)
Dump[z,t]
Curtailment: cost = max(ε, -min(wind_mc, solar_mc) + ε), prevents negative-cost gaming

The objective is to minimize total cost. This will dispatch the cheapest available supply to meet demand every hour.

③

Constraint Families

The constraint matrix contains thousands of rows, grouped into families. Each family models a different physical or market rule.

1. Energy Balance (Mandatory)

Energy Balance Constraints
n_zones × T rows
For each zone z and hour t, generation must equal demand:
Σg∈z P[g,t] + W[z,t] + S[z,t] + Σs∈z Dis[s,t] − Σs∈z Chg[s,t] + Σl incidence[z,l] × Flow[l,t] + Slack[z,t] − Dump[z,t] = Demand[z,t]

The dual of this constraint IS the zonal locational marginal price (LMP).

2. Storage Dynamics (Mandatory for Battery Units)

Storage State of Charge
n_storage × T rows
For each storage device s and hour t, track energy stored:
SOC[s,t] − SOC[s,t−1] − ηchg × Chg[s,t] + Dis[s,t] / ηdis = 0
(Cyclic boundary at t=0: SOC[s,t−1] wraps to SOC[s,T−1], closing the year-end cycle)

Enforces energy conservation: the change in stored energy equals charge minus discharge losses.

3. Generator Bounds (Implicit)

Generator Capacity & Availability
Enforced via variable bounds
pmin[g,t] ≤ P[g,t] ≤ pmax[g] × availability[g,t]
Each unit's output is bounded by its nameplate capacity and hourly availability (outages, seasonal derates, age-based forced-outage probability).

4. Transmission Constraints (Mandatory)

Transmission Limits
n_links × T rows (or static capacity)
−TTC[l] ≤ Flow[l,t] ≤ TTC[l] for each link l.
Power on a transmission link is capped by its thermal transfer capability (measured or modeled). This enforces congestion and price separation between zones.

5. Optional Row Families

Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS)
1 row per year
Σt (W[z,t] + S[z,t] + P[nuclear, z, t]) ≥ rps_target × Σt Demand[z,t]
Forces minimum clean-energy fraction each year. The dual of this constraint is the REC (renewable energy certificate) shadow price.
Hydro Monthly Energy
n_hydro × 12 rows
For each hydro unit and month: Σt∈month P[hydro,t] ≤ monthly_budget
Enforces seasonal hydroelectric budgets (limited water availability).
Reserve Co-Optimization
varies (ERCOT: ~100+ rows)
When enabled: shared headroom, reserve balance, and supply-cap rows that co-optimize energy and ancillary services. See Policy & Scarcity §5.5.
④

From Duals to Prices

How the LP Discovers Prices
When you solve a linear program, you get two solutions: the primal (decision variables: generation, flows, etc.) and the dual (shadow prices: the marginal value of relaxing each constraint). The dual on the energy-balance constraint is the most valuable quantity in the entire model: it IS the locational marginal price (LMP).

Why Are Duals the Prices?

Consider the energy-balance constraint for zone z at hour t. The LP is minimizing cost subject to satisfying demand. If you could relax the demand constraint by 1 MWh — i.e., allow demand to be 1 MWh less — how much would the total cost decrease?

The answer is the dual. If the dual is $45/MWh, relaxing demand by 1 MWh saves $45 of cost. This is exactly the price the marginal unit is bidding — any generator running at that hour would accept that price and want to produce more.

In real markets, prices emerge from bid/offer matching. In our LP, prices are the duals. We don't need a bid-stack simulator; the LP IS the market-clearing algorithm. The duals are the market-clearing prices.

Multi-Zonal Congestion

When transmission is uncongested (flows below TTC), zones have similar prices — the marginal cost of supplying one more MWh is the same everywhere. But when a transmission link hits its limit, the constraint becomes binding. The dual on the transmission constraint then adjusts the LMPs on either side: the importing zone's price rises, the exporting zone's price falls. The price difference compensates for congestion cost.

This is why congestion pricing works. It emerges naturally from the LP's solution structure. No special logic needed — just let the duals do their job. The Solving & Pricing page explains how P0→P1 refines these prices to account for startup costs.

⚡ No separate pricing model: Many dispatch simulators have an LP for dispatch and a separate bid-stack module for pricing. We have just one. The LP solves once; the duals ARE the prices. This is faster, simpler, and mathematically correct.
⑤

Solving with HiGHS

The LP is solved using HiGHS, a state-of-the-art open-source LP solver. The model builds the constraint matrix as a sparse CSC (compressed sparse column) matrix and feeds it to HiGHS via its Python API.

Matrix Sparsity
Typical 8760-hour solve: ~1.8M columns, with far fewer rows (mostly the n_zones×T energy-balance + n_storage×T SOC equalities); a sparse CSC matrix (well under 1% dense)
Algorithm
Dual simplex (fast warm-starts from prior year's basis)
Presolve
Off (the model is already tight; presolve overhead not worth it)
Parallel Threads
Configurable via env var MARKET_SIM_HIGHS_THREADS (unset → HiGHS automatic threading)
Solve Time
Typical: 30–60 seconds per year on a modern CPU
P0→P1 Sequence
After P0 solves, P1 only changes the cost vector (startup markup) and warm-starts from P0's basis. Converges in <5 iterations. P1 is the scored run.

Warm-Starting & Cross-Year Basis

The P0→P1 sequence is fast because P1 warm-starts from P0's optimal basis. Instead of solving P1 cold, HiGHS uses P0's variable statuses (in-basis, at-lower, at-upper) as a starting point. Since the matrix and bounds haven't changed, only 1–5 simplex iterations are needed to re-optimize after the cost change.

Similarly, year N+1 warm-starts from year N's basis. Surviving generators keep their statuses, new generators start non-basic-at-bound, and the solve converges in seconds instead of minutes.

This is a key reason the 25-year forecast solves in under an hour — warm-starting buys enormous speed gains.
