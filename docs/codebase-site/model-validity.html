Backcast

Model Validity & Holdouts

How the model earns trust out-of-sample. The three-tier train / validation / locked-test holdout program, the touch-once discipline and its quarantine gates, the forecast-side hindcast, and the backcast-data vs scored vs forecast year spans.

①

Data vs solving

Raw data sits on disk from 2018 through H1-2026. That is not the same as what the model is allowed to solve and score. Under CLAUDE.md rule 22, data flows through two entirely separate channels, and conflating them is the most common way to accidentally launder a holdout year into the calibration.

  • Intake — ungated. Data for any out-of-training year may be fetched at any time, for any ISO, under explicit, session-logged owner authorization. It is validated no-LP only — byte-identity checks and loader-resolvability, never a dispatch solve — and each intake is recorded in the intake_log of calibration-complete.json.
  • Solve · Score · Register — quarantined. Solving a year, scoring its dispatch against actuals, or registering it on the dashboard stays under full quarantine until that ISO carries a calibration-complete marker. Even then, only the frozen-config one-shot is permitted.

②

Three tiers

The holdout program splits every year into one of three disciplines. The distinction that matters most is iterable vs touch-once: whether a miss on that year is allowed to change the model.

TierYearsDiscipline
Train / calibration 2023–2025 The only years ever tuned against. Every keeper is built and scored here — all three in one bundle (rule 16).
Validation holdout 2022 (ladder → 2020–2022 → earlier) Iterable. Unlocked by the calibration-complete marker; a miss may send you back to re-tune 2023–2025. Selection evidence — never a certified skill number.
Locked test 2019 & H1-2026 Touch once, ever. Scored exactly once per ISO with the frozen config; the result stands, whatever it is. 2019 is the clean-regime test; H1-2026 the forward edge.

First 2022, then 2019 — operationally

The order is not arbitrary. Validation comes first because you are permitted to learn from it; the locked test comes last because you are permitted to learn from it exactly once.

1

Declare the ISO calibration-complete

Add the ISO's marker to frontend/data/backcast/calibration-complete.json. This is the gate: it unlocks the rule-22 holdout solves for that ISO and nothing else. No marker → every out-of-training year stays quarantined.

2

Iterate the 2022 validation

Solve the frozen keeper on 2022 and score it. If it misses, you may go back, re-tune the 2023–2025 train window, and repeat — that back-and-forth is exactly the point. 2022 is a model-selection instrument.

↺ miss → re-tune 2023–2025 → re-solve → re-score
3

Fire the one-shot 2019 + H1-2026 locked test

Only when satisfied with the validation, solve the frozen config once on 2019 and H1-2026, record the number whatever it is, and never let it re-enter tuning. A calibration change may respond to a locked-test result only by designating a new, never-touched year as its replacement.

Why 2019 is the honest number and 2022 is not. The moment you iterate against a dataset — re-tuning whenever it misses — that dataset stops being an unbiased out-of-sample estimator. It has, through the selection loop, participated in fitting the model. 2022 is therefore selection evidence: useful for choosing between candidate models, but it must never be quoted as a certified skill number. 2019 (and H1-2026) is the honest out-of-sample figure precisely because it is never iterated against — it sees the frozen model exactly once, so its error is a clean estimate of forward skill. This is the classic train / validation / test separation, enforced as discipline rather than convenience.

How it's enforced

The in-sample window {2023, 2024, 2025} is a hard-coded frozenset in three parallel places, kept as separate stdlib literals by repo convention with a parity test asserting they agree:

scripts/run_calibration_full.py
HOLDOUT_CALIBRATION_YEARS
frozenset({2023, 2024, 2025})
Drives the CLI solve gate.
scripts/audit_keepers.py
CALIBRATION_YEARS
frozenset({2023, 2024, 2025})
Drives the CI keeper-registration gate.
scripts/legitimacy_diagnostics.py
D6_CALIBRATION_YEARS
frozenset({2023, 2024, 2025})
Drives the D-6 holdout-quarantine diagnostic.

Solve gate (CLI). enforce_holdout_year_gate hard-fails any --year outside the window unless both conditions hold:

Requested
--year 2022 or --year 2026 — outside {2023,2024,2025}.
Requires ①
--holdout-authorized passed on the command line.
AND
Requires ②
The target ISO already carries a calibration-complete marker.
Else
SystemExit — the solve is refused.

The marker, not the flag alone, is what turns a holdout year into an authorized one-shot score. Score / registration gate (CI): the quarantine-gates job in .github/workflows/ci.yml runs audit_keepers.py --check (E1–E9 plus the H1 holdout-quarantine check) and legitimacy_diagnostics.py --keepers (D-6 + D-9 + the D-2 recompute-vs-committed staleness check), failing any registered bundle whose solve years breach the window for an ISO without a marker.

The code enforces one binary — the tier distinction is discipline. Every gate above checks a single thing: is the year in {2023, 2024, 2025} or not? The validation-vs-locked distinction is deliberately not machine-enforced — the CI gate is tier-agnostic. So re-solving a locked-test year after its one-shot would be a governance breach, not a CI failure: nothing in the pipeline would stop it. The touch-once rule lives in the operator's discipline, not in an assertion.

Current state. Only NEISO carries a complete marker (declared 2026-07-07); its 2019 + H1-2026 one-shot was scored once with the then-frozen neiso-53 config and stands. The other five ISOs remain fully quarantined for holdout solving.

Forecast-side validation

The three-tier holdout program validates dispatch. The forecast machinery — capacity evolution, entry/retirement, forward invariants — is validated separately, and honestly: its headline results are documented misses, because the job is to measure the machinery, not to certify it.

Capacity hindcast. Initialize the fleet at a 2020 vintage and run the capacity-evolution loop forward, solving {2021, 2023, 2024, 2025} only:

  • 2021 is solved to seed the price/margin signal but not scored.
  • 2022 is a bridge — evolved but never solved, its data never read (rule 22).
  • Run for ERCOT and PJM, in both fuel variants (realized-fuel and as-known / AEO2021).
  • Registered under results/hindcast/ + frontend/data/hindcast/ — outside the backcast registry.

The current runs are documented bad-misses — e.g. ERCOT builds ~0 GW solar vs ~25 GW actual — and that is the point: they measure the capacity-evolution machinery rather than pretending it is calibrated.

Forecast invariants & stability probes.

  • I1–I14 single-run invariants over one scenario cache, plus P1–P3 paired-run invariants (two scenarios differing in one driver) — scripts/check_forecast_invariants.py, wired into .github/workflows/forecast-invariants.yml.
  • D-7 — the overlay-vs-statistical-mode fail-count gap per keeper (reported beside each keeper on the status page, never gating).
  • D-8 — frozen-coefficient stability probe. Both D-7 and D-8 are computed only on 2023–2025, never on a holdout year.

Backcast and forecast spans

Three spans, cleanly separated. Data on disk is the widest; scored backcast dispatch is a narrow governed subset; forecast runs forward from 2026. "The backcast covers 2018–2025" is true of data intake — never of what is solved or scored.

Figure MV3 — implemented today. Data spans 2018→H1-2026; scored backcast dispatch is confined to 2023–2025 (dashed indigo marks = the single authorized NEISO 2019 + H1-2026 locked-test one-shot); forecast runs 2026→2050 per constants.py START_YEAR / END_YEAR.
◇ Roadmap — planned, not yet implemented

The intended 2018–2025 backcast / 2024–2030 forecast crossover

The target design widens the backcast to 2018–2025 and starts the forecast at 2024, with 2024–2025 (extending to H1-2026) scored in both modes against the same actuals to measure the backcast→forecast input gap. This is a design intention, drawn here with dashed bars to keep it visually distinct from what runs today.

⚠️ The 2024–2025 dual-mode overlap is not behaviour — it is a target design. No code path today scores 2024–2026 dispatch in both modes: the forecast horizon starts at 2026 (START_YEAR = 2026), and grepping crossover across the Python sources returns only unrelated coal-sigmoid code. The closest implemented analogue is the capacity hindcast (§5), which overlaps the 2023–2025 backcast years at the capacity layer only, not in dual-mode dispatch scoring. Any description of the crossover — here or in the diagram above — is a planned design, not current behaviour.

🎯 Key takeaway: Data intake is open; solving is governed. Train (2023–2025) is tuned, validation (2022) is iterable selection evidence, and the locked test (2019 + H1-2026) is the touch-once honest number. The code enforces a single binary — in-window or not — and the tier discipline rides on top of it. Forecast-side skill is validated separately and reported as honest misses, and the 2024–2025 dual-mode crossover remains a labelled roadmap item.
