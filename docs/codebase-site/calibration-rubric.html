Calibration Rubric

How a backcast run is graded. The v2.x determination rubric: eight tiered criteria (C1–C8), two-band tolerances, forced-energy budgets, D-4 driver windows, and the frontier-achieved designation.

①

How a run is judged

A calibration verdict is produced by exactly two scripts, and the division of labour between them is the single most important fact on this page. One measures the model's behaviour; the other gates on those measurements and emits a determination. Keeping them separate is what stops the scorer from quietly re-deriving — and thereby re-defining — the very thing it grades.

FileRoleWhat it does
scripts/legitimacy_diagnostics.py Measures Computes the D-1…D-10 legitimacy diagnostics — in particular the D-1 diurnal-shape rows and the D-2 per-class forced-share summary — into the bundle artifact legitimacy_diagnostics.json, alongside a gates block recording the gate values in force when it was written.
scripts/calibration_verdict.py Gates RUBRIC_VERSION = 2.4. Scores C1–C8 per year, applies tiers, two-band tolerances and caveat budgets, and emits one of CALIBRATED, CALIBRATED-WITH-CAVEATS, or NOT-YET. Stdlib-only; reads committed artifacts only and never re-solves the LP.
The one-sentence rule

The diagnostic MEASURES; the verdict GATES. The scorer reads the committed legitimacy_diagnostics.json — it never recomputes it (the diagnostic suite stays the single implementation). Conversely, the diagnostic's own embedded per-row verdicts do not decide the outcome: for C8 the scorer re-gates the artifact's measured forced share against the rubric's current caps, and for C7 it applies its own materiality floor before honouring the D-1 gate result. So a bundle written under an earlier gate value re-scores correctly with no regeneration.

Everything the scorer reads is a committed artifact: the registry sidecar, the run payload (the same numbers the Run Explorer renders), the benchmark parts, the committed day-ahead tail counts, and the bundle's run_config.json / calibration_attestation.json / legitimacy_diagnostics.json. It never touches a solver parquet — P2 is archived and the dispatch parquets are gitignored for keepers. The committed run payload is the model side of every score. This is the reproducibility contract: re-running the scorer on any keeper reproduces its verdict byte-for-byte. For the broader validation program these verdicts feed into, see Results & Calibration.

②

The eight criteria

Twelve scored criterion ids are grouped as C1–C8, each carrying a tier that sets how hard it gates:

  • Load-bearing — certifies the intended uses directly (price level & shape, generation mix by class, system CO₂). Two-band scored where a published commercial comparable exists.
  • Supporting — informative sub-annual dynamics (hourly correlation, storage cycling, the scarcity-tail count). Single wide band; a gross breach still FAILs.
  • Protective — the anti-self-deception gates (C6 governance, C7 diurnal shape, C8 forced share). These catch a run that hits the right numbers through the wrong mechanism.

Any FAIL on any tier forces NOT-YET. The table below is interactive: click a criterion to see its coded threshold and the note behind its status. Badges use the full vocabulary — PASS CAVEAT GROUNDED SKIP FAIL.

Two bands and the caveat budget

Load-bearing criteria with a published external comparable are scored against two bands: a stricter target band and an evidence-anchored commercial-grade outer band. Inside target is a clean PASS; between target and commercial is an auto CAVEAT (classified within commercial band); beyond commercial is a FAIL (reclassifiable to a caveat only by an explicit exceptions-ledger entry naming the measured-input limitation).

determine_from_artifacts scores every criterion-year, applies the exceptions ledger, aggregates per criterion (FAIL > CAVEAT > PASS > SKIPPED), then decides in order:

  1. C6 governance not PASS (FAIL or UNATTESTED) → NOT-YET.
  2. Any FAIL on any criterion → NOT-YET.
  3. Caveat budget exceeded → NOT-YET.
  4. Otherwise → CALIBRATED iff there are zero caveats of any kind, nothing SKIPPED, and no data-blocked target years; else CALIBRATED-WITH-CAVEATS with the reasons enumerated.

Protective ledgered (C7 / C8) MAX_PROTECTIVE_CAVEATS = 1 The v1 hard budget of one, unchanged. C6 itself is never caveatable.
Non-protective ledgered MAX_LEDGERED_CAVEATS = 3 An out-of-band criterion reclassified by an explicit ledger entry as an accepted measured-input limitation. Three recurring data-limitation classes exist by construction (preliminary-923 vintage, EIA-930 storage coverage, data-blocked scarcity series), so the budget bounds excuses without mechanically forcing NOT-YET on data availability.

⚠️ C3a and C3b are single-band in effect

Since v2.3 the two price criteria's target bands were set to coincide with their commercial bands — PRICE_MEAN_TOL == PRICE_MEAN_COMMERCIAL == 0.10 and PRICE_SHAPE_NRMSE_MAX == PRICE_SHAPE_NRMSE_COMMERCIAL == 0.20. So there is no live commercial-band caveat range on price: inside ±10% mean LMP / 0.20 monthly NRMSE is a clean PASS, and outside is a FAIL (ledgerable only as a measured-input limitation). The former stricter ±5% / 0.15 targets survive only as reported magnitudes, never as gates. Do not describe a price caveat range that no longer exists.

Forced energy and the D-4 escalation

C8 is the protective gate on forced energy: the share of a class's annual energy dispatched at a binding, non-exempt min_gen floor (structural must-run — nuclear, CHP-steam, coal take-or-pay — is exempt from the arithmetic). Floors are commitment scaffolding, not the dispatch model, so a class must not lean on them too hard:

  • Caps: forced share below 15% for peakers (FORCED_SHARE_PEAKER_MAX, raised 10% → 15% in v2.1) and below 30% for other merchant classes (FORCED_SHARE_MERCHANT_MAX).
  • Materiality floor: C7 and C8 gate only classes whose annual energy — max(model, actual), so forcing can't hide a class below the line — is at least 2% of ISO load (PROTECTIVE_MIN_LOAD_FRAC). Smaller classes are reported by the D-1/D-2 diagnostics but never gated.

The grounded-above-budget escalation (v2.2) is the key subtlety. A material class above its cap is no longer an automatic FAIL — because forcing can be legitimate past the budget when it reproduces a real grid/RA/AS driver. It escalates to a conditional pass requiring both:

  • (a) Provenance — every binding non-exempt merchant mechanism forcing the class clears D-4 off-window binding: it binds only inside its driver-justified hour window. A mechanism with no declared D-4 window fails this leg by definition (the "no floor without a window" rule).
  • (b) Shape — the class's D-1 hour-of-day profile clears the artifact's own gates (profile r ≥ 0.8, off-peak CV ratio ≥ 0.5), applied to any escalating class.

Both clear → GROUNDED, a clean PASS surfaced as a report note, never a caveat (so the high forcing stays visible and auditable without spending a budget). Either leg fails → FAIL, described as a forcing shape/provenance mismatch — the "forcing variables are wrong" signal. The escalation is scorer-only: both signals already live in every committed legitimacy_diagnostics.json, so keepers re-score in place with no re-solve.

The D4_WINDOWS registry is the driver-window declaration table the provenance leg reads — a dict mapping (mechanism id, class-or-None) → [start, end) local hours, each entry carrying a cited CAMPD evidence base. Off-window forced energy above D4_MAX_OFFWINDOW_SHARE = 5% fails the window.

Version history

The rubric is versioned in the scorer's RUBRIC_VERSION constant. The v2 line is the 2026-07-06 fitness-for-purpose re-anchor: it replaced the v1 HARD/SOFT split with role tiers and evidence-anchored bands, and each subsequent point release is a named owner amendment.

  1. v12026-06 → 07-05

    Original machine-enforced rubric. Mid-line: the 2026-07-02 re-balance (C1 loosened, C3 tightened, soft budget 3 → 2) and the 2026-07-04 addition of the C7/C8 protective gates.

  2. v22026-07-06

    Fitness-for-purpose re-anchor: tiers replace HARD/SOFT; two-band target/commercial tolerances with published anchors; C3c re-scoped to the DA-expressible tail; budgets re-derived (ledgered ≤ 3, commercial-band unbudgeted).

  3. v2.12026-07-06

    C7/C8 materiality floor (≥ 2% of load); C8 peaker cap 10% → 15%; the scorer gates D-2's measured shares against the rubric's caps, not the artifact's embedded verdicts.

  4. v2.22026-07-07

    C8 grounded-above-budget escalation: an above-cap material class passes clean iff D-4 provenance and D-1 shape clear — surfaced as a note, not a caveat.

  5. v2.32026-07-09

    C3a/C3b target bands set to the commercial values (single-band in effect); C5a re-based to the full-plant, CHP-inclusive CO₂ basis (measured BTM CHP host supply added back on both sides).

  6. v2.42026-07-09current

    C3a/C3b score on the like-for-like load-weighted actual (rt_lw/da_lw) instead of the legacy equal-hour hub mean — a wedge that grew with tail realism. The legacy basis remains an explicitly-labelled fallback for ISO-years the retrofit doesn't cover.

Frontier achieved

Frontier achieved is an owner-declared, purely declarative dashboard designation. It means: every named admissible mechanism for the ISO's residual caveat family has been tried on record, and what remains is either inadmissible to close (residual-fitting, CLAUDE.md rule 26) or blocked on data that does not exist publicly. It is never gating and never touches the verdict — build_status.py attaches verdict["frontier"] after determine() runs, purely for rendering a badge and note on the Calibration Status page.

It is frequently conflated with the calibration-complete marker, and the two must not be. One is a gate; the other is a label.

NYISO carries a frontier designation but no complete marker: frontier does not imply holdout authorization, and complete does not imply frontier. Both ISOs earned the frontier label the same way — every hard and volume criterion is in band, and the entire remaining residual is the C3c price scarcity tail, gated by reserve-requirement dynamics the real ISO either has not implemented or that would need new measured identification to add:

  • NEISO — four named mechanisms tried on record (winter fuel-security stack, in-LP reserve co-optimization, measured dynamic reserve requirements, the measured fast-start DA offer surface). The real tail forms while the model still carries several GW of cheaper non-fast-start headroom, so no repricing of the fast-start band reaches it. Further C3c work needs a new measured identification — its own charter.
  • NYISO — the deep >$300 tail's two candidate reserve levers are chased to ground (largest-contingency requirement already in-model as the measured NYCA families; the ORDC/RCPF stack verified complete and SOM-grounded). The sole remaining gap is the net-load forecast-uncertainty reserve increment (IMM Recommendation 2021-1), which NYISO itself has not implemented and which has no published formula — adding it would be residual-fitting.

Closing either tail in-model would violate CLAUDE.md rule 26 (no fitting to a residual). That is precisely why the designation is declarative and non-gating — it records an honest stopping point, not a passing grade. The live FRONTIER badge renders on the Calibration Status page (style .cs-badge.det-frontier). The whole holdout-and-validity program these designations sit within is on Model Validity.

🎯 Backcast match is NOT the objective

A keeper is the most structurally faithful run, not the lowest-error one. The whole point of this rubric is to certify that the model's mechanisms mirror the real market — reserve co-optimization, scarcity pricing, congestion, commitment, fuel physics — and only then to check that the levels land in band. A structurally-correct mechanism stays in even if it makes the fit worse (then you fix the real root cause); a lower-MAE run that is missing real structure is not a keeper. That is why the protective tier (C6/C7/C8) can veto a run the load-bearing metrics would wave through, why forced energy is budgeted, and why "frontier achieved" is an honest stopping point rather than a passing grade. (CLAUDE.md rule 1.)
