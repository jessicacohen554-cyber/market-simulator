The Data Pipeline

From raw EIA and EPA sources to FleetArrays: how the data layer turns public datasets into the numerical inputs the LP dispatch model consumes.

①

The Data Journey: Five Layers

The model consumes electricity data from multiple public sources (EIA, EPA, NOAA, gas price indices) and transforms it through five layers:

①
Raw Source Downloads
Public datasets: EIA-860 (plant inventory), EIA-930 (demand & generation), EIA-923 (fuel costs), EPA CAMPD (emission records), eGRID (CO₂ rates), gas price indices (Henry Hub), weather data (MERRA-2). Stored immutably in data/raw/. Never modified in place.
②
Cleaned & Validated Parquet
Raw sources pass through loader modules (eia_loader.py, campd.py, fuel.py) that parse, validate, and normalize schemas. Output: curated Parquet files in data/clean/ with typed columns, missing-data handling, and range checks. These are disposable — regenerated every run from raw sources.
③
Struct-of-Arrays (FleetArrays)
Cleaned data is vectorized into parallel numpy arrays: pmax[g], pmin[g], heat_rate[g], zone_idx[g], availability[g,t], .... This is the memory-efficient struct-of-arrays format that the LP builder consumes directly — no loops over generators, pure matrix ops.
④
Marginal-Cost Assembly
Arrays combine into generator marginal costs: mc[g,t] = heat_rate × fuel_price + VOM + carbon_price × emission_rate − credits. Fossil fuel costs reflect real delivered prices by zone. Carbon pricing uses either flat config values or state-measured prices (CA cap-and-trade, RGGI auction settlements).
⑤
LP Model Input
The LP builder receives FleetArrays, demand, transmission limits, and policies. It constructs a sparse constraint matrix and solves for dispatch and prices. Duals on the energy-balance constraints ARE the zonal prices.

Data Pipeline Flow

The five-layer data pipeline. Raw public sources flow through cleaned Parquet files, get vectorized into numpy arrays (FleetArrays), combine with fuel costs and carbon pricing into marginal costs, and feed the LP model. Each layer is disposable except the raw sources — they are regenerated from scratch every run.
②

Fleet Construction & CAMPD Binning

The core of the data layer is the fleet module (fleet.py), which turns EIA-860 power plants into LP units with rising offer curves.

Generator Representation

Each generator has basic physics: pmax_mw, pmin_mw, heat_rate, vom (variable operating & maintenance), and emissions: emission_rate_co2, nox_rate, so2_rate. In ERCOT, the default (and calibrated) approach is CAMPD per-plant binning: one physical plant becomes one LP unit split into tranches with different marginal costs.

Tranche-Based Offer Curves

Each plant is split into five tranches forming a rising offer curve:

Tranche Applies To Role Marginal Cost
_mustrun Coal Mine-mouth take-or-pay floor; fuel sunk from long-term contract VOM + carbon (no fuel)
_sync Coal Spot-market coal; price-responsive at real-time gas price Full SRMC
_committed All Part-load when online; carries startup cost and min-run/min-down HR × committed_mult × fuel
_econ All Incremental dispatch above committed; rising cost steps HR × econ_mult × fuel
_peak All Duct-firing / scarcity band at extreme supply shortage HR × peak_mult × fuel

Coal Take-or-Pay & PRB Passthrough

The _mustrun tranche models contracted coal: its marginal cost excludes fuel (already paid). The _sync tranche is spot (non-contracted) coal at full SRMC. The economic coal tranches above must-run use a logistic sigmoid function that adjusts the fraction of cost covered by fuel based on gas prices. Cheap gas → the passthrough sits at its floor (low fuel fraction): coal must discount its fuel cost to clear against cheap gas CCs. Dear gas → the passthrough rises toward its ceiling (full cost, or a markup > 1.0): coal is inframarginal and prices at full SRMC. This captures the real economics of PRB (powder river basin) coal supply without explicit supply curves.

The total offer curve is the concatenation of all five tranches, each with its own capacity (drawn from CAMPD plant binning) and heat-rate multiplier. This mirrors real plants' multi-speed economics.

⚡ Why binning matters: A single thermal plant often has different operating modes: boiler-minimum (must-run), incremental loading (committed/econ), and rarely-used duct-firing (peak). Without tranches, the LP would mis-dispatch by lumping all capacity at one marginal cost. Binning captures the real cost steps, which is essential for accurate pricing in scarcity conditions and accurate revenue attribution to each plant.
③

The ISO-Specific Corpora

The federal sources above are the cross-ISO backbone: they describe every plant in every market on one schema, which is what lets one LP serve nine registered regions. What they cannot describe is how a particular market behaves — what its participants actually bid, where its network actually binds, which units its operator declared unavailable, and what it holds back for reserves. That is what the roughly two dozen ISO-specific corpora under data/raw/ are for. They are the reason a mechanism can be identified from a market's own conduct rather than assumed, and they are why per-ISO verdicts never transfer between markets.

How a corpus enters the model matters more than what it contains — project rule 13 [R-MEASURED] allows measured data as a reproducible physical or market input, never as the answer. Each corpus below is tagged with which of those it is:

  • Solve — read at solve time by src/market_sim/; it shapes the dispatch.
  • Derived → Solve — the bulk corpus is not read at solve time; a small committed artifact derived from it is.
  • Identify — grounds a parameter (a min-load fraction, an offer band, a reserve ρ). Never enters a solve.
  • Score — used to grade the model's output. Never an input, by construction.

Two further directories hold publications rather than data: data/raw/ERCOT/ and data/raw/MISO/ carry Potomac Economics' State of the Market volumes and IMM quarterly reports for those markets. No code opens them — they are hand-read citation sources, and the values taken from them are recorded with their citations in the constants and the parameter-citation register. ⚠ These payloads are not in the repository — and most cannot be recovered from it.

For most of the corpora above, only README.md and SHA256SUMS.txt are tracked; the bulk payload is gitignored. The 2026-08-16 history rewrite also stripped the previously-tracked payloads out of history, so checking out an older commit will not bring them back and any recovery command pinned to a pre-rewrite commit is dead. The recovery route is re-fetch, and every corpus README carries the verified source-URL table and the exact fetch command for it.

Some are unrecoverable from this repository at all — the CAISO OASIS group-zip dailies, and SCED columns dropped before slimming that have since aged past ERCOT's MIS retention window. Size is not the only reason for gitignoring: PJM DataMiner2 data carries a non-member redistribution restriction, and some ISO-NE workbooks sit behind a CAPTCHA-gated form that no committed script can refresh. What is tracked is the small derived artifact each solve actually reads — the CAISO outage-window Parquet, the per-ISO loss surfaces, the PJM virtual-bid multiplier surface and the capacity-deliverability CSVs. Read a corpus's README before concluding its data is missing.

Fuel Pricing & Marginal Cost Assembly

The LP objective is the sum of marginal costs. Each generator's marginal cost is assembled in real time from fuel prices, emissions rates, policy credits, and plant physics:

mc[g,t] =
heat_rate[g] × fuel_price[g,t]
+ vom[g] (variable ops & maintenance)
+ emission_rate[g] × carbon_price[t]
+ nox_rate[g] × nox_price[t] (where applicable)
+ so2_rate[g] × so2_price[t] (where applicable)
− coal_fuel_sunk[g,t] (take-or-pay reduction, apply_coal_tranches)
− ira_credits[g] (45V hydrogen, 45Q CCUS, etc.)
− eac_credits[g] (environmental attribute credits)

Fuel Price Resolution

Natural gas follows a transparent formula:

  • Base price: Henry Hub daily or seasonal trajectory (forecast) or measured daily settlements (backcast).
  • ISO basis: Regional adders (e.g., ERCOT electric-power gas, Algonquin for NYISO) reflect pipeline distance and congestion. Basis shapes are measured real-world price differences stored in config.
  • Monthly/daily refinement: Optional intra-month volatility shapes (mean-preserving) or measured EIA-923 monthly basis swings (backcast only).

Coal uses a logistic sigmoid keyed to gas price, not a flat assumption. The coal supply is split:

  • Contracted (must-run tranche): fuel_fraction = 0, cost = VOM only. Reflects take-or-pay long-term contracts.
  • Spot (sync tranche): fuel_fraction = 1.0, full SRMC — non-contracted coal pays its delivered fuel cost in full.
  • Economic tranches (above must-run): fuel_fraction = sigmoid(gas_price), rising from a floor (~0.3) at cheap gas to ~1.0 (or a markup > 1.0) at dear gas. When gas is cheap, coal discounts its fuel to clear against gas CCs; when gas is dear, coal is inframarginal and prices at full cost.

Oil & Hydrogen use measured or self-grounding formulas. Hydrogen's fuel cost is the minimum of solar/wind LCOE divided by electrolyzer efficiency — it grounds itself in renewable costs with no circular pricing.

🔍 Why real fuel prices matter: Real electricity prices move with commodity costs. Using measured fuel prices (Henry Hub, EIA-923 delivered costs) makes the model's prices responsive to real drivers, which is essential for capacity-economics screens in later years. Flat or estimated prices hide this signal, making new-entry and retirement decisions appear arbitrary.
⑤

Vectorization & FleetArrays

The data layer's final payload is the FleetArrays dataclass: a set of parallel numpy arrays that the LP builder consumes directly. No Python loops over generators. The LP construction uses vectorized operations: np.tile, np.repeat, scipy.sparse.kron, block_diag.

The FleetArrays Struct

Array Shape Meaning
pmax (n_gen,) Maximum capacity [MW] of each generator
pmin (n_gen,) Minimum stable generation [MW] when online
heat_rate (n_gen,) Fuel heat rate [MMBtu/MWh], including tranche multipliers
vom (n_gen,) Variable operating & maintenance cost [$/MWh]
zone_idx (n_gen,) Which zone [0…n_zones-1] this gen belongs to
fuel_type_idx (n_gen,) Fuel code [0…15], maps to FUEL_TYPE_MAP (gas_cc, coal, wind, solar, …)
availability (n_gen, T) Optional; hour-by-hour availability [0…1]. Backcast: outage overlays. Forecast: age-based POF/WEFOR.
min_gen (n_gen, T) Optional; hourly minimum generation floor [MW]. Reliability binding, must-run, CHP steam-following.
emission_rate (n_gen,) CO₂ emission rate [tCO₂/MWh]; used in carbon cost
nox_rate (n_gen,) NOx emission rate [t/MWh]; used when NOx pricing is on

The LP builder then creates column vectors by tiling these arrays across the 8,760 hours, concatenates them, and constructs the sparse constraint matrix. The duality between arrays and matrix shape is key: n_gen × T total columns, grouped as P | W | S | Chg | Dis | SOC | Flow | Slack | Dump.

This vectorization is non-negotiable in the codebase (§2 of CLAUDE.md). It keeps the memory footprint down and makes the LP solve fast — but "down" is relative. A full 8,760-hour year is a memory-bound solve: measured peaks are 5.7–5.9 GB for plant-level ERCOT (7 zones, 13.2M LP columns) and 12.4 GB for plant-level MISO (25.4M columns, 50.3M non-zeros), against a 15 GB host. MISO's peak was 13.95 GB until a 2026-08-19 solution-marshalling fix; it is why project rule 12 caps concurrent per-plant multi-zone runs at about two. It does solve in minutes — 3–6 per year on the plain calibration recipe.

Sources: docs/handoffs/wallclock-baseline-2026-07.md (ERCOT/MISO peaks and per-year walls); results/calibration/FINDING-miso169-15gb-memory-fit-2026-08-19.md (the MISO LP dimensions and the 13.95 → 12.40 GB fix).

⚡ Why this matters for forecast credibility: Real data sources (EIA, EPA, NREL) are the bridge between the model's assumptions and the real world. By staying close to real fuel prices, real plant CEMS rates, and real capacity factors, the model's results carry credibility. When you read a forecast price or a capacity decision, you can trace it back to a real number in a real dataset. No black boxes, no unexplained tuning parameters.
