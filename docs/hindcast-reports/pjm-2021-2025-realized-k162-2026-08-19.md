## Root cause of the retirement-band FAIL and invariant I7

**One cause dominates all three misses, and it is the information gate working as
designed — not a model defect.**

The 2022 bridge-year evolution retires **4,097 MW of nuclear** on `reason="announced"`:
Byron 1+2 (EIA plant 6023, 1,164 + 1,136 MW) and Dresden 2+3 (EIA plant 869, 902 + 895 MW).
The 2020-vintage EIA-860 carries Exelon's planned retirement dates (2021-09 / 2021-11) from
its 2020-08-27 PJM generator-deactivation notifications. Illinois CEJA
(P.A. 102-0662, signed **2021-09-15**) created the Carbon Mitigation Credit programme,
Byron and Dresden were awarded CMCs, and Exelon reversed both exits — so PJM actually
retired **0 GW** of nuclear across 2021-2025 and both plants run today.

The reversal is in the registry (`data/raw/confirmed-retirements/pjm.csv`, four REVERSAL
rows), but `data.confirmed_retirements.load_announced_reversal_plants` applies the RC-1B
hindcast information gate: a reversal is suppressed only once its **own** counter-instrument
existed, keyed on `superseding_instrument_date` (2021-09-15) against this run's vintage
cutoff (**2020-12-31**). The reversal was not yet knowable, so the announced exit correctly
fires. The loader's docstring names this exact case as the worked example. A forecaster
standing at end-2020 with EIA-860 2020 in hand would have retired both plants.

Consequences, all one cause:

|---|--:|---|
| nuclear retired | model 4.097 GW vs actual 0.0 | the announced Byron/Dresden exit |
| false-retire ❌ | 6.645 GW total | nuclear is **62 %** of it |

suppress the gate — suppressing it would leak 2021 information into a 2020-vintage forecast.
