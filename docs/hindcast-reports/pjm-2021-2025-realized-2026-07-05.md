## Diagnostic notes (findings, not fixes — rules 1/11/14)

- **The unscored 2021 seed year is corrupted by 3 bad hours in the raw demand
  source, and the distortion likely propagates into the scored years via the
  2022 bridge.** `data/raw/eia-930/eia_demand_profiles.parquet` (PJM, 2021,
  hour-of-year 6983-6985 — mid-October) carries `raw_mw` values of 4.3×10⁸,
  2.1×10⁹, and 1.5×10⁹ MW against a normal ~90,000 MW backdrop — a ~4-5 order
  of magnitude data defect in the immutable raw source (not a code bug; not
  touched, per the raw/ data being off-limits for in-place edits). This single
  3-hour window drives the reported `peak_demand_mw` for 2021 to 2.147 billion
  MW and, in turn: I3 FAILs (83.75% of load served by slack that year), I7
  FAILs (reliability floor computed as 2.47 billion MW), I12 WARNs (-100%
  reserve margin), I14 WARNs (average price $1,681/MWh, an ORDC/VOLL scarcity
  artifact of the bad hours). **2021 itself is never scored (rule 22), but its
  `prior_results` (price/margin signal) is exactly what the 2022 evolution step
  consumes to make retirement/entry decisions carried into 2023** — so this
  data defect is a plausible confound for some of the additions/retirements
  misses above, on top of the genuine capacity-screen gaps (Byron/Dresden,
  under-retirement of coal/gas_ct/oil). Root-causing which share is data-defect
  vs. screen-gap is out of scope for this diagnostic session (rule 14) —
  flagged for the intake-fix + hindcast re-run that should follow.
- **I9 storage-integrity FAIL** (2023: 6.09% simultaneous charge+discharge of
  throughput; 2024: 1.32%) exceeds the ε-tiebreak's expected near-zero
  simultaneous-dispatch band — a distinct, real finding independent of the 2021
  data defect (I9 is scored on 2023/2024, not 2021). Not root-caused here.
- **The "false-retire" GW (4.097 of 4.106 modelled GW, tagged `nuclear`) is Exelon's
  Byron (plant 6023, 2×1225 MW) and Dresden (plant 869, 2×1009 MW) nuclear stations.**
  The 2020-vintage EIA-860 carries a "Planned Retirement Year" of 2021 for both —
  Exelon's real 2020 retirement announcement — so the model's non-fossil
  announced-retirement channel (`apply_announced_retirements`) executes them in the
  2022 evolution step (consuming 2021's `prior_results` per the rule-22 bridge).
  In reality Illinois passed the Climate and Equitable Jobs Act in September 2021,
  subsidizing both plants and cancelling the retirements — a policy reversal with
  no representation in any admissible model input (it postdates the 2020 vintage
  and is not a physical/market driver rule 14 would admit). This single pair of
  plants accounts for essentially the entire retirement-recall and false-retire
  failure; every *other* actual PJM retirement (coal, gas_ct, oil, biomass) the
  model missed entirely (0 GW modelled) is the "under-retirement" failure mode
  visible in the per-fuel table above, a distinct mechanism (see the additions/coal
  discussion — the economic-retirement screen, not the announced-date channel, is
  responsible for those).
- **Baseline (b) "announced-only" (65.381 GW retire / 147.445 GW add) is a
  scorer artifact, not a PJM number**: `score_capacity_hindcast.py`'s
  `baseline_announced()` reads `data/raw/eia-860/vintage_2020/eia860_generator_{operable,proposed}.parquet`
  without filtering to the ISO's balancing-authority codes, so it returns the
  *nationwide* 2020-vintage planned schedule regardless of the `--iso` argument —
  the identical figures appear in the ERCOT report. Flagged here as a pre-existing
  scorer limitation (not a model or fleet-data issue); left unfixed per this
  session's no-tuning-in-response-to-results scope — a corrected per-ISO baseline
  is a scorer bug-fix for a future session, not a capacity-screen finding.
