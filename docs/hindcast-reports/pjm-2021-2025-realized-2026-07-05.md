## Diagnostic notes (findings, not fixes — rules 1/11/14)

- **[RESOLVED 2026-07-05] The unscored 2021 seed year was corrupted by 3 bad
  hours in the raw demand source, and the distortion propagated into I9 via
  the 2022 bridge.** Repaired at the curation layer — see "Update" below for
  the full writeup (root cause, repair rule, and the I9 re-triage).
- **The "false-retire" GW (4.097 of 4.106 modelled GW, tagged `nuclear`) is Exelon's
  Byron (plant 6023, 2×1225 MW) and Dresden (plant 869, 2×1009 MW) nuclear stations.**
  The 2020-vintage EIA-860 carries a "Planned Retirement Year" of 2021 for both —
  Exelon's real 2020 retirement announcement — so the model's non-fossil
  announced-retirement channel (`apply_announced_retirements`) executes them in the
  2022 evolution step (consuming 2021's `prior_results` per the rule-22 bridge).
  In reality Illinois passed the Climate and Equitable Jobs Act in September 2021,
  subsidizing both plants and cancelling the retirements — a policy reversal with
  no representation in any admissible model input (it postdates the 2020 vintage
  and is not a physical/market driver rule 14 would admit). This single pair of
  plants accounts for essentially the entire retirement-recall and false-retire
  failure; every *other* actual PJM retirement (coal, gas_ct, oil, biomass) the
  model missed entirely (0 GW modelled) is the "under-retirement" failure mode
  visible in the per-fuel table above, a distinct mechanism (see the additions/coal
  discussion — the economic-retirement screen, not the announced-date channel, is
  responsible for those). Unaffected by the demand-defect repair.
- **Baseline (b) "announced-only" (65.381 GW retire / 147.445 GW add) is a
  scorer artifact, not a PJM number**: `score_capacity_hindcast.py`'s
  `baseline_announced()` reads `data/raw/eia-860/vintage_2020/eia860_generator_{operable,proposed}.parquet`
  without filtering to the ISO's balancing-authority codes, so it returns the
  *nationwide* 2020-vintage planned schedule regardless of the `--iso` argument —
  the identical figures appear in the ERCOT report. Flagged here as a pre-existing
  scorer limitation (not a model or fleet-data issue); left unfixed per this
  session's no-tuning-in-response-to-results scope — a corrected per-ISO baseline
  is a scorer bug-fix for a future session, not a capacity-screen finding.
  Unaffected by the demand-defect repair.

## Update 2026-07-05: PJM demand-defect repair + re-run

**The 2021 raw-demand defect noted above has been fixed at the curation
layer and this report re-run at HEAD (bundle
`results/hindcast/pjm-2021-2025-realized/PJM/df18e9c13581a99f`).** Root cause:
`data/raw/eia-930/eia_demand_profiles.parquet` is immutable (CLAUDE.md), so a
new `demand-profile` clean datatype (`scripts/curate_demand_profile.py`,
`data/dictionary/schema/demand-profile.schema.yaml`) repairs it — a
physical-bounds screen (`raw_mw <= 0`, or > 5x the (iso, year) series median;
every legitimate series in the file has max/median <= 2.1) flags impossible
hours, linear interpolation fills them, and a `repaired` column marks exactly
which hours were touched. `eia_loader.load_demand` / `load_demand_meta` now
prefer this repaired series unconditionally (PJM has no dedicated per-BA
hourly extract for any year, so this legacy file is its *only* demand
source).

**The defect was not PJM-2021-only.** Sweeping every `(iso, year)` series in
the raw file (7 ISOs incl. SPP x 2021-2025) found 15 series affected, not
just the 3-hour PJM 2021 spike: CAISO 2021/2022/2023, MISO 2021/2022/2024,
NEISO 2024, NYISO 2024/2025 and PJM 2022/2023/2024 all carry `0.0` "missing
data" sentinel hours (impossible for whole-BA system demand; several
day-long runs coincide with DST transition dates), and SPP 2023 carries its
own order-of-magnitude spike. CAISO and MISO's 2021-2022 draws are live —
their dedicated per-BA hourly extracts don't start until 2023, so those two
ISOs' demand for those years was **also** silently corrupted at runtime
before this fix (PJM's fix is not the only beneficiary; see the curation
script's screen report for the full list). A second, independent bug was
found and fixed alongside it: `scripts/run_capacity_hindcast.py` (invoked
directly, `python scripts/run_capacity_hindcast.py ...`) put its own
directory on `sys.path[0]` rather than the repo root, so
`scripts.lib.clean_io` silently failed to import and the repaired series
never engaged on the first re-run attempt — `load_demand` fell back to the
raw, uncorrected parquet with no error (by design, for the opt-in clean-seam
gating elsewhere in `eia_loader.py`). Fixed by adding the repo root to
`sys.path`, matching `scripts/regenerate_clean.py`'s existing subprocess
bootstrap.

**I9 storage-integrity triage (task item 4): resolved as a scoring artifact of
the demand defect, not a checker bug or a real storage/LP defect.** The
original report called I9 "a distinct, real finding independent of the 2021
data defect" since I9 scores only 2023/2024. That call does not survive
contact with the repaired data: re-running with the fixed 2021 series makes
I9 PASS outright, and directly inspecting the per-unit
`storage_charge`/`storage_discharge` arrays confirms it is not a fluke —
simultaneous charge+discharge is now ~1e-18 of throughput (float noise) in
**every** year, 2021 included:

| year | simultaneous frac (post-fix) | simultaneous frac (pre-fix, from the original report) |
|---|--:|--:|
| 2021 | -5.7e-19 | (not reported; 2021 is unscored) |
| 2023 | -1.5e-18 | 6.09% |
| 2024 | -8.5e-20 | 1.32% |
| 2025 | -2.9e-18 | (not reported; PASS) |

Mechanism: 2021's fake 2.1-billion-MW demand spike drove VOLL-level scarcity
pricing (`$1681/MWh` average, per I14 above) into `prior_results` — exactly
the signal the 2022 bridge and 2023 entry/dispatch economics consume (rule
22's own bridge convention: "2021 seeds the price signal... consumed to make
retirement/entry decisions carried into 2023"). That corrupted price signal,
not a defect in the ε=0.001 $/MWh storage tiebreaker or the LP formulation,
is what pushed 2023/2024 into a degenerate multi-optimum region where HiGHS
picked a solution with non-trivial simultaneous charge+discharge. With the
repaired 2021 series the price signal is sane and the ε tiebreak behaves
exactly as documented elsewhere (`docs/storage-modeling-audit-2026-07.md`
§1.3: "no simultaneous chg/dis via ε or binaries ✅"). No change to
`model/storage.py` or `model/dispatch.py` was needed or made.
