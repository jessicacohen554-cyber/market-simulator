## §2 fixed-payment pattern — does it reproduce? (P-3D)

Extends the `{ercot,pjm}-…-realized-p2c-2026-07-12` re-score to MISO, testing the
`forecasting-entry-exit-assessment.md §2` prediction: the flat capacity payment ÷ going-forward
cost (GFC) is ≥ 1 for every fossil class → **fossil exit is arithmetically impossible**, and
**nuclear is the only class below 1.0**. MISO's registry ratios (`capacity-revenue-fom-ratio-2026-07-13.md`,

**Verdict: MISO is the cleanest reproduction of the §2 pattern in the program.**

- **Fossil-exit-impossible — REPRODUCED, decisively.** The economic screen retired **0 MW of
  every fossil class**: coal **0 vs 10.93 GW** (−100 %), gas_ct 0 vs 2.44, gas_cc 0 vs 0.52, oil 0
  vs 0.50 — all −100 %. No `reason:"economic"` retirement appears in any year. The **coal miss
  alone (10.9 GW, 76 % of MISO's real 2021→25 retirements, 0 modelled) is the single largest
  capacity miss in the whole hindcast program.** This is exactly §2: even MISO coal at the 1.26–1.3×
  cliff-edge — the closest any fossil class comes to a possible loss year — still clears GFC on the
  capacity payment alone and cannot retire. Contrast the energy-only ERCOT control, which
  *over*-retires coal 14.0 GW; MISO under-retires it to zero.
- **Nuclear-inversion — same announced-channel signature as PJM/NYISO.** The only nuclear
  retirement — **Palisades (`1715_1`, 768 MW), reason `"known"`, applied at the 2022 bridge step**
  — is an *announced*-channel event honoring the 2020-vintage EIA-860 Planned Retirement Year
  (2022), **not** the economic screen (which retired 0 nuclear). Palisades really shut May 2022 but
  is undergoing a first-of-kind **restart** (~2025), so the *latest* EIA-860 no longer books it as a
  2021→25 retirement — hence it scores as 98 % false-retire. This is the same mechanism as PJM's
  Byron/Dresden false-retire (announced channel honoring a 2020-vintage nuclear date later reversed
  by policy/economics with no admissible model input). Across all three capacity-market ISOs scored
  to date, the "nuclear-only retirement" the ratio table predicts materialises through the
  **announced non-fossil channel**, while the *economic* screen is inert on every class — fossil
  (payment ≥ GFC) and nuclear alike (the surviving reactors clear their margin + §45U/ZEC).

## Ranked misses & mechanism hypotheses (findings only — rules 1/11/14, no tuning)

1. **coal retirements 0 vs 10.93 GW (−100 %, 16/16 large units missed)** — the program's largest
   single miss. Root cause: `capacity.py::apply_economic_retirements` reads
   `net_revenue = energy margin + reserve value + capacity payment ≥ GFC`; with MISO's fixed capacity
   payment at 1.3× coal GFC (`MARKET_DESIGN` net-CONE 80 × (1−EFORd) vs coal FOM×1.3), no coal unit
   can post the 1 consecutive loss year the screen requires (`retirement_years_coal=1`). BLK-9/BLK-3.
2. **solar additions 6 vs 18.65 GW (−68 %)** — the solar-entry miss (BLK-8), softer than the ERCOT/PJM
   0 GW but still the largest additions shortfall. MISO built 6 GW solar (3 GW in the 2022 bridge, 3 GW
   in 2023) then stopped; the entry-economics stack (cost/queue/negative-price interaction) under-clears
   solar against MISO's real 18.6 GW queue. `capacity.py` economic new-entry + `_NEW_ENTRY_TECHS`.
3. **gas_cc additions +133 % (9 vs 3.87 GW) and wind +67 % (12 vs 7.2 GW)** — over-build driven by the
   **reserve-margin adequacy backstop** (default-on in capacity-market ISOs) + economic entry: chunky
   3 GW gas_cc + 4 GW wind blocks each year push the reserve margin to 26–31 % (I12 WARN, 2025 31.3 %),
   the same over-firm overshoot the corridor/full-horizon work flags. Mechanism: entry sizing +
   `resolve_reserve_margin_build_enabled`, not a retirement screen.
4. **CO2 error rises −7 % → −9 % → +14 % (2023→2025)** — the clearest **fleet-vs-dispatch CO2
   decomposition** in the program. Early years the *dispatch* error dominates (model under-dispatches,
   −7/−9 %, inside ±10 %); by 2025 the accumulated **fleet error** — 10.9 GW of coal reality retired but
   the model kept running — overtakes it and CO2 crosses to **+14 % over**. The sign flip *is* the
   coal-retention failure showing up in emissions: supply the keeper CO2 gap (plan §1.4) to split the two
   formally, but the trajectory already localises it to the retirement screen.
5. **gas_ct 0 vs 1.38 GW and storage 0 vs 0.74 GW (both −100 %)** — minor directional misses; MISO builds
   no peakers or storage over the window (`storage.py` value stack does not clear against the small real
   744 MW).

## Data & scoring caveats

- **Palisades absent from the scoring target (restart).** Plant 1715 (Palisades) is not in the committed
  EIA-860 retired sheet or `capacity_actuals_miso.csv` — the latest registry reflects its ~2025 restart,
  so it is not booked as a 2021→25 retirement. The model's honoring of its 2020-vintage planned-2022 date
  therefore scores false. Directionally defensible (Palisades is coming back), and the same
  announced-channel behaviour as PJM's reversed Byron/Dresden. NYISO's Indian Point is missing for a
  different reason (a pure coverage gap — plant 8907 absent entirely). Flagged, never used to tune.
- **CO2 reference is a facility-exact BA crosswalk, not a state sum.** MISO's footprint spans 15 states
  and overlaps PJM in IN/IL/KY/MI, so a state sum would double-count PJM and grossly overstate MISO CO2;
  `score_capacity_hindcast.py` instead sums CAMPD `co2Mass` over the ORISPL/plant codes EIA-860 maps to
  the MISO balancing authority (rule 11). This yields ~283–301 Mt/yr (vs ~500+ Mt a 15-state sum would
  give). (The auto-generated CO2 note's "ERCOT≈TX" boilerplate does not apply to MISO.)
- **Announced-only baseline (b) is national, not ISO-filtered** (`baseline_announced` reads the whole
  2020-vintage operable/proposed sheets) — same figure across all ISO reports; report-only context, not a
  MISO-specific baseline.
- MISO's BLK-1 forecast blocker (`STORAGE_BASE_FLEET_MW` omitted MISO) is fixed (PR #2223), so this is the
  first runnable MISO capacity hindcast.
