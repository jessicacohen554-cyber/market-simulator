**Does the 2025 overnight worsening reproduce in 2022? No — and the reason is the SPAN's own mechanism,
not a contradiction of it.** The SPAN named the rule exactly: the cut is uniformly *more gas*, so it
*helps* where the keeper **under**-dispatches and *hurts* where the keeper is **already unbiased**.
2022's overnight baseline is **−673.4 MW — under-dispatching, with 2023 (−991.5) and 2024 (−464.2)**,
not with 2025 (+202.4). The rule therefore predicts improvement overnight in 2022, and that is what
happened. **The objection does not bite overnight in 2022 because 2022's overnight bias has the
opposite sign to 2025's, which is a fact about the baseline, not a defence of the cut.**

**But the over-dispatch signature that the objection is really about DOES reproduce, at the fleet
level, and it is the sharpest confirmation in the table.** 2022's keeper fleet mean error is
**−95.6 MW** against a 7,964 MW fleet — **the second near-unbiased year, and closer to zero than 2025's
−107.2**. The SPAN's rule predicts a near-unbiased year is pushed into **over**-dispatch. 2022 crosses
zero to **+417.0 MW — a larger excursion than 2025's +209.0.** Two near-unbiased years, two crossings,
the larger one on the held-out year. The one place 2022 is kinder than 2025: fleet RMSE is flat
(1,806.5 → 1,803.8) rather than worse.

**Net:** the cut buys price level and price shape, and pays for it in fossil volume — in 2022 that
payment lands as a C1 band break and a fleet-mean crossing into over-dispatch, rather than as the
overnight degradation 2025 showed. Same mechanism, different accounting entry.
