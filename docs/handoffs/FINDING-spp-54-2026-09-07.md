### 4.4 What C-4 says the object is — and what it is not

The North gains wind under the three-zone split in EVERY year, and the gain is not the SPS pocket's own shape (that
alone would LOWER the North by 0.55–0.74 TWh, because the Panhandle sample's mean level is higher than the old
South's). It is the residual South's sample: the old South's six largest plants mixed Oklahoma and Panhandle sites;
the new residual South's six are Oklahoma-only and their mean level is lower, so the redistribution — which weights a
zone by `cap_z · SHAPE_z(t)` — hands the North a larger share of the same M(t). The README's "only the relative
inter-zone shape is used" is true of the diurnal SHAPE and not of the LEVEL: six sites per zone are a noisy estimate
of a zone's capacity-factor level, and that level is load-bearing in the split. A zone's level from its whole fleet
(capacity-weighted over all its plants, 254 in SPP) would be the strictly-more-measured construction under rule 14,
with zero degrees of freedom and forward reproducibility; it is the candidate repair routed in §8 R-21. **It was not
applied here**: the number that surfaced it is a window-hour margin, the builder is SPP-32's (and MISO's, by the same
code), and a re-cut after the gate fired is what rules 23 and 29 exist to forbid.
