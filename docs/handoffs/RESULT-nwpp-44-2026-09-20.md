### 1.1 The mechanism behaves EXACTLY as an offer repricing should

**Coal displaces gas one-for-one and nothing else moves.** In 2025 coal rises
+6.692 TWh while the gas classes fall −6.694 in total (CC_REGULAR −5.254, CT_PEAKER
−1.117, ST_GAS −0.108, CC_CHP −0.200, CT_CHP −0.015); in 2023, +2.749 against −2.746.
**hydro, nuclear, wind, solar, biomass and OTHER are unchanged to 0.000 TWh in both
years**, and system total is conserved to 0.003 TWh.

That is the whole argument that this is a real mechanism rather than an artifact, and
it is worth stating against its own counter-example: **wave 1 looked superficially
better** — its coal landed on the EIA actual to 0.01 TWh — and was worthless, because
hydro had collapsed 38 TWh and *every* thermal class rose to fill the hole, including
9.6 TWh of peakers. A repricing that makes 13 coal rows cheaper can only move coal up
and gas down. Wave 2 does exactly that; wave 1 did not.
