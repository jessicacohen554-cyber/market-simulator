## §2 — the envelope every C3c candidate must clear BEFORE it is solved

This is the memo's most important content: it is a **kill-before-solve**
envelope derived from committed data, in the caiso-127 §5 / caiso-129 discipline
that let S1 be killed for the cost of a derive.

A C3c candidate is admissible only if, on a no-LP pre-check:

- **E1 — 2025 spillover ≤ 0.** Its predicted change to the 2025 annual
  demand-weighted mean LMP is **≤ +$0.00/MWh**. 2025 needs no tail and has
  −$0.31 of band room. A candidate that lifts 2025 at all makes the only
  currently-failing C3a year worse. *A candidate whose trigger cannot be shown
  inert in 2025 from committed data fails E1 outright.*
- **E2 — 2024 spillover ≤ +$0.30/MWh** off the tail hours. 2024 has +$0.69 of
  room and the tail lift itself already spends ~+$0.36.
- **E3 — hour-count reach.** Its trigger must cover ≥ 24 hours in 2023 and
  ≥ 18 in 2024 *without the threshold being chosen to achieve that*. The
  threshold must be derived from the source event, not from the count.
- **E4 — no fitted value anywhere in the trigger or the adder** (rules 13
  `[R-MEASURED]`, 24 `[R-DOF]`, 26 `[R-DELETE]`). A residual closable only by a
  tuned threshold is an open root-cause issue, not a parameter.

E1–E3 are computable from the keeper's committed sidecars plus the candidate's
own trigger series. **No solve is authorized until all four are shown to pass
and the gates are committed** (prereg frozen before arm B).

Already killed by this envelope, so they are not re-proposals:

- **`caiso_endogenous_wecc_node` (caiso-114)** — the only mechanism on record
  that has achieved a CAISO C3c PASS. Fails E1 and E2 by an order of magnitude
  (it moved the evening +18/+12/+4 $/MWh, i.e. $2–4/MWh of annual mean). Its
  C3a breakage is now identified in advance as the FINDING-caiso127 §1 storage
  fixed point, and its original justification (C5a) was removed from the rubric
  by v2.9.
- **Any quantity-side derate** — killed by the 12.6–12.8 GW band (FINDING §5).
- **Any offer-curve / heat-rate / offer-rung work** — killed by the stack top
  (FINDING §4).
- **A citygate price threshold** — the only non-arbitrary threshold tried
  (> $8/MMBtu) reaches 27 h in 2023 (clears) and **17 h in 2024** (misses 18).
  Moving it until 2024 clears fails E4.

## §3 — A1 (PRIMARY): arm the caiso-121 corridor / export-path family in surplus

**This is the item to fund first.** It is the only one of the three that
targets a *load-bearing* criterion, has a diagnosed cause, has a family already
selected on the record, and is a structural improvement in its own right
(rule 1 `[R-STRUCT]`) rather than a criterion chase.

**The defect.** CA λ exceeds the model's own WECC_DSW import node by **+$2.61
annually and +$3.87 in Sep–Dec 2025** — CA cannot reach an import node the
model prices approximately correctly, while WECC_PNW is stranded at $4.97
against a CA λ of $46.19. caiso-121 measured the congestion share at
59–101 % across the three years and selected the family: **the corridor's
export-direction deliverability envelope** (measured p95 net-export) and a
**surplus-scoped `caiso_wecc_export_floor`** — noting that the caiso-113 L1a′
rejection was a *fixed hub in ALL hours*, which this is not.

**Size of the required move: −$0.31/MWh**, 0.8 % of the model level. Closing a
quarter of the Sep–Dec congestion term suffices.

**Derive-first gates (all no-LP, all from committed data):**

- **D1 — the export-direction envelope exists and is stable.** Derive the
  measured net-export envelope on each corridor leg from the committed
  interchange series. Gate: pairwise cross-year `r ≥ 0.99` on the hour-of-day
  shape, the caiso-106/107/129 standard. *Rationale: caiso-129 killed S1 on
  exactly this gate; the discharge side failed at 0.9726 while the accepted
  charge side cleared at 0.9919.* **A fail here kills A1 for the cost of a
  derive.**
- **D2 — the envelope binds where the defect is.** The derived envelope must be
  binding (or within 10 % of binding) in ≥ 50 % of the Sep–Dec 2025
  surplus-regime hours that carry the +$3.87 term. A mechanism slack where the
  defect lives is the caiso-129 §3(a) failure mode and is killed here.
- **D3 — direction check.** The mechanism must *reduce* CA λ − WECC_DSW λ. A
  floor that can only add flow in the import direction cannot reduce a
  congestion rent (the caiso-129 §3(c) "a floor can only ADD" form) — verify
  the sign before building.
- **D4 — guards.** C3a-2023/2024 must stay PASS (they have +$3.67/+$0.69 of
  room); C1/C2/C3b/C4/C6/C7/C8 must not regress; C3c must not go *up* in 2025
  past 18 h (the small-count ceiling).

**A/B if the gates pass:** single delta, `--year 2023 2024 2025` in one
invocation (rule 16 `[R-ALLYEARS]`), both arms registered (rule 15
`[R-DASHBOARD]`), `legitimacy_diagnostics.json` generated for BOTH arms before
scoring, arm A verified digit-for-digit against the keeper. Rule-22 LOYO before
any promotion talk. Promotion is a separate owner act.

**Pre-registered kill:** C3a-2025 fails to reach ≤ +10.0 %, **or** any of D4's
guards breaks. Disposition frozen and committed before arm B is solved.

## §4 — A2: re-specify the CAISO LOLP overlay's reserve measure to plant-level ONLINE

**A structural correction, offered as such — not as a C3c fix.** It should be
judged on whether the measure is right, and per rule 1 `[R-STRUCT]` it must not
be rejected because the residual did not move.

**The defect, in the code's own words.** `src/market_sim/results/scarcity.py`
carries an explicit HONESTY GATE for PJM: *"On TOTAL fleet headroom the
perfect-foresight LP carries ~39 GW and never goes short; on per-tranche online
headroom it carries ~0 (the LP runs each tranche bang-bang). The only
defensible measure is PLANT-LEVEL online (synchronized) reserve."* PJM acted on
that. **CAISO's overlay still evaluates `reserve_headroom` on total fleet
headroom** (`caiso_scarcity_overlay` → `reserve_headroom(...)`, `runner.py`
~2029), i.e. precisely the measure the repo documents as never-binding — and
the FINDING measures the consequence: 12.2–13.1 GW minimum against MCL 1,400 MW
and σ 2,500 MW.

**Zero new free parameters.** VOLL $2,000, MCL 1,400 MW, σ 2,500 MW are
unchanged and remain the published CAISO-tariff-backed values; only the
*measure* the LOLP is evaluated on changes, to the basis PJM already uses. This
is a rule-14 `[R-ACCURATE]` correction: the current measure is the inaccurate
input.

**Derive-first gates:**

- **D1 — the online measure is materially smaller.** Compute plant-level online
  (synchronized) headroom on the keeper and show it is < 5 GW in the model's
  own tightest decile. *If it is not, A2 is inert and is killed before any
  solve.*
- **D2 — E1/E2 pre-check.** The predicted adder must satisfy §2's E1 (2025
  ≤ +$0.00) and E2 (2024 ≤ +$0.30). An overlay that fires broadly fails here.
- **D3 — no fitted parameter is introduced.** Verified against `run_config.json`
  and the DOF ledger.

**Blocking prerequisite — a data gap this session hit.** The plant-level online
measure needs **per-unit hourly dispatch**, which the slim keeper bundle does
not carry (it has class-level `class_hourly_<y>.parquet` and per-tech
`storage_<y>.parquet` only). D1 therefore needs either a `unit_hourly` sidecar
added to the solve writer (write-only and solve-invariant, the same shape as the
caiso-127 storage sidecar) or a keeper replay. **The sidecar is the right
answer** — it is the caiso-127 precedent, it is cheap, and it unblocks every
future unit-level CAISO diagnostic instead of one.

**Stated honestly:** §2's band arithmetic says A2's C3c yield is expected to be
**small** — it corrects the measure but does not remove the 12.8 GW band. Fund
it as a structural correction with an honest expectation, or not at all; do not
fund it as a C3c fix and then judge it on the tail count.

## §5 — A3: data intake ask — the SoCalGas OFO declaration record

**Not a mechanism. A measured-input intake, and the only route that could make
C3c-2024 reachable.**

The FINDING establishes that CAISO's 2023/2024 measured tail is a **gas
deliverability** event: 24/47 and 26/35 hours in January, citygate at the 90th
and 99th percentile, the model's own gas passthrough working correctly (CC
offers $103/$121 against year means $61/$42) and λ still stuck at $112/$123
because 22.6/27.5 GW of headroom remains. The real market was pricing a
commodity — deliverable gas — that the LP does not represent.

The repo holds citygate **prices** (`data/raw/gas-prices/caiso_citygate_daily.csv`)
but no **event** record. Without the event record, the only available trigger is
a price threshold, which fails §2's E4 the moment it is tuned to reach 18 hours
in 2024 (the > $8/MMBtu threshold reaches 17).

**Ask:** authorize intake of the SoCalGas / PG&E Operational Flow Order
declaration record (2023–2025), through the `data-intake` skill and the data
contract, as an additively-committed raw source. It is rule-13 `[R-MEASURED]`
admissible on its face — a physical availability event with an exact forward
analogue, the same class as the CAMPD outage windows the model already uses —
and it is inert in 2025 by construction (the 2025 citygate never exceeds
$5.61/MMBtu against 2023's $24.29 and 2024's $17.34), which is exactly §2's E1.

**Explicitly NOT asked here:** any mechanism built on it. If the intake lands,
the mechanism is a separate ask with its own D-gates, and it must still clear
the 12.6–12.8 GW band arithmetic — which, stated plainly, it may not.
