## 2. The mechanism: coal offer prices are fixed at 2024–25 levels while gas moves

The keeper arms `coal_perplant_offer_level`, from ERCOT-144.
- **How it prices coal:** the committed and econ tranches get the ERCOT-144 per-plant SCED offer curves. These curves were measured on **2024–25** 60-Day SCED disclosures only.
- **No fuel term:** the offer level does not move with fuel. The ERCOT-144 claim that levels are "fuel-invariant by measurement" was measured *inside* 2024–25.
- **The 2023 exception:** only 2023 has its own year table (ercot-168, `coal_perplant_offer_curves_2023_ERCOT.json`).
- **What 2019–2022 get:** the table is simply missing those years. The code falls through to the static 2024–25 levels **with no log line** (`legacy_bins.py:1042-1043`).
- **The coal peak tranche:** ercot-192's year table holds only 2023. 2019/2020 keep the static 35.20 level, and that path *does* log the fallback.

The census shows the result:
- The committed/econ offers are **byte-identical in 2019 and 2020**. For example, Martin Lake is $21.53 / $22.23, Parish $17.07 / $21.12 and Fayette's top tranche $80.13.
- Meanwhile the gas stack fell with Henry Hub ($2.57 → $2.03).

- In 2020, 4.6 GW of coal sits above three-quarters of CC capacity. That is 2.8× the 2019 and 2023 figure.
- The same 2024–25 conduct explains the Fayette miss in every year. In 2024–25 Fayette's joint-owner offers put its top 760 MW at about $80, so those MW almost never clear. Actual Fayette output (7.1–10.3 TWh) shows they did run in earlier years.

**The ERCOT keeper supplies no measured coal offer conduct for 2019–2022.** The model prices those years' coal at the owners' 2024–25 behaviour, and in low-gas years that pushes coal behind gas.

## 4. Admissible routes (rules 1 / 13 / 14 / 23): no arm is warranted now

1. **Measured 2019/2020 per-plant SCED offer curves.** This is the ercot-168 construction applied to earlier years, and it is the only route that fits the existing mechanism with zero new DOF.
   - **Blocker:** the source is not on disk. The SCED corpus covers 2023-03..2024-03. ERCOT MIS holds only a rolling ~2-month window, and the historical public-API archive redirects to authentication.
   - **So this is an owner data decision** (a credentialed ERCOT API archive, or a procured history), not a modelling choice.
2. **Reusing the 2023 curves, or picking between extrapolations** by whichever fits 2019/2020, is per-year fitting (rule 1(b)/(c)). It is refused.
3. **Re-coupling the coal econ level to fuel or gas** is the `coal_offer_level_rebasis` lane, adjudicated **R** (DO-NOT-REDO). Only new evidence can re-open it, and that evidence would be exactly the 2019/2020 conduct in (1).
4. **Secondary, rule 14:** the 7 coal plants on base $1.87 in 2019/2020 could take the measured F923 PRB proxy series, which exists for those years. This only touches `_mustrun`, so it is not expected to close a 12–15 TWh gap. It is a candidate for a later input-correction arm.

**Outcome:**
- **Measured:** the mechanism is named, and no shard was launched.
- **2019 / 2020 remain NOT-YET.** These are validation tier and do not gate the ERCOT headline (rule 30(c)).
- **Needs the owner's call:** fetching or procuring the 2019/2020 60-Day SCED Gen Resource disclosures for the 10 coal plants.
