## 2. What was built, and how

The charter's `derive_actual_lmp.py --iso SPP` was executed as `--isos SPP` (the flag's real name).
The substantive design question the charter left open is **where** SPP's block is produced. SPP's raw
market exports are not staged in the repo, so SPP is in MISO's situation, not PJM's — and MISO's
block is built by a *separate* script (`build_miso_lmp_reference.py`) that reduces the committed
hourly parquet. This lane put SPP's path **inside `derive_actual_lmp.py`**, the file the charter
names, as a `_spp()` builder registered in `BUILDERS`:

- It reads the committed `actual_lmp_hourly_SPP.parquet` + `actual_lmp_hourly_zonal_SPP.parquet` and
  returns `(record, None)` — **the `None` is load-bearing**: `main()` only writes a parquet for an
  ISO that produced an hourly frame, so `--isos SPP` can never overwrite SPP-12/SPP-14's committed
  sidecars. Those parquets are an INPUT here, never an output.
- Record schema matches the other ISOs exactly (`da`, `rt`, `da_mon`, `rt_mon`, `da_pct`, `rt_pct`,
  `src`), plus `da_cov`/`rt_cov` (MISO's coverage convention, warranted by the 6–12 unpriced
  hours/year), plus `zones` / `zones_src` / `comment`.
- `--lw-retrofit --isos SPP` then added the rubric v2.4 `*_lw` load-weighted fields, so SPP's C3a/C3b
  actual is on the same basis as the model's, like every other ISO.

`build_calibration_reference.py` gained SPP in `CALIBRATION_ISOS`, `CALIBRATION_YEARS_BY_ISO`
(**2023–2025 only** — §5b), `_ISO_BA_CODE` and the eGRID `BACODE` map (both **`SWPP`**, verified
against the workbook: 661 PLNT23 rows, no `SPP` code exists), and `_EIA923_EXTRA_FUELS_BY_ISO`
(**hydro only**, not oil — §5c).

## 3. P9: the `NG:` unit-slip screen

### 3.1 What was ruled, and what the ruling could not literally be

P9 (RULED r#2) and audit §3.4 recommendation 1 ask for "the SAME median-ratio test
`_screen_demand_spikes` uses (2.5× the annual median)" applied to every `NG:` column, **and** proof
that the six registered ISOs' benchmarks stay byte-identical. **Measured, those two halves are
incompatible as literally worded.** A bare 2.5×-median test on the fuel columns, over all seven ISOs
and 2021–2025, flags:

| ISO | year | series | hours flagged by a bare median test |
|---|---|---|---:|
| MISO | 2023 | solar | 4,116 |
| MISO | 2025 | solar | 4,219 |
| NEISO | 2024 | solar | 3,860 |
| NEISO | 2025 | oil | 3,131 |
| NEISO | 2025 | coal | 2,563 |
| SPP | 2023 | solar | 4,158 |

Those are real midday solar and real peaker starts. The demand screen's own derivation says why its
bar is valid *there* — "every legitimate (iso, year) series in this file has max/median ≤ 2.1" — and a
fuel series has no such property: it runs from zero to nameplate, so its median is not its operating
scale. The largest legitimate max/median ratio measured anywhere is **NEISO 2025 oil at 523×**, which
is *above* SPP 2023 wind's 299×, so no median-ratio threshold whatsoever separates the artifact from
real generation.

### 3.2 What landed instead

`build_calibration_reference._screen_fuel_spikes` repairs an hour only when it clears the demand
screen's bar against **both** scale statistics — the annual median **and** the series' own robust
operating peak (its 99.9th percentile, ≈ the 9th largest of 8,760 hours, immune to a handful of
artifact hours). **The factor is the demand screen's own 2.5×, unchanged; no second threshold is
introduced.** The repair is the same one (`NaN` + linear interpolate + bfill/ffill), and it lives in
the benchmark builder, never in `data/raw` (audit §3.4 recommendation 3).

Scope is the `NG:` columns exactly as ruled — `net_gen` (`NG`) and `interchange` (`TI`) are excluded,
and **that exclusion is load-bearing, not cosmetic**: PJM 2021's `NG` total carries three
int32-overflow hours (2,147,480,064 MW at h6981–6983) whose `NG:` fuel cells are entirely ordinary
(coal 11,275 MW, gas 34,449 MW, wind 4,337 MW). Screening the total would have repaired real fuel
data and would have moved a committed PJM block. Routed, §5a.

**Measured effect over all seven ISOs, 2021–2025 — exactly two series move:**

| ISO | year | series | hours | TWh before | TWh after |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **SPP** | **2023** | **wind** | **1** (h3907, max 3,589,445 MW vs p99.9 22,597 MW) | **106.6345** | **103.0488** |
| NYISO | 2024 | other | 5 (h6759–6763, max 16,117 MW vs p99.9 3,595 MW) | 3.3846 | 3.3486 |

**The 2023 SWPP wind figure the charter asked for: 106.6345 TWh before, 103.0488 TWh after —
−3.5857 TWh, reproducing the audit's ~3.59 TWh.** The repaired EIA-930 total now agrees with the
independent EIA-923 figure (102.1214 TWh) to 0.9 %; before the repair it read 4.4 % high.

The NYISO hit is disclosed rather than tuned away: h6759–6763 is the **same NYIS reporting-gap
window** `_screen_demand_dropouts` already documents for that BA's `Demand` column, so it is the same
artifact class, caught on a series that reference consumes for nothing. Only `wind`, `solar` and
`net_gen` are read downstream, which is why NYISO's committed block is byte-identical regardless.

### 3.3 What this does NOT close — READ THIS BEFORE SPP-40 SCORES C4

The screen closes P9 **inside the artifact P9 named** (`calibration_reference.json`). It does **not**
reach the C4 scoring path. `scripts/run_calibration_full.py::_eia930_frame_generic` calls
`load_eia_hourly_benchmark` directly and writes the run's `e930.parquet`, from which
`render_calibration_html` builds `bench/<ISO>/<year>.json.gz` — the benchmark C4 actually scores
against. **That file is not in this lane's ownership** (`scripts/run_calibration_full.py`; and `src/`
is forbidden outright), so it is routed, not fixed.

Concretely: unless §5a is closed first, **SPP-40's C4 for 2023 will score model wind against a
benchmark inflated by 3.5857 TWh** — 3.48 % on a class carrying ~36 % of SPP's energy, i.e. roughly
1.25 pp of spurious system fuel-mix error, all of it in one hour. It is also worth stating that the
screen's protective effect on `calibration_reference.json` itself is **latent for 2023**: that year's
EIA-923 vintage is complete, so its `generation_twh` wind comes from EIA-923 (102.1214) and would not
have moved either way. The screen's value there is that it stops the inflated EIA-930 figure being
used as the *reference* the completeness guard compares against.

isolates this lane.
