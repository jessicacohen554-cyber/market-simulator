## 2. WHY IT IS THE KEEPER — rules 14 and 1, never the residual

Unarmed, `load_fleet_from_csv` resolves **every** backcast year against the canonical EIA-860 **2025
Early Release** snapshot. Keeper 5's 2023 and 2024 fleets therefore carry a fuel vintage and a unit
population those years did not have.

**Measured registry totals (`run_year(fleet_only=True)`, phase 0):**

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| control `_TOTAL` | 60,749.0 MW | 60,739.9 MW | 60,739.9 MW |
| arm `_TOTAL` | **58,033.6 MW** | **57,564.6 MW** | 60,739.9 MW (no `vintage_2025/`) |
| Δ | **−2,715.4** | **−3,175.3** | 0.0 |

A registry that moves 9.1 MW across three years is the **pjm-167 defect** this field was built for.

Harrington 6193 lands exactly on its own vintage: **2023 → 1,018.0 MW all `COAL`**; **2024 → 679.0
`COAL` + 339.0 `ST_GAS`**; **2025 → 1,018.0 `ST_GAS`**, *correctly*, the units having repowered
Feb/Mar/Jun 2025.

**The footprint is deliberately not confined to plant 6193, and the PRECOMMIT said so before the
solve.** Largest non-Harrington 2023 moves: CT_PEAKER plants **64547** (−442.2 MW, absent entirely),
**64548** (−151.2 MW, absent entirely), **57881** (763.0 → 232.8 MW) — units that **did not exist in
2023** and are correctly absent from the 2023 release. *The wider footprint is the vintage being
right, not the vintage being broken.*

Governing rules: **rule 14 `[R-ACCURATE]`** — *"never revert to an estimate just because it fits the
backcast better… treat the worse fit as a discovered bug"* — and **rule 1 `[R-STRUCT]`** — *"a run is
a keeper because it is the most structurally faithful, not because it has the lowest MAE."*
