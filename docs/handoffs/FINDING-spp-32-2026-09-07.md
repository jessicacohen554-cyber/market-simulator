### 2.5 A corrupt EIA-930 publication hour, and why it does not reach the shares

Scanning the sub-BA export for outliers found **one hour — 2023-06-13 02:00 UTC — in which all
17 sub-BAs are inflated 104×–132×** (OKGE reads 434,669 MW against a 4,157 MW median; CSWS
661,194 against 5,204). It is a single bad EIA-930 publication, not a data-entry error in one
series.

**It does not reach the curated shares, and the reason is structural rather than lucky.** Because
every sub-BA is inflated by the same publication-wide artifact, the *ratio* survives it: the
curated North share at that hour is 0.5209, sitting exactly on the interpolation between its
neighbours (0.5236 → 0.5209 → 0.5174). Normalisation divides the artifact out. **No special-case
handling was added**, because adding one would be un-triggerable machinery. Recorded here so a
future lane that reads the raw MW (rather than the shares) knows the hour is there.

### 3.1 The measurement contradicted the prediction — and the docs were corrected, not the data

The builder's docstring, written **before** the first fetch, asserted the MISO-shaped story: a
nocturnal-jet North against a flatter, afternoon-weighted South. The measurement says the
opposite, and **both docstrings (`build_spp_wind_shape.py`, `paths.py`) plus the `renewables.py`
comment were rewritten to the measurement**:

| night(00-06)/afternoon(12-18) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SPP-South** | 1.04 | 1.06 | 1.03 | **overnight-weighted** |
| **SPP-North** | 0.97 | 0.96 | 0.91 | afternoon-weighted |

The **South** is the nocturnal zone. That is what the meteorology says once you look: the
Great-Plains nocturnal low-level jet's climatological core sits over Oklahoma / Kansas / the Texas
Panhandle — i.e. over SPP-**South** — and weakens northward into Nebraska and the Dakotas. In MISO
the contrast points the other way because MISO-North *is* the upper Plains while MISO-South is the
Gulf. Every doc now carries an explicit "do not carry the MISO intuition across" warning.

The arming proof logs the same numbers at load time, so the claim is checkable without rebuilding:

    SPP per-zone wind SHAPE (2023): 2 zone(s) from spp_2023_wind_zone_shape.parquet,
      measured night/afternoon ratio SPP-North=0.97, SPP-South=1.04

**Fleet split** (EIA-860 operable, the basis for the whole exercise): SPP-North 17,664.3 MW / 135
plants, SPP-South 17,799.1 MW / 119 plants — 35,463.4 MW total, against SPP's own published
35,934 MW of registered nameplate at end-2025 (a 1.3 % EIA-operable-vs-SPP-registered gap; no
adjustment made, since neither number is used as a level).

### 3.2 Reconciliation — ruling (c)

`Wind Self`), **both series normalised to unit mean so the score is invariant to level by

|---|---|---|---|---|

**The weaker diurnal column is a real effect and is reported as one rather than tuned away.**

**The level is never pinned.** `--reconcile` prints and returns; the written parquet is
byte-identical with and without the flag. Nothing in the reconciliation can set a value.

### 4.1 On the "36 rows" gate

The charter's gate reads "the hub csv has 36 rows per zone-month set". The committed CSV is
**annual**, in the sibling schema, because `_zonal_gas_basis_by_zone` filters on `year` and
every sibling table (`pjm_`, `miso_`, `caiso_`, `ercot_`, `nyiso_`) is annual — a monthly CSV
would be unreadable by the loader it exists to feed. The gate is satisfied on the source side and
is **exactly** satisfied: each zone's annual basis is the mean of 12 monthly EIA prints, and each
zone's committed 3-year span rests on **36 monthly source observations**. A test asserts it.
Flagged here rather than silently reinterpreted.

### 4.2 The committed span stops at 2024, on purpose

`N3045OK3` is **intermittent**: EIA prints it for 2022, 2023, 2024 and **nothing** for 2019–2021
or 2025. `N3045KS3` is complete 2019–2025. The table is committed on the **intersection**.

This is the safe choice, not the maximal one. `_zonal_gas_basis_by_zone` returns
`{zone: basis}` for whatever rows it finds, and consumers read each zone with `.get(name, 0.0)`.
Committing the 2025 **North** row without its South partner would hand an armed applier
`{"SPP-North": +1.129}` and let SPP-South default to `0.0` — **a fabricated ~$0.78/MMBtu
cross-zonal spread produced entirely by a publication gap**. With no 2025 row the lookup returns
`None` and the applier no-ops: a *visible* absence instead of an invisible error. A test pins that
hazard as a live demonstration, so anyone tempted to add the row sees the consequence.

### 4.3 The obvious repair was measured and REJECTED

Weighting SPP-South over the states EIA *does* publish would fill 2025. Measured, it must not:

| Year | OK only | OK+TX+NM | TX+NM only | \|TX+NM − OK\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | +0.418 | +0.258 | −0.012 | 0.429 |
| 2024 | +0.861 | +0.451 | −0.239 | **1.100** |
| 2025 | — | −0.483 | −0.483 | — |

TX+NM does not track OK — $0.43/MMBtu away in 2023 and **$1.10** in 2024. Renormalising for 2025
would swing SPP-South from +0.861 to −0.483, a **$1.34/MMBtu year-on-year move manufactured by
which states EIA happened to publish**. That is precisely the "bury the error back inside an
inaccurate input" rule 14 `[R-ACCURATE]` forbids, so the table has a hole where the source has
one, and `SOURCES.md` states what a consumer must decide (re-fetch when EIA prints, or declare a
hold-last rule) rather than deciding it here.

**NO APPLIER IS ARMED.** No `ScenarioConfig` field (G8); nothing reads this in a solve yet.
Arming is SPP-40's / SPP-52's call, and it needs the 2025 question answered first.

**Stated limitation:** KS is only 30 % of SPP-North's gas fleet, so the North row is a genuine
proxy for a zone whose gas also sits in MO / NE / the Dakotas — states EIA does not publish this
series for. Same construction and same limitation MISO-West carries on its IA proxy.

## 5. Reference curtailment rate — ruling (c), both legs measured

`_UNCURTAILED_FALLBACK_ISOS += SPP`, backed by `_spp_wind_reference_curtailment_rate()` reached
through a new `_ANNUAL_REFERENCE_RATE_PROVIDERS` registry.

**Both legs are measured, from two INDEPENDENT SPP publications:**

* **curtailed** — `avg_hourly_curtailment_mw`, printed verbatim in the MMU's Annual State of the
  Market reports (2023 p. 55, 2024 p. 47, 2025 p. 54), landed by SPP-12;
* **delivered** — `avg_hourly_wind_delivered_mw`, the mean of SPP's own 5-minute metered
  generation mix, **measured by this lane** and appended to
  `spp_wind_curtailment_annual.csv` as `derived=yes` rows carrying the formula, per the ruling.

Both are **average MW**, so `rate = curtailed / (delivered + curtailed)` needs no hours-per-year
convention at all and is leap-year-safe — which is why these rows are used in preference to the
table's `curtailed_energy_gwh` rows (those multiply by a flat 8760 and are 0.27 % off in 2024).

| Year | delivered avg MW | curtailed avg MW | **rate** | ASOM's own derived share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 11,818.9 | 1,097.0 | **8.49 %** | (none published) |
| 2024 | 12,559.0 | 1,483.0 | **10.56 %** | 10.53 % |
| 2025 | 12,583.5 | 1,382.0 | **9.90 %** | 9.90 % |

**2023–2025 mean = 9.65 %**, tagged 2025 — about **twice MISO's 4.89 %**, and far past where
explicit re-curtailment moves dispatch. `_SPP_REFERENCE_RATE_YEARS` is 2023–2025, so SPP's real
2019 and 2022 rows — both rule-22 holdout years — are excluded **by construction rather than by
discipline** (a test pins that).

**A bonus resolution for SPP-12.** SPP-12 had to flag `wind_generation_gwh` as resting on an ASOM
sentence whose year labels are internally inconsistent ("dropped … from 110,400 GWh in 2025 to
110,200 GWh in 2024"), and read it as 2024 = 110,400 / 2025 = 110,200. **The metered series
settles it independently: 110.3 TWh (2024) and 110.2 TWh (2025)** — SPP-12's reading is correct in
direction, and the two years are within 0.1 TWh. The metered denominator also reproduces SPP-12's
derived shares to 0.03 pp *without* depending on that sentence at all.

SPP **solar** takes no rate (0.73 % of SPP's curtailment per ASOM 2025 p. 54, no separable
published series) and keeps the delivered profile, exactly as MISO solar does.
