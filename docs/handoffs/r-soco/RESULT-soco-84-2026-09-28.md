## 3. Zero-LP diagnosis of the gap (`scripts/probes/_soco84_price_gap.py`)

Model minus λ ($/MWh), load-weighted, by system-load quintile:

| year | q1 (low) | q2 | q3 | q4 | q5 (peak) | coal-conduct counterfactual C3a |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2019 | +6.6 | +6.3 | +5.6 | +3.6 | −0.6 | +14.0 → +7.1 % |
| 2020 | +6.0 | +5.6 | +4.7 | +3.2 | −0.2 | +15.6 → +7.0 % |
| 2021 | +6.6 | +4.3 | −0.5 | −5.4 | −11.1 | −6.1 → −9.4 % |
| 2022 | +12.3 | +4.3 | +1.5 | −7.5 | −53.0 | −16.1 → −15.0 % |
| 2023 | +5.7 | +4.7 | +3.8 | +0.7 | −7.5 | +1.9 → −3.3 % |
| 2024 | +6.9 | +5.3 | +3.5 | −0.9 | −14.1 | −4.3 → −9.2 % |
| 2025 | +6.8 | +4.9 | +4.2 | +2.0 | −14.5 | −1.9 → −2.1 % |

1. **The model's price curve is too flat, in every year.** The model is about $6–7 above λ off-peak and below it at
   peak. The annual C3a miss is the net of the two: the off-peak premium dominates mild low-gas years (2019–20), and the
   peak deficit dominates 2022.
2. **The "same conduct" test (soco-82/83 out-of-merit coal) explains part of 2019–20, but it is not the cause.**
   - Method: shift model gas output down by the hourly CEMS-minus-model coal gap, and read the model's own per-month
     price-vs-gas curve.
   - Result: 2019–20 halve, to about +7 %, but 2021, 2023 and 2024 get worse. It is not year-consistent. It is also
     already the ledgered, no-admissible-lever conduct (FINDING-soco-82).
3. **Off-peak setter.**
   - Method: a fleet-only rebuild on the soco83 recipe; a unit's offer counts as "at the price" within $0.25.
   - Result: CC_REGULAR sets the price in 46 / 81 / 49 % of q1 hours (2019 / 2022 / 2023), at a median mc of $22.78 /
     $55.71 / $24.06, against λ q1 of $19.69 / $45.47 / $21.04.
   - Southern's Sch. 6 formula is **incremental** HR × replacement fuel cost (FINDING-soco-82 §3). The model offers
     at **average** HR, and a ~0.88 incremental/average ratio lands on λ in 2019 and 2023.
   - Caveat: SOCO-63/64 showed that in this start-cost-free LP, average HR is the only carrier of no-load fuel.
     Incremental HR alone sent 2023 CT_PEAKER +13 TWh. The census must answer that first.
4. **The peak deficit sits in cold snaps.**
   - Winter Storm Elliott (Dec 23–26, 2022) is −4.5 of the −13.0 $/MWh 2022 load-weighted gap.
   - Uri (Feb 2021), Jan 2024 and Jan 2025 lead the other years.
   - Consistent with λ carrying **daily** replacement gas cost while the model uses a monthly gas price.
   - Removing Elliott still leaves 2022 at about −10 %.
