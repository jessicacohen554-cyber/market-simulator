## 2. What landed

### `src/market_sim/data/neighbor_price.py`

`_neighbor_lmp_clean` previously indexed `interval_start_local` — the
DST-*prevailing* wall clock — and took a simple mean over every node in the
partition. It now reproduces **both** halves of the realized product's reduction
(`scripts/data/derive_actual_lmp.py`):

1. **Clock.** `_std_hour_of_year(interval_start_utc, year, std_tz)` — a mirror of
   `derive_actual_lmp._std_hour_index`: convert the authoritative UTC column to
   the ISO's **fixed standard offset**, map (month, day, hour) onto the non-leap
   8760 calendar, drop the standard-clock Feb 29 and any row whose standard-clock
   year is not `year`. This is the chronological clock `eia_loader._eia_hourly_frame`
   sorts by and every model series rides.
2. **Hub.** A per-ISO table, `_HUB_SPECS`, carrying each ISO's standard-time zone
   and its hub's `node -> weight` map, cited to the producer constant it mirrors:

   | ISO | std offset | hub definition | producer constant |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | PJM | `Etc/GMT+5` | every node (the raw export is hub-only: the 12 trading hubs) | `_pjm` / `_hub_mean_hourly` |
   | CAISO | `Etc/GMT+8` | TH_NP15 0.3969 / TH_ZP26 0.0646 / TH_SP15 0.5385, **load-weighted** | `CAISO_HUB_WEIGHTS` |
   | NYISO | `Etc/GMT+5` | the 11 **internal** zones, equal weight (H Q / NPX / O H / PJM excluded) | `NYISO_INTERNAL` |
   | NEISO | `Etc/GMT+5` | the `.H.INTERNAL_HUB` sheet **alone** ("ISO NE CA") | `NEISO_HUB_SHEET` |

   The reduction is one vectorized weighted mean per slot — `Σ(price × weight) /
   Σweight` over `np.bincount` — which degenerates to the simple mean for the
   equal-weight hubs, so all four ISOs take one code path.

   **The table is FAIL-CLOSED.** An ISO absent from it returns `None` rather than
   guessing a clock or a hub. Only the four ISOs `curate_lmp.py` curates have a
   clean `lmp` partition, so nothing is lost today; adding a fifth means
   declaring its conventions first.

The prevailing-clock `_hour_of_year` is **deleted, not left callable** (rule 26
`[R-DELETE]`: a helper that indexes the wrong clock is a re-armable wrong
answer). It had exactly one caller.

Three stale docstring/comment claims in the same file were corrected alongside:
the realized product is on the *chronological* calendar (not "local"), the
module's calendar constants are standard-clock, and the module block comment now
states what "identical" costs and why, with the 2026-07-15 genealogy so the
producer/consumer split cannot silently re-open.

**Which convention matches NYISO's published definition was NOT adjudicated** —
it needs the NYISO data dictionary, and guessing would put a wrong answer into a
committed product. The residual is reported here at full magnitude and is **not**
absorbed by a widened tolerance, a per-ISO carve-out, or a suppressed test: the
parity test pins PJM, which is now clean, and NYISO's number stands in the table

## A.1 Verdict

**NYISO's P-24A "Time Stamp" is interval-ENDING.**

* `curate_lmp.parse_nyiso_zip` — `(ts_end − 1s).floor("h")` — is **CORRECT**.
* `derive_actual_lmp._nyiso_wide` — plain `.floor("h")`, interval-beginning — is
  **WRONG**, and it is the producer of the committed raw product
  `data/raw/_validation-source/actual_lmp_hourly_NYISO.parquet`.

This is the reverse of the direction the fix lane's option **§B** was rejected on
("fix the raw product — measured wrong: raw matches the published source at
1.4e-05"). That measurement stands and is not contradicted: it was made on
**PJM**, whose raw product is bit-exact. It was never made on NYISO's RTD clock,
which is the third defect §4 isolated.

## A.2 What "published" turns out to mean here

**NYISO publishes no prose definition of the P-24A `Time Stamp` column.**
Searched and found silent on it: the Market Participants User's Guide
(`mpug.pdf`, 267 pp — its "data dictionary" governs bid-submission templates, not
the public MIS pricing reports), Manual 12 *Transmission and Dispatch
Operations*, Manual 14 *Accounting and Billing*, the A536 *Real-Time Scheduling
Market Information Posting* Concept of Operation, and the MIS report index
`mis.nyiso.com/public/menu.htm` / `P-24Alist.htm` (whose only notes concern price
corrections and a FERC filing). On the narrow question "does a NYISO document
state the convention in words", the answer is **NOT-ESTABLISHED**.

**The convention is nonetheless established from NYISO's own published data, to
the last published digit** — by a cross-product identity that is stronger
evidence than a sentence would be, because it is NYISO's own arithmetic on
NYISO's own inputs rather than a description of it.

NYISO publishes **two** products off the same 5-minute prices:

| report | file | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **P-24A** | `csv/realtime/<yyyymmdd>realtime_zone_csv.zip` — our source | Real-Time Market LBMP [Zonal], the 5-minute preliminary ex-post prices |
| **P-4A** | `csv/rtlbmp/<yyyymm>01rtlbmp_zone_csv.zip` | Time-Weighted/Integrated Real-Time LBMP [Zonal], hourly |

and publishes, in prose, that the second is built from the first and exactly how:

> "Zonal, Generator and Transmission Node Time Weighted/Integrated LBMP
> information will be produced by the MIS, **using the 5-minute real-time
> prices**, also from the MIS. The time weighted/integrated LBMPs will be posted
> on an hourly basis within 10 minutes after top-of-hour."
> — **Manual 12, *Transmission and Dispatch Operations Manual*, §"Real-Time
> Market Information" (p. 136)**

> "Each RTD interval [value] is multiplied by **the length of the RTD interval
> (in seconds) and then divided by 3,600 seconds per hour**, the results of which
> are summed over the hour to arrive at the hourly integrated real-time [value]."
> — **Manual 14, *Accounting and Billing Manual*, §4** (`acctbillmnl.pdf`)

> "**Preliminary ex-post** LBMPs for NYISO Zones … made available **every
> 5-minutes after real time**. … The following Time Weighted/Integrated LBMP
> information will be produced by a separate pricing program, **using the
> preliminary 5-minute ex-post prices**."
> — **A536, *Real-Time Scheduling Market Information Posting* (COO), §3.2.2,
> pp. 5–6**

So **P-4A *is* NYISO's own published answer to "which 5-minute stamps belong to
which hour"**, and it is stamped hour-**beginning** `00:00 … 23:00` (24 rows/day),
consistent with the hourly convention the MPUG uses throughout ("hour beginning
00:00 and ending with hour beginning 23:00", §8.2). Binning P-24A under each
candidate convention and comparing to P-4A therefore *decides* the question
rather than merely scoring it.

choice left is the hour assignment. 11 internal zones; 9 months spanning
2022–2025 including both DST-transition months. Published P-4A values carry two
decimals, so **|Δ| ≤ 0.005 is exact agreement**.

## A.4 Three independent corroborations

1. **The file's own span.** `20240601realtime_zone.csv` runs `06/01/2024
   00:05:00` → `06/02/2024 00:00:00`, exactly 288 stamps. A day's 288 five-minute
   intervals labelled by their **end** (`00:05 … 24:00`). Under beginning-labels
   a daily file would run `00:00 … 23:55`.
2. **Ex-post posting is incompatible with beginning-labels.** A536 §3.2.2 makes
   P-24A *preliminary ex-post* prices published "every 5-minutes **after** real
   time". A price stamped `22:05` cannot be the ex-post price of an interval that
   begins at 22:05.
3. **Live check, 2026-08-14 22:06:59 EDT:** the newest row of
   `mis.nyiso.com/public/realtime/realtime_zone_lbmp.csv` is stamped
   `08/14/2026 22:05:00` — the interval that *ended* at 22:05, posted ~2 min later.
