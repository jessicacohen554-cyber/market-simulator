## 2. What landed

### `src/market_sim/data/neighbor_price.py`

`_neighbor_lmp_clean` previously indexed `interval_start_local` — the
DST-*prevailing* wall clock — and took a simple mean over every node in the
partition. It now reproduces **both** halves of the realized product's reduction
(`scripts/data/derive_actual_lmp.py`):

1. **Clock.** `_std_hour_of_year(interval_start_utc, year, std_tz)` — a mirror of
   `derive_actual_lmp._std_hour_index`: convert the authoritative UTC column to
   the ISO's **fixed standard offset**, map (month, day, hour) onto the non-leap
   8760 calendar, drop the standard-clock Feb 29 and any row whose standard-clock
   year is not `year`. This is the chronological clock `eia_loader._eia_hourly_frame`
   sorts by and every model series rides.
2. **Hub.** A per-ISO table, `_HUB_SPECS`, carrying each ISO's standard-time zone
   and its hub's `node -> weight` map, cited to the producer constant it mirrors:

   | ISO | std offset | hub definition | producer constant |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | PJM | `Etc/GMT+5` | every node (the raw export is hub-only: the 12 trading hubs) | `_pjm` / `_hub_mean_hourly` |
   | CAISO | `Etc/GMT+8` | TH_NP15 0.3969 / TH_ZP26 0.0646 / TH_SP15 0.5385, **load-weighted** | `CAISO_HUB_WEIGHTS` |
   | NYISO | `Etc/GMT+5` | the 11 **internal** zones, equal weight (H Q / NPX / O H / PJM excluded) | `NYISO_INTERNAL` |
   | NEISO | `Etc/GMT+5` | the `.H.INTERNAL_HUB` sheet **alone** ("ISO NE CA") | `NEISO_HUB_SHEET` |

   The reduction is one vectorized weighted mean per slot — `Σ(price × weight) /
   Σweight` over `np.bincount` — which degenerates to the simple mean for the
   equal-weight hubs, so all four ISOs take one code path.

   **The table is FAIL-CLOSED.** An ISO absent from it returns `None` rather than
   guessing a clock or a hub. Only the four ISOs `curate_lmp.py` curates have a
   clean `lmp` partition, so nothing is lost today; adding a fifth means
   declaring its conventions first.

The prevailing-clock `_hour_of_year` is **deleted, not left callable** (rule 26
`[R-DELETE]`: a helper that indexes the wrong clock is a re-armable wrong
answer). It had exactly one caller.

Three stale docstring/comment claims in the same file were corrected alongside:
the realized product is on the *chronological* calendar (not "local"), the
module's calendar constants are standard-clock, and the module block comment now
states what "identical" costs and why, with the 2026-07-15 genealogy so the
producer/consumer split cannot silently re-open.

### `tests/curation/test_consume_lmp.py`

`_TOL` is **unchanged at 1e-2**. What changed is the false claim around it. The
line-35 comment — *"float32 storage in the realized product is the only source of
disagreement"* — was true when written (2026-06-19) and stopped being true on
2026-07-15, by which point float32 accounted for 0.0000033 % of the gap. It now
records the measured 1.4e-05 agreement, states that the tolerance is sized to
**catch** a clock/hub mismatch rather than absorb one, and says explicitly not to
widen it (F6 option C, rejected). The module docstring gains the same framing:
this test is the only instrument in the repo that can see either convention
drifting.

Read it by ISO:

* **PJM — 16 of 16 bit-exact.** Every partition goes to **0** hours above
  tolerance, max 4e-06 to 9.8e-05, i.e. float32 storage in the realized product
  and nothing else. Clock-only was sufficient, as the finding said: PJM's
  partition already holds exactly the 12 hubs the producer averages.
* **CAISO — 7 of 8 bit-exact** (≤2.7e-05, 0 hours). **2023 DAM does not close:
  48 hours, max 37.24.** That is the raw product's own OASIS-retention gap — the
  finding attributes it to the ~120 hours the realized 2023 DAM series carries as
  interpolated NaN, which the clean partition has real values for. Not a
  reduction defect, and not something this fix could reach.
* **NEISO — down to 2–45 hours a year**, mean $0.0001–$0.058, from ~8,500. The
  survivors are DST-transition-day hours where `curate_lmp.parse_neiso_file`'s

  `derive_actual_lmp._neiso_sheet_series`'s positional intra-day offset. 2024 and
  2025 land at 2 hours; 2018–2023 at 44–45.

**Provenance of the numbers.** `before` and `after` are two runs of the committed

lane**, which this fix's output matches on **all 44 partitions**. That
agreement is the cross-check that the shipped code implements the recipe the
finding measured, rather than a recipe that merely scores well.

## 4. The NYISO residual — OPEN, at full magnitude

**NYISO does not close, and this lane did not try to close it.** All four
partitions still sit at **8,507–8,723 hours** above tolerance after both repairs
— from 8,739–8,753 before. Per year:

| year | before max | after max | before n>tol | after n>tol | after mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 802.79 | 257.21 | 8,739 | 8,570 | 1.98 |
| 2023 | 1,120.54 | 1,121.24 | 8,748 | 8,660 | 8.95 |
| 2024 | 969.59 | 646.56 | 8,742 | 8,507 | 9.86 |
| 2025 | 2,038.68 | 2,038.44 | 8,753 | 8,723 | 30.99 |

The maxima move where dropping the four external proxy buses happens to remove
the worst outlier (2022, 2024) and not at all where it does not (2023, 2025 —
2023's max even rises fractionally). **The hour count barely moves in any year,
which is the tell:** a clock or hub repair fixes hours wholesale, and this
doesn't. The finding measured a **third, independent** defect there, and neither
leg of this fix touches it.

The two products adopt **opposite conventions** for NYISO's RTD "Time Stamp":
`derive_actual_lmp._nyiso_wide` bins it as interval-**beginning**,
`curate_lmp.parse_nyiso_zip` as interval-**ending** (`(ts_end − 1s).floor("h")`).
Measured on June 2024 against the published 5-minute zip, each convention matches
its own product exactly and the other not at all (finding, scope sweep). One
boundary sample in twelve swaps per hour, so the mean effect is small (~$0.62 for
the month) but the max is large on a spiky RT series.

**Which convention matches NYISO's published definition was NOT adjudicated** —
it needs the NYISO data dictionary, and guessing would put a wrong answer into a
committed product. The residual is reported here at full magnitude and is **not**
absorbed by a widened tolerance, a per-ISO carve-out, or a suppressed test: the
parity test pins PJM, which is now clean, and NYISO's number stands in the table
above as the open item.

## 6. Keeper risk: none, affirmatively

Unchanged from the finding's R4, and re-verified here:

* the **raw** backend is byte-untouched and is what every production path reads;
* the clean backend is **unreachable at defaults** — `MARKET_SIM_USE_CLEAN` is set
  nowhere in any run path, defaults OFF, and `data/clean` is gitignored;
* the one production consumer of `neighbor_lmp_hourly`,
  `inject_nyiso_import_hub_prices`, therefore reads the raw series exactly as
  before. The NYISO keeper `2026-08-08-nyiso-132-cf-arm` carries
  `nyiso_import_hub_prices: true` and **does not move**.

**What this closes is forward exposure, not a live error.**
`docs/iso-model-unification-plan.md` §3 plans to "remove the
`MARKET_SIM_USE_CLEAN` env var gating — clean is now mandatory". Before this fix,
the day that landed a one-hour-shifted neighbour price would have flowed straight
into the NYISO keeper's import stack. After it, PJM — the neighbour that seam
actually prices against — is bit-exact. **NYISO's own partition is still off by
the §4 convention, so that migration should not be treated as unblocked for
NYISO until §4 is adjudicated.**

   defect survived 28 days. Needs an owner call on cost, since making CI build
   `data/clean` is billed private-repo runner time.
