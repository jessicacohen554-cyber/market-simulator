## 3. What the disagreement is — the structure of ψ₁ vs ψ₂

**On the corridor.** Of the 12 ψ₁-identified N→S constituents, 4 are unresolvable (Franklin, Nashua, First
Creek–Roanridge, Mullergren–Ellsworth — 5,617 of the set's 9,254 hours, Franklin alone 44 %), and of the

(ψ₂ 0.099, ψ₁ 0.169) and Marshall–Knob 115 (ψ₂ 0.008, ψ₁ 0.365). The others — a 115 kV line at Lawrence,
the two Sibley and the Hawthorn 345/161 transformers, the Edwardsville 115/161 transformer, Seward–St
John — carry |ψ₂| ≤ 0.026 and mostly ≤ 0.005: in a DC network a 115/161 kV under-lay element in parallel
with the 345 kV backbone takes a few tenths of a percent of a bubble transfer, as PRECOMMIT §6.1
expected. ψ₁ gave those elements 0.035–0.365. The interpretation this lane can support: a regression of
one spread on 133 correlated shadow-price regressors loads onto a small element's coefficient whatever
spread coincides with its binding, and ψ₁'s LOYO folds (Marshall–Knob 0.088–0.850; Sibley 0.000–0.088;
Franklin 0.009–0.066) are the visible form of that. **The pooled 3,400 rests on Franklin (the median),
which ψ₂ cannot see at all, and on a set of small-element sensitivities ψ₂ does not reproduce.**

Where the two identifications look at the same 345 kV object they agree in sign and order: Cooper–St Joe
(+0.169 / +0.099), Waverly–LaCygne (−0.054 / −0.056), LaCygne–Stilwell (−0.070 / −0.109), Viola 345/138
