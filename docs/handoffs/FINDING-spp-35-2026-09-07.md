## 3. S-2 — `.iso-badge` contrast (commit 2)

### 3.1 The measurement, before and after

Before = `#fff` on the ISO accent. After = the accent at 12 % over white with `--text-primary`
(`#000`) text — shared.css's own `.badge--iso-*` construction. **Both columns are measured in

computed on paper; the before column reproduces FINDING-spp-34 §7 S-2 to the hundredth.

**7 / 7 ≥ 4.5:1** (WCAG AA normal text). Five were failing; NYISO was failing too (4.35 < 4.50) —
only NEISO passed before.

**Painted pixels, not just computed styles.** Each of the seven badges on `config-reference.html`
was screenshotted and its background pixel decoded from the PNG: CAISO `(254,243,225)`, ERCOT
`(228,248,235)`, MISO `(254,238,227)`, NEISO `(243,229,245)`, NYISO `(252,228,236)`, PJM
`(226,244,252)`, SPP `(226,246,244)` — the arithmetic tint to within ±1/255. Zero page errors.

### 3.2 What changed, and why it had to be done this way

The seven accents are **untouched** — no new hue is introduced anywhere. The tint is produced by an
opaque `linear-gradient(rgba(255,255,255,0.88), rgba(255,255,255,0.88))` layer, which over any
`background-color` computes exactly `rgba(accent, 0.12)` over white.

That indirection is forced by ownership: the accent reaches the badge as **inline style** on two of
the four consumer pages — `js/iso-configs-table.js:180` (`style="background: ${color}"`) and
`js/backcast-runs.js:898` (`badge.style.background = c`, plus an inline **white dot** at :899) —
while `forecast-runs.html:507`, `forecast-status.html:82` and `forecast-bands.html:107` pass it as
`--c`. Neither JS file is this lane's, and an inline shorthand beats any non-`!important` author
rule, so `background-image: … !important` is what lets a stylesheet re-tint a value it cannot read.
Verified in the browser: `backgroundColor` stays the inline accent, `backgroundImage` is the
gradient.

`.iso-badge .dot` becomes `currentColor !important`. The inline white dot would be invisible on the
tint, and the accent itself only reaches **1.96–3.61:1** against its own 12 % tint (measured; below
the 3:1 of WCAG 1.4.11, even though a dot beside the ISO's own name is decorative). Both code paths
were then simulated verbatim in-page and render **identically** — same tint, same `rgb(0,0,0)` text,
same dot, all seven ISOs, on both paths.
