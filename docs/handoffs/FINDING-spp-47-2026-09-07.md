## 1. What was changed, and why it is not a re-derivation

`_incomplete_renewable_fuels` decides which fuels the incomplete-vintage guard swaps from EIA-923 to EIA-930.
It iterated a **literal** `("wind", "solar")`, while the same file's `_EIA923_EXTRA_FUELS_BY_ISO` already
declares, per ISO, the additional fuels this builder treats as first-order and benchmarked
(`NYISO`/`NEISO`/`MISO` → `("hydro", "oil")`, `SPP` → `("hydro",)`). The two never met, so SPP 2025 hydro —
ratio **0.0026**, by far the worst of SPP's three benchmarked classes, an order of magnitude worse than the
wind (0.7941) and solar (0.6853) the same call swapped in the same year — was scored on the preliminary
release that its own file's logic rejects for wind.

**Rule 23 `[R-FROZEN-DERIVE]` is satisfied because nothing was re-derived.** The 0.80 threshold, the EIA-930
authority and the per-fuel evaluation are untouched; no measured-behaviour parameter moved; no constant was
added (rules 5 `[R-NO-MAGIC]` / 21 `[R-DOF]`). What changed is the loop's *candidate set*, composed from a
table that already existed. **Rule 25 `[R-ISO-SCOPE]`**: an ISO that declares no extras evaluates exactly the
former pair, so ERCOT/PJM/CAISO are unreachable — by construction, by test, and by the §3 diff.

Files: `scripts/data/build_calibration_reference.py` (1 executable line + docstrings/comments),
`data/raw/_validation-source/calibration_reference.json` (regenerated, §3),
`tests/unit/data/test_calibration_reference_extra_fuel_guard.py` (new, §5).
