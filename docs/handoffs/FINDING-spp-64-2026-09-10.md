series into the model's **own** observed window — `[−26.00, +73.77]` for 2025 — and re-score the same
model months against that tail-corrected actual, on a common weight (the model's own system demand,
stated because the load-weighting is this lane's, not the scorer's):

| year | model window | C3b-style NRMSE vs RAW actual | vs actual CLIPPED to the model's own window |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2024 | [−26.00, 421.22] | 0.1724 | 0.1771 |
| 2025 | [−26.00, 73.77] | 0.1669 | **0.2562** |

**The summer deficit VANISHES under the correction.** 2025 Jul/Aug/Sep go from **−4.00 / −3.50 /
−7.43** against the raw actual to **−0.81 / −1.53 / +0.40** against the clipped one. What remains is
an almost uniformly **positive** body error of ~+5 to +10 $/MWh, worst in the high-wind months
(Jan +9.88, Mar +9.82, Apr +8.75, Dec +8.40).

So the keeper's price shape decomposes into **two structural errors of opposite sign**:

1. **The model's price body is systematically TOO HIGH** — because it has no upper tail to carry the
   annual mean, the ordinary hours must carry it instead. Clipping the actual to the model's window
   moves the actual's 2025 annual mean **27.10 → 23.47**, so the missing tail is worth **+3.64
   $/MWh** annually (2023: **+2.71**).
2. **The phantom wind pushes the body back DOWN**, and it does so in the shoulder months where the
   wind is (§3).

They cancel to a PASS. **Note the arithmetic**: keeper 2025 against the tail-corrected actual is
**0.2562** — and SPP-63 reports its ceiling arm at **0.253** against the raw actual. The two errors
are of such nearly equal magnitude that removing one lands the model almost exactly where the other
already put it. *That* is what "the shape is right for the wrong reason" means, quantified. **2024
is the control on this reading**: its window is contaminated to $421 by two LP-infeasibility hours
(§4), so its clip is nearly a no-op (0.1724 → 0.1771) — the correction only bites where there is a
real tail to restore.

## 3. What sets the price in the negative hours — and what replaces it when the wind headroom goes

**The negative price-setter is wind, at a single flat offer.** The model's minimum price is **exactly
−26.000** in every year and in both zones. SPP wind's dispatch offer is `-config.ira_ptc_wind` =
**−$26.0/MWh** (`policy/ira.py::compute_dispatch_credits`, `ira_ptc_wind = 26.0` in the keeper's own
`run_config.json`), applied flat to the entire fleet. So the model's lower tail is a **clamp, not a
distribution**: whenever wind is on the margin the price is −26.00, and it is never anything else.
The measured floor is −37.25 / −36.03 / −35.56.

**The negative hours sit exactly where the wind is.** Model negative hours by month, against wind's
share of load:

| 2025 | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| model neg hours | 0 | 3 | **34** | **47** | 32 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 26 | 4 | 21 |
| wind / load | 0.349 | 0.411 | **0.615** | **0.625** | 0.438 | 0.333 | 0.280 | 0.265 | 0.301 | 0.440 | 0.474 | 0.443 |
| C3b error | +8.00 | +3.05 | **+6.78** | **+5.76** | −1.29 | −3.07 | −4.00 | −3.50 | −7.44 | +0.07 | +2.89 | +2.83 |

Mar and Apr carry the negative regime **and** two of the three largest positive C3b errors. Jul–Sep
have **zero** negative hours and carry every large negative error. The phantom wind therefore
suppresses precisely the months the model over-prices and cannot touch the months it under-prices.

**And nothing replaces it, for a structural reason.** `spp_curtailment_ceiling` is applied as a
**multiplier on the CF upper bound** (`data/curtailment_share.py` module docstring: *"applied as a
multiplier on the CF upper bound"*; the SPP leg is `spp_curtail_multipliers`, consumed through
`data/renewables.py`'s `_spp_ceiling` predicate). A unit held **at** a reduced bound is never
marginal, so bounded-off wind cannot set a price at all.

> **This is the mechanism of the G-5 failure, and it is structural rather than a matter of level.**
> In the real market the 11.8 TWh of curtailment **IS** the negative-price regime — SPP curtails
> *because* a constraint binds, and the same binding constraint drives the local LMP negative. The
> ceiling reproduces the **quantity** half of that object and discards the **price** half. Applied to
> a stack that is already missing the price half, it must take the lower tail from bad (167 hours) to
> absent (0), while leaving the upper tail untouched. **No value of `spp_curtail_depth_wind` changes
> this** — the defect is in the channel, not the coefficient. The handoff's hard constraint against
> re-cutting the depth is therefore not merely a governance rule here; re-cutting it would be
> *ineffective*, and this lane did not do it.
