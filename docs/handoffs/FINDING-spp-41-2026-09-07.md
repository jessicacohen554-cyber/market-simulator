### 2.2 The test, restated

An hour is repaired iff it is finite and exceeds **2.5×** BOTH the series' annual median AND its 99.9th
percentile (≈ the 9th-largest of 8,760 hours). Since p99.9 ≥ median always, the peak limb implies the median
limb: the test is the demand screen's, strengthened, never loosened, and what it can flag is exactly an hour
above 2.5× the series' own ninth-largest hour. A series whose p99.9 is not positive (an all-charging storage
net series) has no operating scale and passes through. Flagged hours become NaN in a COPY of the frame (the
raw extract is `lru_cache`d in `frames.py` and is never written back), and each reader's existing
`interpolate().bfill().ffill()` bridges them.

### 2.3 Where the live seam differs from the SPP-31 projection, and why the seam is right

SPP-31's helper ran on the benchmark dict — i.e. **after** each series had been gap-filled. The seam runs on
the **raw column before** any gap-fill. The order is load-bearing in exactly one situation, a slip hour
abutting a NaN gap, and NYIS `NG: OTH` has two of them:

| case | raw row(s) | SPP-31 copy (post-fill) | seam (pre-fill) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NYIS 2024 | h6759 = 16,117 MW, then h6760–6768 **NaN** (9 h), h6769 = 2,986 | interpolated THROUGH the spike first (16,117 → 2,986 over 10 h), then flagged the five interpolated hours above the bar (h6759–6763) and re-interpolated them: **3.3486** | flags the one real artifact hour; the loader then bridges h6759–6768 from h6758 (3,156) to h6769 (2,986): **3.3197** |
| NYIS H1-2026 | h2962–2964 = 12,993 / 12,680 / 12,617, h2961 = 5,117, then NaN | the smear through three spike hours put the p99.9 of the 3,912-hour filled series at 10,644, above the artifact: **nothing flagged, 5.3153** | p99.9 over the 3,871 raw observations = 3,758; three hours flagged: **5.1552** |

The seam's construction is the demand screen's (it screens the observed series and lets the loader's
gap-fill do the bridging) and it is the one that cannot repair its own interpolation, so it is kept and
the projection is not matched. Stated as a limit, never tuned around: on a ~3,900-hour partial year the
p99.9 sits at the 4th–5th largest hour, so a run of four or more consecutive slip hours could lift the anchor
above itself. The h6759 row, incidentally, is a whole-row artifact (`Demand` = `Net generation` = `NG: OTH`
= 16,117 with `NG: WND` = 0): the `Demand` half is already `_screen_demand_dropouts`'s documented NYIS
reporting-gap window; this seam closes the `NG:` half.
