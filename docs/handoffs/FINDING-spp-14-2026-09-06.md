## 1. How the route was found — the charter's step (2), and why it was the one that mattered

Step (1) — the **gridstatus.io hosted API** — is reachable and useless here: `GET /v1/` returns
`200 {"name":"Grid Status API","version":"1.3.0"}` and `openapi.json` returns 168,774 bytes, but
**every data endpoint returns `401 {"detail":"Missing API Key."}`** (`/v1/datasets`,
`/v1/pricing_locations`, `/v1/datasets/spp_lmp_day_ahead_hourly/query`, with and without an empty
`api_key=`). There is **no key-free tier**. Its licence terms could not be quoted: `docs.gridstatus.io`
and `www.gridstatus.io` (`/terms`, `/pricing`) return Cloudflare `403 "Attention Required!"` to curl
under any User-Agent and to the WebFetch tool alike, and the OpenAPI document declares no
`securitySchemes` and carries no licence field. **They are recorded as UNOBTAINED rather than quoted
from memory.**

Step (2) — the **open-source `gridstatus` package** — was chartered as "read its source for the
endpoints it hits; if any is an HTTPS host other than `portal.spp.org`/`pubftp.spp.org`, probe it."
The sdist (v0.36.0, BSD-3-Clause, from `files.pythonhosted.org`) yields five hosts in
`gridstatus/spp.py`: `portal.spp.org` (14 references), `marketplace.spp.org` (3), `www.spp.org` (2),
`pricecontourmap.spp.org` (2), `opsportal.spp.org` (1). The two non-portal candidates were probed and
neither serves an archive (§2 rows 5–6).

**But the source said something the charter did not anticipate.** `gridstatus/spp.py` reads every SPP
product with a bare `pd.read_csv(url)` against
`FILE_BROWSER_DOWNLOAD_URL = "https://portal.spp.org/file-browser-api/download"`, carrying **no token,
no header and no cookie** on that path. A widely-used client fetching SPP anonymously is incompatible
with "the portal requires `X-SPP-UI-Token`". So the portal was re-probed, and the wall turned out to
be two path-shape artifacts — both of them correct server behaviour:

| Call | What was sent before | What SPP-14 sent | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| download | a folder path, or a path absent from that product's archive layout | the exact **file** path | `404` → **`200`, real CSV** |
| listing | `path=` (**empty**) — the SPA's own first call | `path=%2F` (root) or a real folder | `200 []` → **`200`, real JSON array** |

Measured, verbatim, 2026-09-06:

```
GET /file-browser-api/download/da-lmp-by-settlement-location?path=/2025/06/By_Day/DA-LMP-SL-202506010100.csv
  → HTTP 200  text/csv  3,037,984 B
    Interval,GMTIntervalEnd,Settlement Location,Pnode,LMP,MLC,MCC,MEC
GET /file-browser-api/?fsName=rtbm-lmp-by-location&path=%2F2025%2F01&type=folder
  → HTTP 200  [{"name":"By_Day",...},{"name":"By_Interval",...},{"name":"RePrice",...},...]
GET /file-browser-api/download/rtbm-lmp-by-location?path=/2024/2024.zip   (Range: bytes=0-0)
  → HTTP 206  Accept-Ranges: bytes   Content-Range: bytes 0-0/5151618687
```

`Range` being honoured is what makes an archived `<year>/<year>.zip` readable member-by-member — the
zip64 central-directory technique `scripts/data/build_spp_lmp_reference.py` **already implements**.
That script therefore needed no change and **was not edited**; it was run unmodified.

### 3.3 P7 — the 2024 ruling STANDS, and here is the part that qualifies it

**2024 is first on every measure the charter asked for**: highest mean \|N − S\| and highest p90, in
both markets. The ruling is confirmed on the hourly evidence that was previously missing.

**But two things the annual signed mean was hiding must be said, because the charter asked for the
number that could disagree:**

1. **The margin is much narrower than the signed measure implied.** On the signed annual mean 2024
   (~$9) beat 2025 (~$1) by roughly **9×**. On the hourly absolute mean it beats 2025 by **13 %**
   ($17.23 vs $15.18), and on the p90 by **14 %**. SPP-12's addendum called the 2024 choice "robust
   to the difference between the published annual mean and the hourly mean it stands in for" — on
   the measured hourly numbers that robustness is real but **thin**, not the three-to-nine-fold
   margin it was described as.
2. **The ordering below first place inverts.** Signed: 2024 ≫ 2023 > 2025. Absolute: **2024 > 2025 >
   2023**. 2025 is the *second* most N↔S-separated year, not the least.

The cause is visible in the last column and it is a structural finding, not a curiosity: in 2025 the
hubs separate in **both directions almost equally** — South is cheaper than North in 4,081 of 8,760
RT hours (46.6 %) — so the signed mean cancels to +$0.15 while the absolute spread stays at $15.18.
**2025's near-zero published spread is cancellation, not absence of congestion.** That is the same
sign reversal the MMU itself reported for 2025 (six months DA, eight RT), now quantified hourly. A
model tuned to reproduce a ~$1 annual 2025 spread by pricing the two hubs together would be matching
the average of a large two-sided distribution, which is not the same thing.

**Verdict: the ruling stands and nothing here overrides it.** The screen year is 2024 on both
measures. The qualification above is reported because it is true, not because it changes the choice.

## 4. The MMU — asked, answered, and moot

SPP's MMU does publish **quarterly** State of the Market reports alongside the annual ones SPP-12
transcribed (`spp mmu qsom winter/spring/summer/fall 2024`, at
`www.spp.org/documents/{71500,72043,72602,73090}/`), and the Fall 2024 edition tabulates hub prices

substitute for P7's hourly mean, and it is NOT a substitute for the p90** — a p90 is a statement
about the tail of an hourly distribution and no aggregate of monthly means recovers it. This is
recorded because it was asked; it is **moot in the event**, because §3.2 computes both from the
hourly series directly.

### 5.3 What it settles

**Card P1's ranking: SPP-57 (Oklahoma) ranks above SPP-54 (SPS), on BOTH legs, in ALL THREE years,
on BOTH membership bases.** Oklahoma binds ~1.6–3.0× as many hours (0.538/0.536/0.665 vs
0.177/0.336/0.403) **and** at ~3.6–4.6× the mean shadow price (261.55/263.58/311.80 vs
56.34/74.10/87.05). Both legs agree, which is the case the README said to watch for and did not get:
there is no trade-off to adjudicate here.

**The README's own promotion test for SPP-54 also passes**: *"promotes the SPS pocket to a registered
zone only if the SPS-tie share is at least the N-S share"* — 0.177 > 0.076, 0.336 > 0.048, 0.403 >
0.115, and trivially on the strict basis. So SPS clears its bar; Oklahoma simply clears a higher one.

**A structural finding that came free, and that the topology card should have.** The STRICT result is
not a defect of the strict basis — it is a fact about SPP. **No registered SPP flowgate, permanent or
temporary, has one endpoint in the North hub's areas and the other in the South hub's.** Nebraska and
Oklahoma are not flowgate-adjacent: the corridor runs *through* Kansas and Missouri, so N↔S transfer
is carried by an **interface**, not by a flowgate. That is consistent with SPP's own ITP Constraint
Assessment treating *"Interfaces and Nomograms"* as a separate category (FINDING-spp-13 §4), and it
is why the broad basis picks up only 11–21 elements at a very high mean shadow price ($199–416/MWh):
the N↔S corridor **binds rarely and expensively**, exactly the object the README warned would be
hidden by a hours-only ranking.

## 6. Row 8 schema — a correction that changes what SPP-53 can measure

concluded that column was SPP-53's measured N↔S limit input.

| Date | Columns |
|---|---|
| 2023-06-01, 2024-06-01 (via yearly rollups), 2025-06-01, 2025-12-01, **2026-01-28**, 2026-03-23, **2026-03-24** | **10** |
| **2026-04-01**, 04-08, 04-15, 05-01, 06-01, 07-01, 08-01, 09-01 | **14** |

The break is the same **2026-03-24** format change the `gridstatus` client independently records for
SPP hourly load (`HOURLY_LOAD_WIDE_FORMAT_END_DATE`). Note that the served 2026-01-28 file is 10
columns even though SPP-13's sample of that same filename was 14 — the sample zip carries a
newer-format file under an older name.

**Consequences, stated at the gate:**

- **2023–2025 have no effective-limit column at all.** The row-8 archive **cannot** supply SPP-53's
  measured N↔S limit for the years the model calibrates on. FINDING-spp-13 §3's *"the same pull serves
  rows 8 and 11"* does not hold for the calibration span.
- From **2026-04** forward it can.
- **SPP-53 stays on the rule-14 `[R-ACCURATE]` Tier-3 reconciled estimate** that ruling P11 set for
  SPP-20. Nothing about that is changed by this lane.
- The better measured substitute now in the tree is `Flowgates.csv`'s per-flowgate seasonal
  `Normal` / `Emergency` MW ratings plus `IROLLimit` — published, dated, and landed.
