row**, because `derive_coal_supply.py` classifies the plants the fleet's coal set names, and 6193 is not

**Why the bench population carries 3.178 TWh of 2023 coal there — the mechanism, from EIA-860's own
vintages:**

| EIA-860 vintage | unit 1 | unit 2 | unit 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2023** | `SUB` — Conventional Steam Coal | `SUB` | `SUB` |
| **2024** | **`NG` — Natural Gas Steam Turbine** | `SUB` | `SUB` |

Harrington was a coal station undergoing a coal-to-gas conversion: in the **2023** information vintage all
three units are subbituminous coal; unit 1's conversion first appears in the **2024** vintage. The
benchmark population is built from measured EIA-923/CAMPD — what was actually burned — so it books
Harrington's 2023 energy to **coal**. The binned fleet carries all three units as `gas_st` in **every**
year, which matches neither vintage (2023: 0 of 3 converted; 2024: 1 of 3).

**This is a fleet-population/vintage question exactly as SPP-36 routed it**, and it is now specific: the
fleet's fuel classification for 6193 does not track the EIA-860 vintage the run's information cutoff
