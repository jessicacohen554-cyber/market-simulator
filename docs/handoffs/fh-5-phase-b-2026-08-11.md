## 0. CAVEATS UP FRONT — read these before any number below

These are **properties of a base-2021 forward run, disclosed by the charter
before it was executed** (FH-5 charter item 4). Neither is a defect to fix in
this lane; both bound what the Phase-B numbers can mean.

### 0.1 ERCOT 2021 does not contain Winter Storm Uri

The forecast stack's outage default is `outage_source="statistical"` — an
age-parameterized availability draw with **no calendar-year dependency** (plan
§4 row 4). It cannot reproduce February 2021's correlated multi-day thermal
failure. ERCOT's 2021 seed year is therefore **low-fidelity by construction**:
the seed dispatch, its price signal, and the margin state the 2023 evolution
step inherits are all drawn from a counterfactual 2021 in which Uri did not
happen. This is disclosed, not corrected — a tuned Uri overlay would be a
measured *outcome* fed back in (rule 13 `[R-MEASURED]`).

Scope of the damage: 2021 is a **seed year and is never scored**, so no Uri
artifact reaches a reported metric directly. It reaches them only through the
evolution chain (2021 margins → capacity screens → the 2023 fleet). ERCOT's
Phase-B reads are read with that channel in mind, and the ERCOT horizon row is
the one row in this deliverable carrying a structural confound the other five
do not.

### 0.2 The base-2021 hydro climatology is a single water year

`full_forward_climatology_years(2021)` returns **`(2021,)`** — verified at this
head. The FH-1 as-of trim admits only `HYDRO_CLIMATOLOGY_YEARS` members at or
before the base year and never quarantined 2022, and the constant starts at
2021, so a 2021-base run's "normal water year" climatology is built from **one
observed year — 2021, a dry year in the West**. (Phase A's base-2023 window is
`(2021, 2023)`, two years; still thin, less so.)

This was disclosed as a finding by FH-1 and **is never widened by hand**: the
window regenerates from the as-of date, it is not a tuning knob (rule 13). It
bears hardest on the hydro-heavy ISOs — CAISO first, then NYISO and MISO — and
is a standing reason a Phase-B hydro-driven miss is not evidence about the
model's hydro mechanism.

### 0.3 Two further degeneracies inherited from the charter's disclose list

- **Coal and oil carry zero historic signal.** Both trajectory families start
  2025, so every year ≤2025 resolves flat (plan §4 row 8, FH-3 §6). Phase A's
  ERCOT leg found this drives a **coal-zero dispatch** — 0.0 TWh of coal
  against ~60 TWh actual in every year. That is a standing property of the
  forward stack at any base year and will reappear in Phase B; it is the
  coal-economics/fuel-trajectory lanes' object, not this lane's.
- **Gas basis is year-invariant** (plan §4 row 9): `GAS_BASIS_DIFFERENTIAL` was
  derived from 2023–2025 receipts, so applying it at a 2021 base is an as-of
  violation in spirit. It rides identically in both arms, so it nets out of the
  K-vs-R spread but not out of either arm's absolute level.

### 1.6 The horizon-degradation read, pre-registered

**The design.** Horizon `h` = scored year − base year (evolution steps from
base; the bridged 2022 counts as a step because the fleet evolves through it).
The two phases score the **same three years against the same actuals, the same
bench, and the same keeper comparator** — only `h` differs:

| Scored year | Phase A horizon | Phase B horizon |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | h = 0 *(base year itself)* | **h = 2** |
| 2024 | h = 1 | **h = 3** |
| 2025 | h = 2 | **h = 4** |

This is a cleaner instrument than the charter block's shorthand ("1-year-ahead
(Phase A 2023) vs 2-year (2024) vs 4-year (2025 from a 2021 base)"), and it is
what the data supports: **year effects are held fixed** and only horizon moves.

**The built-in control.** 2025 in Phase A and 2023 in Phase B are both **h = 2**
but different calendar years. Their spread separates *horizon* effect from
*year* effect — without it, a Phase-B degradation could be nothing but 2023
being a harder year than 2025. This control is pre-registered as part of the
read, not selected after seeing it.

**Metrics.** `scripts/score_crossover.py` run verbatim per arm, no new metric
and no re-derivation: `dispatch_skill.metrics` = **fuelmix, price_mean,
price_shape** (+ `co2` reported) per scored year, each carrying `forecast_err`,
`keeper_backcast_err`, and `input_gap = |forecast_err| / |keeper_backcast_err|`.
Keeper comparators are the six ids in §1.1.

**Pre-registered directions (to test, never targets).**

1. **Arm R errors grow with h, monotonically or near it**, on price_mean and
   fuelmix. This is the null the phase exists to test. Magnitude: none
   pre-registered.
2. **Coal-zero (§0.3) does not degrade with horizon** — it is a price-level
   defect, not an accumulation, so the fuelmix gap should be roughly h-flat
   wherever coal dominates it. If fuelmix degrades *beyond* the coal-zero floor,
   that increment is the real horizon signal.
3. **Arm K's horizon read is confounded by the AEO-edition luck of §1.3** and is
   reported, but the headline horizon claim rests on Arm R.
4. **I6 pressure rises with horizon** — more evolution steps, more chances for a
   retirement wave to mature. A Phase-B I6 FAIL where Phase A passed is a
   finding about screen stability under horizon, reported as such.

**Reading rule (rule 1 / rule 13).** A large or growing gap is **the
measurement, not a failure to fix**. Nothing is tuned in response. Anything
closable only by an unidentified value is an open blocker, written up.

### 4.3 Reading it honestly

1. **The pre-registered null — "Arm R errors grow with h, monotonically or near
   it" — is NOT supported.** Of 33 Arm-R (ISO × metric × year) cells with both
   phases present, the error moves in both directions and the moves are small
   relative to between-year variation. The clean statement the data supports is
   the §4.1 headline, and it is a *more useful* result than a decay curve would
   have been: it says a 4-year-ahead forecast of an ordinary year is worth about
   as much as a 2-year-ahead forecast of that same year, while *either* is worth
   much less in a year the forward stack handles badly.
2. **Pre-registered direction 2 held exactly.** Coal-zero is a floor, not an
   accumulation: ERCOT's 2025 fuelmix is **62.215 TWh in both phases and both
   arms, to three decimals** — identical, because only the coal classes are
   scoreable in 2025 and every arm dispatches 0.0 TWh of coal. Where fuelmix
   *does* move with horizon (ERCOT 2023/2024, +30 TWh) the increment sits on top
   of that floor and is the real horizon signal.
3. **Two ISOs get materially BETTER at longer horizon on fuelmix** — NYISO
   (27.3 → 13.0 in 2023) and NEISO (15.9 → 9.8). Both are the ISOs whose Phase-A
   legs identified a *forward demand/interchange construction* defect (the NEISO
   leg's §4.1: import links pinned at TTC ~8,760 h, CC_REGULAR −13.6 TWh under
   actual). A base-2021 fleet evolving four steps lands on a different — here
   less wrong — generation mix than a base-2023 fleet evolving zero. This is
   **not** evidence that longer horizons forecast better; it is evidence that the
   Phase-A mix error was fleet-composition-driven rather than horizon-driven, and
   it belongs to those lanes, untouched here (rule 1).
4. **ERCOT is the one ISO with a real, visible horizon cost on price**, and it is
   concentrated in 2024 (0.149 → 0.362, a 2.4× degradation). ERCOT is also the
   only ISO carrying the §0.1 Uri confound in its evolution chain, so its horizon
   row must not be read as a clean measurement — see §4.4.
5. **The h=2 control repeatedly separates effects that would otherwise be
   conflated.** CAISO is the sharpest case: 2025@A = 0.556 and 2023@B = 0.156 at
   the *same* horizon — a 3.6× spread that has nothing to do with how far ahead
   the model was forecasting, and everything to do with 2025 being a year the
   CAISO forward stack handles badly (consistent with §0.2 — a single dry water
   year as the whole hydro climatology).

### 4.4 The ERCOT caveat, quantified

§0.1 said ERCOT's 2021 seed is low-fidelity because statistical outages cannot
reproduce Uri. The runs make its shape concrete, and it is **asymmetric in a way
worth stating plainly**: Arm R runs **per-solve-year weather**, so the 2021 seed
carries February 2021's *actual demand* profile while the outage draw is purely
statistical — Uri's load event without Uri's supply failure. Both ERCOT arms
report an **unserved-energy FAIL in 2021 (slack 0.06 % of load)** and Arm K adds
a 2021 price-sanity WARN (load-weighted $60.1 against a CC marginal cost of
$18.4), which is that incoherent seed showing up in the invariants.

Arm K compounds it: it pins weather to the **base year for every solve year**, so
**every ERCOT Arm-K year runs on 2021 weather**, Uri demand included. Phase A's
Arm K pinned to 2023, an unremarkable year. The two phases' Arm K legs therefore
differ in weather regime as well as horizon — another reason §4.1's headline
rests on Arm R.

## 5. Arm K — the driver-forecast error, and why it must not be read as horizon skill

§1.3 pre-registered the trap: **AEO2021's ex-ante gas error is far smaller than
AEO2023's** (+22.0 % / +34.7 % / −12.2 % on 2023/24/25, against +116 % / +98 % /
+7.6 %), so a Phase-B Arm K will tend to *look* better than a Phase-A Arm K for
reasons that have nothing to do with horizon. It did, and the mechanism is
measurable.

**Driver-forecast error = the Arm K − Arm R spread on `price_mean`** (both arms
identical but for the driver vintage, so the spread isolates it):

| ISO | 2023 (A) | 2023 (B) | shrink | 2024 (A) | 2024 (B) | 2025 (A) | 2025 (B) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PJM | +0.613 | +0.104 | **5.9×** | +0.235 | −0.007 | −0.018 | +0.038 |
| NEISO | +0.542 | +0.112 | **4.8×** | +0.325 | +0.109 | +0.015 | +0.008 |
| NYISO | +0.667 | +0.146 | **4.6×** | +0.213 | −0.132 | −0.016 | +0.060 |
| ERCOT | −0.359 | −0.035 | 10.2× | +0.036 | −0.144 | −0.044 | +0.151 |
| MISO | +0.149 | −0.112 | *(sign flips)* | −0.068 | −0.179 | −0.020 | −0.015 |

**The 2023 column is the clean test**, because that is where the two editions'
errors differ most: **+116 % vs +22 % is a 5.3× ratio, and the measured spread
shrinks 4.6×–5.9× in the three ISOs whose Arm R is well-behaved** (PJM, NEISO,
NYISO). The driver-forecast error scales with the driver error, as pre-registered
— the ex-ante fuel vintage is doing the work, not the horizon.

**Consequences, stated so they cannot be misquoted:**

- **Phase B's Arm K reading "better" than Phase A's is edition luck.** AEO2021
  happened to sit closer to what 2023–2025 gas actually did than AEO2023 did.
  Nothing here says a 4-year-ahead as-known forecast beats a 1-year-ahead one.
- **This is why §4.1's headline rests on Arm R**, whose only cross-phase
  difference is base year and horizon.
- ERCOT's 10.2× and MISO's sign flip are **not** clean measurements of the same
  quantity: ERCOT's Phase-A Arm R was deeply under (−65 %, the error-compensation
  artifact the FH-4 leg named), and MISO's Arm K lands *closer to actual than its
  own Arm R* in 2023 (0.051 vs 0.163) — a second compensation, not skill.

## 7. RECOMMENDATION — do NOT charter a pre-2021 base

The charter set the §3.2 refusal record as the starting position and asked for it
to be argued against only with evidence. **The evidence strengthens the refusal
rather than weakening it**, and it narrows to a single decisive fact.

**7.1 The instrument does not exist at base 2020.** The as-of hydro climatology
window is EMPTY at that base:

```
full_forward_climatology_years(2021) = (2021,)
full_forward_climatology_years(2020) = ()          <-- no water years at all
```

and `forecast_monthly_hydro(..., climatology_years=())` **returns `None`
silently** — it does not raise. A base-2020 T1-FF run would therefore carry *no
hydro climatology whatsoever*, and would not announce it. §0.2 already recorded
base-2021's single water year as a disclosed thinness finding; base-2020 is not
a thinner version of that, it is the absence of the input.

> **Filed as an open blocker (not fixed here, out of this lane's scope):** the
> empty-window path degrades silently. Whatever is decided about pre-2021 bases,
> `full_forward_climatology_years` returning `()` should fail closed like the
> FH-2 demand-growth resolver does, rather than handing the runner a `None`. The
> FH-1 as-of trim made the window as-of-correct; it did not make the empty case
> loud.

**7.2 It was never a six-ISO programme.** From §2, a base-2020 run needs jointly
a 2020 weather year, a vintage ≤2020, 2020 demand profiles and — because 2020 is
validation tier — the ISO's `complete` marker. Intersecting those leaves
**NEISO alone**. The other five are excluded by weather pool (NYISO, PJM) or by
marker (CAISO, ERCOT, MISO).

**7.3 The measurement would not pay for itself.** §4 is the substantive
argument: out to h=4 the horizon effect is **small relative to the year effect in
12 of 13 ISO×metric cells**. Extending one ISO to h=6 buys a further increment on
the axis this phase just showed is *not* the dominant one, and it spends a
validation-tier authorization to do it. If the goal is better forward skill, §4
says the productive lanes are the ones that make a *given year* easier — the
coal-zero fuel-trajectory gap, the NEISO/NYISO forward demand-interchange
construction, CAISO's 2025 — not a longer baseline.

**7.4 The honest counter-argument, and why it does not carry.** NEISO — the only
eligible ISO — is also the **one ISO in the whole table where the horizon effect
edged out the year effect** (price_mean 0.070 vs 0.051, §4.1). So the single
eligible ISO is, on this phase's own evidence, the most interesting one to
extend. That tension is real and is recorded rather than smoothed over. It does
not carry, for two reasons: (a) §7.1 forecloses it on inputs regardless of how
interesting it is, and (b) the NEISO cell is the closest in the table — a tie,
not a signal, and a 0.019 margin is not a basis for spending a holdout tier.

**7.5 What to do instead, if the horizon question is pressed.** Two cheaper
instruments, neither needing a new base year or a holdout spend:

1. **Re-cut the existing pair at h=1 vs h=3.** Phase A 2024 (h=1) and Phase B
   2024 (h=3) are already solved and registered; the same is true at h=0/h=2 and
   h=2/h=4. Nothing further need be run to study horizon at three separate
   offsets — this lane's artifacts already contain them.
2. **Fix the empty-window path (§7.1) first.** Any future pre-2021 charter is
   blocked on it, and it is a small, rule-13-clean change (fail closed, like the
   FH-2 resolver) rather than a data intake.

**Recommendation, stated plainly: keep the §3.2 refusal, extend it explicitly to
base 2020 on the §7.1 grounds, and do not charter a pre-2021 base.**
