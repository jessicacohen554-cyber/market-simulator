## 3. RTM CSV column layout — the deliverable

### 3.1 Header (verbatim, 28 columns)

```

**This is character-for-character identical to the DAM header** (checked programmatically;
record in `results/rtm-intake/caiso281/2025q3/header_parity.txt`). The layouts were NOT
assumed to match — they were compared, and they do. Only `MARKET_RUN_ID` distinguishes the
two files (`RTM` vs `DAM`, constant within a file).

```

Consecutive rows are **breakpoints of one piecewise bid curve**, not independent records:
same resource, same hour, ascending `SCH_BID_XAXISDATA` (MW) against
`SCH_BID_Y1AXISDATA` ($/MWh). The curve is reconstructed by grouping on
`(RESOURCEBID_SEQ, SCH_BID_TIMEINTERVALSTART, MARKETPRODUCTTYPE)`.

### 3.3 The two row shapes a parser must handle

Rows come in exactly two mutually-exclusive shapes, distinguished by which time-interval
column family is populated:

| Shape | Count (RTM, 2025-08-15) | `SCH_BID_*` | `TIMEINTERVAL*` | `SELFSCHEDMW` | Meaning |
|-------|------------------------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|---------|
| **Curve** | 178,582 | populated | empty | empty | one breakpoint of a piecewise bid curve |
| **Self-schedule** | 21,302 | empty | populated | populated | price-taker MW, no curve |
| Self-sched w/o MW | 152 | empty | populated | empty | degenerate; carry as null |

A parser keyed only on `SCH_BID_TIMEINTERVALSTART` silently drops the 21,454 self-schedule
rows — which is 10.7% of the file and the entire price-taker block.

Always-empty columns in RTM: `PRODUCTBID_DESC`, `PRODUCTBID_MRID`, `MARKETPRODUCT_DESC`
(all-empty in DAM too).

### 3.4 Time columns and granularity — **RTM is hourly**

| Column family | RTM | DAM |
|---|---|---|
| `STARTTIME` → `STOPTIME` | **60 min, all 200,036 rows** | 60–1440 min, 23 distinct durations |
| `SCH_BID_TIMEINTERVALSTART` → `..STOP` | **60 min, all 178,582 rows** | 60–1440 min, 23 distinct durations |
| `TIMEINTERVALSTART` → `..END` | **60 min, all 21,454 rows** | 60–1440 min, 22 distinct durations |
| Distinct start values per day | **24** | 24 |

**There is no sub-hourly dimension.** RTM does not expose 5-minute (RTD) or 15-minute
(FMM) bid intervals in `PUB_RTM_GRP`; bids are published per trade hour, exactly as DAM is.
GMT twins (`*_GMT`) are provided for every local timestamp — use those to avoid the DST
fold; `STARTDATE` is the trade date.

**The RTM parser is therefore STRICTLY SIMPLER than the DAM parser**: RTM never uses
multi-hour block bids, so no interval expansion is needed. The converse is the trap — a
parser written against RTM's fixed hour and then pointed at DAM will silently mis-handle
DAM's 1,440-minute block rows.

## 4. Volume — one representative day (2025-08-15)

| Measure | RTM | DAM | RTM ÷ DAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zip bytes | 1,794,556 | 489,465 | 3.67× |
| CSV bytes | 53,029,554 | 13,851,189 | 3.83× |
| **Rows** | **200,036** | **52,425** | **3.82×** |
| Distinct **`RESOURCEBID_SEQ`** | **2,074** | 1,997 | 1.04× |
| Distinct `SCHEDULINGCOORDINATOR_SEQ` | 322 | 308 | 1.05× |
| Hour-equivalent rows (Σ duration/60) | 200,036 | 305,579 | **0.65×** |

The masked resource-bid key is named **`RESOURCEBID_SEQ`** in RTM — the same column name as
DAM, no RTM-specific equivalent.

**The 3.8× row multiple is a representation difference, not more information.** DAM
compresses a flat multi-hour bid into ONE row spanning up to 1,440 minutes (9,720 such rows
on this date); RTM writes every hour separately. On an hour-expanded basis DAM actually
covers *more* resource-hours than RTM (305,579 vs 200,036). Sizing the remaining intake off
the raw row count would overstate it.

The genuine information gain is narrower and more specific:

| EN priced ladder, hour-expanded | RTM | DAM |
|---|---|---|
| Resources with a priced EN ladder | 1,344 | 1,370 |
| (resource, hour) cells covered | **28,851** (89.4% of possible) | 21,484 (65.3%) |
| Breakpoints per cell, mean | **4.82** | 3.68 |
| Breakpoints per cell, median / max | 3 / 46 | 2 / 36 |

RTM prices a similar resource count but covers **89.4%** of their hours against DAM's
65.3%, with deeper ladders. `SCH_BID_Y1AXISDATA` spans −150 to 1,000 $/MWh in both (the
CAISO bid floor and cap), so the offer surface is bounded identically.

## 5. RTM ↔ DAM join, and ID persistence

The charter's question is whether the DAM-derived offer surface is the right ladder to
price a real-time-benchmarked LP against. Answering it requires joining the two surfaces.
**The masked IDs share a namespace**, so the join is mechanically possible:

| Key, 2025-08-15 | RTM | DAM | Shared | RTM-only | DAM-only | Jaccard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RESOURCEBID_SEQ` | 2,074 | 1,997 | **1,536** | 538 | 461 | 0.606 |
| `SCHEDULINGCOORDINATOR_SEQ` | 322 | 308 | 276 | 46 | 32 | 0.780 |

1,536 resources bid into both markets on this date — 74.1% of RTM's and 76.9% of DAM's. The
non-overlap is real market behaviour (a resource bidding one market only), not an ID
mismatch.

RTM IDs also persist across dates the way the DAM docstring documents for DAM: 2025-07-03 vs
2025-08-15 share **1,907** of 2,074 / 2,082 seqs (84.8% Jaccard).

**Caveat, stated rather than buried:** this is a one-day measurement on both counts. The
persistence and overlap rates should be re-measured across the full quarter before any
derivation leans on them.
