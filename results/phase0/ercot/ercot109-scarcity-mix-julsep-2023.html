The cheap stack, taken together

Summing wind + coal + solar + hydro against actual, the model carries a surplus of cheap resource in 118 of 122 scarcity hours — median +871 MW, reaching +3,470 MW. It exceeds 1 GW in 48 hours and 1.5 GW in 21. Against the gas deficit the correlation is -0.77 — close to a one-for-one displacement, which is what a merit-order substitution looks like.

Reading it

Method and caveats

Scarcity is defined on the actuals, not the model. An hour qualifies when the load-weighted zonal settlement price clears $300/MWh — the same tail definition this lane uses throughout. The model’s own price plays no part in selecting the hours.
Load is an input, so the level matches by construction. The backcast is driven by measured demand; what is being compared here is which resources served that load, not how much was served.
Storage is the one leg this cannot test. EIA‑930 publishes no battery series for ERCOT in 2023, so there is no measured counterpart at any grain, and the model’s committed artifacts carry storage only as an annual total — 0.77 TWh of throughput for the year, 116 GWh in August (~156 MW monthly mean). That bounds the model’s battery contribution at these hours to a few hundred MW against a 1.7 GW gas gap, but it does not resolve it. Whether the model over-discharges batteries into scarcity hours is open and needs an hourly series on both sides.
The gas sub-classes are model-only. Measured gas is a single series at this grain; the CC / CT / steam split shown above is the model’s internal composition and has no measured counterpart here.
Hydro doubles off a tiny base. +176 MW on 174 MW actual is a real relative error but a rounding error against a 77 GW stack — it is in the table for completeness, not as a finding.
