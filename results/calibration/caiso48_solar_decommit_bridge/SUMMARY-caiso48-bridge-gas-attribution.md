## Key finding — the caiso-45 "bridge pads gas" theory is refuted

| year | P1 gas TWh | P2 gas TWh | bridge net | neg-price h (P1 → P2) |
|------|-----------|-----------|------------|------------------------|
| 2023 | 79.41 | 79.98 | **+0.58** | 0 → 25 |
| 2024 | 79.90 | 79.02 | **−0.88** | 148 → 148 |
| 2025 | 73.98 | 74.12 | **+0.14** | 0 → 32 |

- The bridge's net annual-gas effect is **≤ 1 TWh in every year**. The caiso-45
  "2025 +34.4% over EIA-923" guardrail trip was the **P1 baseline** measured
  against the raw **preliminary 2025 EIA-923 vintage** (55.2 TWh gas; the
  vintage totals 132.6 TWh vs EIA-930's 186.4 — ~71 % reporting). On the
  verdict machinery's repaired basis the 2025 gas overhang is **+7.9 %** (C2
  HARD FAIL, but improved from the caiso-42 keeper's +12.3 %), and it exists
  *before any floor is applied*.
- The decommit control is correspondingly quiet in-sample: P1 import dispatch
  plus the 3.5 GW export-sink headroom exceed the candidate floors in nearly
  every gap hour, so the surplus screen rarely binds, and the DA-horizon cap
  only removes multi-day bridges the plain restart inequality mostly rejected
  anyway. The control is real structure and stays available
  (`--caiso-ra-bridge-decommit`), but the 2025 gas overhang is a **P1
  merit-order property** (import tranche prices / CC offer curves / hub
  coupling), not bridge padding.
- 2024's 148 negative-price hours exist **already at P1** — they come from the
  bidir export sink + negative renewable offers + solar depth, not from bridged
  commitment. 2023/2025 negatives (25/32) appear only at P2, where the bridge
  floors create the marginal midday surplus.
