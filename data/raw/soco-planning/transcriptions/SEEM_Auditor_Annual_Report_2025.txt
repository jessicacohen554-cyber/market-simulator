I.  OVERVIEW 
This is the 2025 Annual Audit Report on the Southeast Energy Exchange Market (SEEM). This 
report covers activity in 2025, includes some historical data from prior years, and incorporates 
observations from 2026. SEEM is a regional energy market that centralizes the collection of bids 
and offers among multiple parties to create matches for bilateral trades every 15 minutes. SEEM 
began operating in November 2022 and currently has 24 members.1  
Trading Highlights: 
• Trading volumes increased in 2025 to 1,303,000 MWh from 1,055,000 MWh in 2024 and 
from 704,000 in 2023.  
• The monthly average volume of cleared trades was 109,000 MWh (compared to 88,000 
MWh in 2024).  
• The highest monthly trade volume in 2025 was 132,878 MWh in May, which was an all-
time high. 
• SEEM volumes indicate a distinct upward trend over time. 
• Total production cost savings since market opening exceed $30 million.  
According to the Auditing Process under the SEEM Market Rules (Section VI.D of the rules) our 
auditing duties fall into two general areas: 
(1)  analyzing and affirming SEEM inputs, constraints, and matching results; and  
(2)  responding to certain inquiries and reporting on the SEEM operation and outcomes.  
Our Annual Report focuses on the results of our auditing activity in 2025. We focus on market 
outcomes using the 2025 data as well as historical data prior to 2025 and data for part of 2026. 
While our report focuses on SEEM outcomes, we monitor SEEM continuously using data screens 
to verify the underlying modeling operated by Hartigen in accordance with specific market rules.  
Hence, we evaluate bids and offers, evaluate matches, and verify SEEM constraints based on 
specific requirements in the Auditing Process in the SEEM Market Rules. We report these results 
each month and have found the SEEM market operated in compliance with the SEEM rules.

We track the time series volumes of trades, offers, bids, and regional demand data (measured by 
Degree Days (DD))3 to gain insight into the performance of SEEM. We calculate statistics for the 
key data to identify key correlations.4  We found excess bids (bids less offers) are positively 
correlated with Degree Days, meaning higher regional load (using DD as the proxy) typically 
results in buyers turning to SEEM in higher volumes than sellers, likely due to sellers shoring up 
their own integrated operations under constrained conditions.  This is what we have observed 
anecdotally as well.   
Interestingly, cleared volume does not statistically correlate with DD, even as buyers tend to push 
into the market at these times. They appear to be chasing vanishing supply at these times. Indeed, 
this is consistent with the SEEM clearing price being statistically higher when DD increases

D. Network Usage 
The SEEM model uses transmission segments made up of paths between directly adjacent 
systems. These segments are used by themselves or combined with connecting segments to form 
larger paths for trading. There are 359 segments. Most segments have an Available Transmission 
Capability (“ATC”) value that determines how much power can be transferred over the segment; 
others have no ATC value and are thus available without limit. Each month we evaluate the ATC 
and trading statistics for all SEEM segments available to the model.5 
Our evaluation of segment usage has found that there are instances when the ATC is posted as 
zero or it is completely used by SEEM transactions.  In these cases, SEEM is constrained and 
cannot clear additional trades using that segment. However, this happens in typically less than 10 
percent of the time. Figure 6 shows the average monthly percentage of unavailable SEEM 
segments. Unavailability peaks in the shoulder seasons (spring and fall) when transmission 
maintenance is undertaken, which can result in reduced ATC values. While availability is high 
overall, we will monitor a recent trend showing increasing unavailability.

III.  CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
Our evaluation of SEEM operations for 2025 has shown that implementation and operation of the 
SEEM platform continues to be successful.  
Given the solid performance of the SEEM platform system, it holds promise to expand the system 
to include longer timeframes. The current SEEM market facilitates very short, near-term 
exchanges of economic energy. Additional benefits and efficiency could be achieved by 
transacting further ahead and for longer periods.  
The main difference between these timeframes and the current market is that holders of 
transmission reservations would have to release the transmission. The current SEEM market uses 
unscheduled available transmission capability after the hourly schedules are processed. Hence, the 
transmission is released and made available at no cost. This would not be possible for longer lead-
time transactions because the transmission reservations could potentially still have significant 
value in these timeframes. Therefore, implementing hourly real-time or day-ahead transactions 
would require: 
• Offers by the holders of transmission reservations to make them available at a submitted 
cost (does not exist in the current market); and 
• Bids by buyers and offers by sellers that are comparable to the current market.

Both of these recommended expansions would facilitate fuller utilization of the transmission 
system. However, an hourly day-ahead market would likely be the most beneficial because it 
could lead to changes in resource commitments that would lower the cost of serving load in the 
region.  
As an illustration of potential benefits, we compare activity in SEEM and the bilateral hourly 
market to assess relative volumes. We make this comparison on a path basis by comparing 
volume on a SEEM path with the corresponding volume of hourly bilateral trades. We have all 
SEEM trade volumes needed for this comparison. For bilateral hourly trade volumes, we use 
transmission reservation (TSR) data from the Open Access Same-Time Information System 
(OASIS). Specifically, we use hourly point-to-point transmission service request data as a proxy 
for bilateral trades. Point-to-point TSRs are defined only on paths between adjacent transmission 
systems, so TSR data for hourly bilateral trades is available only on one-segment paths. These 
paths connect adjacent systems, and we use them as a proxy for bilateral trades between the two 
systems. Participants in the hourly bilateral market can string together multiple transmission 
reservations to create longer paths across multiple systems. However, because transmission costs 
exceed $10/MWh, we conclude that this is a rare use of point-to-point reservations. Accordingly, 
we confine our comparison of SEEM volume and hourly bilateral volume to one-segment paths.  
We collected data for April 2025 to April 2026. The data show slightly lower volume in SEEM 
than in the hourly bilateral market, indicating that most short-horizon economy energy for the 
period was traded in the hourly market. However, the monthly data do not always show this 
pattern. Table 1 compares volume over this time period. It shows higher total and average volume 
for OASIS, although the difference from SEEM volume is not significant and does not occur in 
every month.

The data suggests that, at least for the one-segment paths that facilitate trade between adjacent 
systems, SEEM markets have slightly lower volumes than the hourly bilateral markets. Expanding 
SEEM to the hourly market could bring these one-segment transactions into a multilateral 
mechanism and improve market access for buyers and sellers. More importantly, however, is the 
potential to extend hourly trades to multi-segment paths. SEEM clears trades very frequently on 
multi-segment paths; typically about 40 percent of trades used them. These trades take advantage 
of low-cost transmission across the footprint and pay only transmission losses. This is a 
substantial advantage over the hourly market, where transmission charges are pancaked and 
participants face accumulating transmission charges that are much higher -- over $10/MWh per 
system compared to about $2/MWh for SEEM. A multilateral mechanism that allows multi-
segment paths to trade hourly with discounted transmission, as in SEEM, would further expand 
competitive offerings and increase production cost benefits for hourly activity.
