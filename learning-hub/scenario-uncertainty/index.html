The base case

One line, full of false confidence

Here is the model's central forecast for the wholesale price: a gentle decline from the mid-$40s today toward the low-$30s by 2050, as cheap renewables crowd out gas.

It is a clean line. It is also a fiction — it pretends we know the next twenty-five years. We do not. The line is only the P50: the outcome with as much chance above as below.

Dimension one

Gas prices — doubt from day one

Switch on the first source of uncertainty: gas prices. Gas still sets the price in most hours today, so this doubt bites immediately — the fan opens even in the near term.

The shaded band is now a P10–P90 range: a 1-in-10 chance of landing below it, 1-in-10 above. A single line has become a fan.

Dimension two

Carbon price — the slow-burn wildcard

Add carbon price. Today there is barely any carbon policy to be uncertain about — so near-term the fan hardly changes.

But stretch to the 2040s and the range of plausible carbon policy is enormous. Carbon uncertainty starts small and compounds steeply — the fan flares wide at the far end.

Dimension three

Demand growth — electrify everything?

Add demand growth. How fast do EVs, heat pumps and data centres pull up electricity demand? Each year's surprise stacks on the last.

Notice the bands do not simply add their widths. Independent uncertainties combine as variances — the fan grows, but more slowly than a naive sum would suggest.

Dimension four

Renewable pace — the full fan

Finally, renewable build pace: how quickly wind, solar and storage actually get permitted and connected. With all four dimensions live, the fan reaches its full spread.

By 2050 the plausible price range spans tens of dollars per MWh. That width is not noise — it is an honest measure of what the model does not know.

Anatomy of the fan

Which doubt dominates when

Break the fan's width into the variance each dimension contributes. Early on, gas is almost the whole story.

But its share shrinks as the others compound past it. By 2050, carbon policy is the single largest band of doubt. The dominant uncertainty in 2026 is not the dominant uncertainty in 2050.

Your turn

The four buttons under the chart are live. Toggle any dimension on or off and the P10/P50/P90 bands recompute instantly.

Turn everything off and you are back to the lonely central line. Turn a single dimension on and you isolate exactly how much doubt it adds — and how that doubt compounds toward 2050.

The takeaway

Plan for the fan, not the line

A capacity plan tuned to the P50 line is tuned to an outcome that almost certainly will not happen. The useful question is whether a plan still works across the whole fan.

That is why the simulator runs many scenarios rather than one. The fan is the deliverable — and a robust grid is one that stays affordable from its P10 edge to its P90 edge.
